Conditions for life on Earth

Revision notes · The living environment · AQA spec 3.1.1 · R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 5-9

Paper 24 sections1 diagram
Earth's mass, distance from the Sun, rotation, tilt and magnetic field created conditions in which liquid water, a suitable temperature and the right gases allowed life to develop. Life then changed the planet - adding oxygen, an ozone layer, biogeochemical cycles and transpiration - and modern techniques let us reconstruct those past conditions.

Early conditions and features of Earth that made life possible Genn pp. 5-6

  • Earth formed about 4.6 billion years ago as gravity pulled rock together; the molten surface cooled to a crust.
  • Mass: gravity strong enough to retain an atmosphere containing carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and nitrogen (as methane, ammonia, CO2, water) and to give a pressure high enough to stop all liquid water boiling.
  • Distance from the Sun: temperatures allowing liquid water - the 'Goldilocks zone' (too close boils, too far freezes).
  • Speed of rotation: the 24-hour day is short enough to prevent excessive heating and cooling.
  • Axis of rotation: tilted relative to the orbit, producing seasons.
  • Magnetic field: generated by the molten layers, deflects the solar wind's damaging radiation; the Moon's gravity stabilises the tilt and produces tides.

Conditions that allowed life to develop Genn pp. 6-7

Life began about 3.5 billion years ago, probably as Archaea around volcanic seabed vents where warmth and a rich mix of chemicals made reactions likely; the early atmosphere had toxic gases such as ammonia, no oxygen and high UV at the surface. Liquid water is essential as the general physiological solvent, for transport in blood and sap, for temperature control by evaporation, because of its anomalous expansion (densest at 4 °C so ice floats and water bodies do not freeze solid), its high specific heat capacity (moderating temperature change), as a habitat, and because it absorbed UV before the ozone layer existed. Temperature range 0-35 °C over most of the Earth keeps water liquid without denaturing proteins; atmospheric gases supply CO2 for photosynthesis and nitrogen for proteins; solar insolation drives photosynthesis, the water cycle and warming, its absorption depending on albedo and atmospheric composition.

How life on Earth caused environmental change Genn pp. 8

  • Atmospheric oxygen: photosynthetic Archaea appeared about 2.7 billion years ago; for millions of years oxygen reacted with iron in the oceans; once the iron was used up oxygen built up in the sea and, from about 2.45 billion years ago, in the atmosphere. Oxygen absorbed UV forming the O / O2 / O3 equilibrium (ozone layer) that made life on land possible; many anaerobes died out and aerobic organisms evolved during the Proterozoic (to about 540 million years ago).
  • Carbon sequestration: photoautotrophs removed CO2 and stored carbon in carbonate rocks and fossil fuels, preventing a long-term temperature rise even though the Sun brightens by about 10% per billion years.
  • Biogeochemical cycles let small amounts of nutrients support life indefinitely; transpiration by land plants returned water vapour and increased rainfall elsewhere.

Researching past conditions Genn pp. 9

Direct measurements are impossible, so proxy data and new analytical techniques are used: radioisotope dating (carbon-12 : carbon-14), oxygen-18 : oxygen-16 ratios for temperature, air bubbles in ice cores for atmospheric composition, magnesium : calcium ratios in calcite (more magnesium at higher temperature), together with understanding of continental drift, ocean currents, ocean chemistry and atmospheric processes.
