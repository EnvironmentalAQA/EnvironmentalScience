Indicative content - credit any of the following (and other relevant, accurate points):
  • Context: Lasky's principle - vast quantities exist at low grade; reserves vs resource; COOG affected by price, technology, energy costs
  • Improved exploration: higher resolution multispectral satellite sensors, IR spectroscopy, gravimetry, magnetometry, seismic, resistivity, portable field analysis, targeted trial drilling

Model answers: what a Level 3 answer looks like
Model answer · Level 3 (8 marks)

Lasky's principle means that as grade falls, the quantity of metal available rises enormously, so depletion of high-grade deposits does not mean running out - it means the cut-off ore grade must fall, which depends on price, technology and energy costs. Better exploration finds what remains: high-resolution multispectral and infrared satellite sensors, gravimetry, magnetometry, seismic and resistivity surveys and portable field analysers narrow the search so expensive trial drilling is targeted. Mechanisation allows deeper underground mines and larger open-cast pits, and previously inaccessible deposits - polymetallic nodules and deep-sea sediments, and Antarctica if its protection were ever lifted - become possible, with serious environmental and legal problems.

The most sustainable route to low-grade ores is low-energy extraction: bioleaching with acidophilic bacteria and fungi, phytomining, iron displacement of copper, bacterial adsorption of rare earths and polymer adsorption from seawater all avoid the energy of crushing and smelting vast tonnages. Equally important is extending the use of metal already mined: recycling, Cradle-to-Cradle design so products can be disassembled, longer product lifetimes and substitution of scarce metals - the circular economy approach.

Each strategy has costs: deep-sea mining and Antarctica risk unique habitats, deeper mines need more energy, recycling is limited by dispersal and mixed alloys. The strategies that mimic natural low-temperature processes and keep metals in use are the most sustainable, and exploration and new mining should be the last resort rather than the first.

Weaker answer · Level 1 (3 marks)

When high grade ores run out we can mine lower grade ores instead, but this uses more energy. Better technology like satellites can find new deposits. We could mine the deep sea or Antarctica. Bacteria can be used to extract metals from low grade ore. Recycling metals means we do not need to mine as much. These strategies will help secure supplies of metals in the future.

What makes the difference
  • Level 1 names most strategy types but Level 3 explains each (what the surveys measure, why low-temperature extraction matters, what Lasky's principle implies).
  • Level 3 weighs environmental and energy costs and ranks the strategies; Level 1 offers no evaluation.
  • Framing the answer with the concept of cut-off ore grade shows understanding rather than recall.
