Question 910 marks · Manipulation of food species & agricultural energetics · spec 3.5.1.2 · Genn pp. 326-328 · FS-04

Table 1 shows food conversion ratios (FCR) for livestock: the mass of feed needed to produce 1 kg of live mass gain.

Table 1
LivestockFCR / kg feed per kg gain
Farmed salmon1.2
Chicken1.8
Pork3.0
Beef (feedlot)7.0
Sheep8.0
[3 marks]09.1Use Table 1 to explain why intensive poultry and fish production convert feed to food more efficiently than beef.
[3 marks]09.2Explain three ways farmers reduce food chain energy losses in livestock production.
[4 marks]09.3Explain why growing crops for direct human consumption usually produces more food per hectare than rearing livestock, and describe one situation where livestock is the better option.
Show mark scheme
09.1 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Chicken and salmon need far less feed per kg gained (1.2-1.8 vs 7.0)
  • 1Fish are ectotherms so no energy is used for thermoregulation and water supports their mass; poultry grow fast and are kept warm indoors with movement restricted
  • 1Cattle are large endotherms, use energy in movement, and lose energy in methane from cellulose digestion
/ 3
09.2 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Control of movement (housing / restricted grazing) so less energy is used in muscle activity
  • 1Temperature control (heated / insulated buildings) so less energy is used in maintaining body temperature
  • 1Species selection: choose species with low FCRs (poultry, fish); high-energy easily digested diets; slaughter at the fastest growth stage
/ 3
09.3 4 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level is passed to the next; most is lost as heat in respiration / metabolism or in undigested material
  • 1Humans eating plants (primary consumers) receive more of the energy captured by photosynthesis than eating animals fed on those plants (secondary consumers)
  • 1Livestock better where land cannot grow crops (upland, semi-arid, steep, thin soils): grazing animals with symbiotic gut bacteria digest cellulose in grass that humans cannot
  • 1Omnivores (pigs) convert food wastes (dairy by-products, catering waste) into meat
/ 4
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 326-328; AQA spec 3.5.1.2.
