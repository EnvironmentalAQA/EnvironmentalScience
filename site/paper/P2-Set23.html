Question 110 marks · Forest resources & deforestation · spec 3.5.3.3 · Genn pp. 369, 386-387 · FOR-05

The Rio Bravo Conservation and Management Area in Belize exploits mahogany under a sustainable management plan while conserving a rainforest of over 200 tree, 400 bird and 70 mammal species.

[5 marks]01.1Describe five features of the timber harvesting at Rio Bravo that make it sustainable.
[3 marks]01.2Explain why involving local communities is essential to the success of the reserve.
[2 marks]01.3Explain why selective logging is better for wildlife than clear-felling, and give one disadvantage.
Show mark scheme
01.1 5 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Harvest rate below the maximum sustainable yield; only economically valuable species (mahogany) selectively logged, not clear-felled
  • 1Trees felled only where other mahoganies stand upwind so seeds recolonise the clearing; large seed trees and small trees left to grow
  • 1Buffer zones with no harvesting or machinery near rivers / wetlands
  • 1Restricted machinery and planned track routes re-used to avoid damaging roots of retained / rare / fruit trees
  • 1Each felled tree tagged and tracked to the retailer for FSC accreditation; rangers control illegal logging; tree nurseries replant over-exploited and ecologically important species
/ 5
01.2 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1The reserve removed traditional uses (fuelwood, timber, farming clearings, hunting, medicines) so people would otherwise resent / ignore it
  • 1Employment as rangers, forestry and tourism workers; ecotourists use local restaurants, produce and souvenirs; offcuts given to local carvers
  • 1Benefits give the community an incentive to protect the forest; carbon sequestration payments from companies fund management
/ 3
01.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Removing individual trees leaves small clearings so remaining populations survive nearby and the forest structure / microclimate is kept; timber resource used over a longer period
  • 1Disadvantage: more labour-intensive and expensive; more tracks needed per tree
/ 2

Question 210 marks · Habitat case studies: woodland, rainforest, reefs, islands, mangroves, Antarctica · spec 3.1.2.3.4 · Genn pp. 57-60 · HAB-06
[4 marks]02.1Explain how the relationship between coral polyps and zooxanthellae allows reefs to support a high biomass in nutrient-poor tropical seas.
[3 marks]02.2Describe the abiotic conditions needed by reef-building corals and explain why these make reefs vulnerable.
[3 marks]02.3Explain three ways in which coral reefs are important to humans.
Show mark scheme
02.1 4 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Zooxanthellae are photosynthetic algae living inside polyp cells (up to 30 000 per mm2)
  • 1They use the polyp's CO2 and excretory wastes (nitrogen, phosphorus) for photosynthesis and growth, so nutrients are recycled efficiently
  • 1They supply the polyp with glucose and oxygen - over 95% of its food energy; the polyp provides a protected habitat and nutrients
  • 1Polyps also trap plankton with cilia and stinging nematocysts; surplus CO2 is used to build the calcium carbonate skeleton
/ 4
02.2 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1High light (shallow, clear water) for the algae; warm stable temperatures (about 18-30 °C); low turbidity; constant salinity; well-oxygenated water
  • 1Found only where all these coincide (about 1% of ocean area)
  • 1Small changes (warming, sediment, freshwater runoff) move conditions outside the narrow range of tolerance
/ 3
02.3 3 marks
Any 3 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 3.
  • 1Fisheries: support over a quarter of marine species including fish, lobsters, conch
  • 1Erosion protection: complex structure dissipates wave energy protecting low coral islands; coral sand builds shores
  • 1Medicinal discoveries: AZT and Ara-C from sponges, dolastatin from sea hares, bryostatin from Bugula, trabectedin from sea squirts, secosteroids, coral for bone grafts
  • 1Climate control: carbonate skeletons sequester CO2
  • 1Tourism (diving, snorkelling)
/ 3
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 57-60; AQA spec 3.1.2.3.4.

Question 910 marks · Importance of biodiversity: resources & ecosystem services · spec 3.1.2.1.3 · Genn pp. 18-19 · BIOV-07
[2 marks]09.1Explain why the ecosystem services provided by other species are often given a low conservation priority.
[3 marks]09.2Explain how soil organisms provide services that humans depend on.
[2 marks]09.3Explain why pollination by insects is more useful than wind pollination to plants growing in forests.
[3 marks]09.4Explain how seed dispersal by animals increases the survival of a plant's offspring.
Show mark scheme
09.1 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1The processes (eg regulation of atmospheric gases by photosynthesis and respiration) act in a dynamic equilibrium and cancel each other out
  • 1Because there is no overall change their actions go unnoticed / their importance is not appreciated until they are lost
/ 2
09.2 3 marks
Any 3 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 3.
  • 1Invertebrates, fungi and bacteria break down / decompose dead organic matter, releasing nutrients for plant growth
  • 1The organic matter and humus produced hold the soil together (reducing erosion) and regulate the water cycle, giving more even river flow and less flooding
  • 1Organic acids from decomposition aid the weathering of rock, producing more soil and releasing more nutrients
  • 1Microbes carry out the processes of the carbon, nitrogen and phosphorus cycles so wastes do not build up and nutrients are not depleted
/ 3
09.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Trees reduce wind velocity so wind pollination would be ineffective / unreliable over long distances
  • 1Insects search over long distances for flowers so dispersed populations can be pollinated, and less pollen needs to be produced (energy saving); specialised flowers reduce pollen being wasted on other species
/ 2
09.4 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Dispersal is not haphazard: animals live in the same habitat as the plant so are likely to carry seeds to locations where they can survive
  • 1Seeds can be carried longer distances than by wind and can be larger, containing more nutrients for the seedling
  • 1Seeds passing through the gut are deposited in faecal material which acts as a fertiliser, and passage through the intestine may stimulate germination
