Model answers: what a Level 3 answer looks like
Model answer · Level 3 (9 marks)

Solar power's problems are intrinsic: it is intermittent with daily and seasonal cycles, cloud makes it unpredictable and scatters light so it cannot be focused, its low energy density demands large collector areas, the angle of incidence keeps changing, cleaning in deserts uses scarce water, manufacturing PV produces toxic wastes such as silicon tetrachloride and cadmium, and PV efficiency falls as cells heat up.

New technology addresses most of these. Evacuated-tube photothermal panels cut conduction and convection losses, and thermal stores of water, sand, concrete or molten salt hold heat for later; passive solar design, brise soleil and light tubes reduce demand; heat pumps deliver up to four times the energy used to run them. Multijunction PV cells absorb more wavelengths, moth-eye anti-reflective surfaces stop the 30% of light a smooth cell reflects, PV-thermal hybrids cool the cell and use the heat, transparent PV turns windows into generators, heliostats keep panels at the optimum angle and nanohydrophobic surfaces self-clean. Concentrating solar power with molten salt stored at 550 °C runs steam turbines when electricity is wanted, directly overcoming intermittency, and grid-connected farms, rooftops that avoid land conflicts, batteries and pumped storage integrate the output.

Even so, no technology changes the fact that winter insolation at high latitudes is tiny, that CSP needs cloudless skies, or that manufacturing has impacts. Solar can dominate supply in sunny, low-latitude regions and contribute strongly elsewhere in summer, but overcoming its problems fully depends on storage and a diverse energy mix rather than on solar technology alone.

Weaker answer · Level 1 (2 marks)

Solar power has problems because it does not work at night or when it is cloudy, and solar panels are not very efficient. New technologies can help. Better solar panels are more efficient. Solar power can be stored in batteries for use at night. Mirrors can concentrate the sunlight to make it hotter. So new technologies can overcome most of the problems with solar power.

What makes the difference
  • Level 1 identifies two problems and three vague technologies; Level 3 names each technology and explains which problem it solves and how.
  • Level 3 acknowledges what technology cannot fix (high-latitude winters, cloud for CSP, manufacturing impacts) - the 'extent' in the question.
  • Numbers (30% reflection, 550 °C, four-fold heat-pump output) show detailed knowledge applied to the argument.

Model answers: what a Level 3 answer looks like
Model answer · Level 3 (8 marks)

A valid investigation begins with a clear, testable hypothesis and a null hypothesis, informed by existing knowledge, so that the data collected can actually accept or reject it. The first design decision is sample location: random sampling (grid coordinates or numbered sites) or systematic sampling along transects avoids the bias that would come from choosing convenient sites, and a preliminary study shows the interval needed to detect variation without collecting unnecessary data.

Timing matters because many variables change diurnally, seasonally or with weather, so samples on different occasions are needed and the interval must match the rate of change. Sample size and number depend on how variable the population is: a running mean shows when enough samples have been taken and the standard deviation shows the scatter around the mean. Techniques must be standardised so results from different sites, times or researchers are comparable, other variables controlled or monitored, and equipment calibrated so that precise readings are also accurate; anomalies should be repeated rather than ignored.

Finally the analysis must be chosen to suit the data - Spearman's rank for correlation, chi-squared for frequencies, a t-test for two means or Mann-Whitney U for medians - so that significance (p < 0.05) can be assessed, remembering that a significant correlation does not prove causation. Every decision trades reliability against cost, time and disturbance; sub-samples can only estimate the whole, so the most valid study is the one whose sampling design makes its limitations explicit.

Weaker answer · Level 2 (5 marks)

When planning fieldwork you need a hypothesis. You should choose sites randomly so it is not biased, for example using random numbers. You need to take enough samples and repeat them so the results are reliable. The method should be the same each time. You should measure accurately with calibrated equipment. Then you do a statistical test such as a t-test to see if the results are significant. If p is less than 0.05 the result is significant. This makes the conclusions valid.

What makes the difference
  • Level 2 is sound but generic: it says 'enough samples' where Level 3 explains how a running mean and standard deviation tell you how many.
  • Level 3 covers timing and the choice between statistical tests; Level 2 mentions one test only.
  • The evaluation is missing from Level 2 - there is no discussion of trade-offs or of correlation vs causation, so it cannot reach the top level however accurate it is.

  • Fossil fuels: choosing lower-impact fuels (gas over coal), desulfurisation, CCS, efficient combustion, siting power stations near cooling water and coal ports; new extraction technologies (directional drilling, secondary / tertiary recovery) and their impacts (fracking, tar sands)
  • Nuclear: high energy density reduces transport; waste properties (half-life) determine storage; thorium and fusion prospects
  • Renewables: matching resource to location (wind, HEP, solar, wave, tidal, geothermal, biofuel siting); intermittency managed by storage (pumped HEP, batteries, hydrogen, P2G, V2G), grid management and diversity; low-impact designs (helical turbines, VAWT, tidal lagoons)
  • Energy conservation reduces demand: transport, buildings, industry (CHP, heat exchangers)
  • Impacts before and during use; life-cycle / embodied energy
  • Evaluation: no resource is impact-free; knowledge allows the best mix and mitigation; judgement
/ 25 (only the higher of the two essays counts)
