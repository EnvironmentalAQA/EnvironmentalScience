    Carbon: combustion of fossil fuels and biomass moving carbon from long-term stores to the atmosphere

    • deforestation reducing photosynthesis
    • ploughing / drainage increasing aerobic decomposition

    Nitrogen: Haber process doubling fixation

    • legume cultivation
    • drainage increasing nitrification and reducing denitrification
    • combustion NOx
    • sewage and fertiliser leaching
    • consequences: eutrophication, smogs, acid rain, N2O warming, NOx toxicity
  3. •

    Counter-strategies carbon: renewables / nuclear replacing fossil fuels, energy conservation, afforestation matched to deforestation and timber use, conserving peat bogs and forests, increasing soil organic matter (low tillage, mulching), CCS (pre / post combustion, storage)

  4. •

    Counter-strategies nitrogen: reduced combustion, catalytic converters / urea sprays, biological fixation instead of Haber, legumes and crop rotation, management of biological wastes as fertiliser, low-solubility fertilisers, timing, buffer strips, low tillage, nitrate vulnerable zones

  5. •

    Evaluation: some strategies well established and cheap (rotation, buffer strips), others expensive or unproven (CCS)

    • scale of fossil fuel dependence
    • feedback and time lags
    • link to circular economy and low-temperature natural processes
    • judgement

  • 'the algae use up all the oxygen' on its own - the oxygen is used by aerobic decomposers breaking down the dead algae
Model answers: what a Level 3 answer looks like
Model answer · Level 3 (8 marks)

Humans have shifted the carbon cycle by burning fossil fuels and biomass, moving carbon from long-term stores into the atmosphere faster than photosynthesis and ocean uptake remove it, while deforestation reduces photosynthesis, ploughing and drainage speed aerobic decomposition, and paddies, landfills and livestock create new anaerobic sources of methane. The nitrogen cycle has changed as much: the Haber process has roughly doubled fixation, legume crops add more, drainage increases nitrification and reduces denitrification, combustion produces NOx, and leached fertiliser and sewage nitrate cause eutrophication, while nitrous oxide adds to warming.

Carbon can be counteracted by replacing fossil fuels with renewables and nuclear power, energy conservation, afforestation matched to deforestation and timber use, conserving peat bogs and forests, and increasing soil organic matter through low tillage and mulching, with carbon capture and storage as an unproven option. For nitrogen, catalytic converters and urea sprays reduce combustion NOx, biological fixation by legumes and rotation can replace manufactured nitrate, biological wastes can be managed as fertiliser, and low-solubility fertilisers, careful timing and buffer strips reduce leaching.

Some strategies are cheap and established - rotation, buffer strips, fertiliser timing - but others are expensive or untested, and the scale of fossil-fuel dependence, together with feedback and time lags, means the atmosphere will respond slowly. The strategies that mimic natural low-temperature cycling, as in the circular economy, are the most sustainable; the most effective is simply to leave carbon in the ground.

Weaker answer · Level 1 (3 marks)

Humans have changed the carbon cycle by burning fossil fuels which releases carbon dioxide, and by cutting down trees. They have changed the nitrogen cycle by using fertilisers which run off into rivers and cause eutrophication. To counteract this we could use renewable energy instead of fossil fuels and plant more trees. We could use less fertiliser. These would help the cycles go back to normal.

What makes the difference
  • Level 1 names one or two changes per cycle; Level 3 covers several processes for each (decomposition, methane sources, Haber process, nitrification, NOx).
  • Level 3 connects each counter-strategy to the process it reverses; Level 1's strategies are general.
  • The evaluation in Level 3 distinguishes established from unproven strategies and comments on scale and time lags - Level 1 has no evaluation.
