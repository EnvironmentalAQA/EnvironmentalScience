Question 110 marks · Renewable energy technologies · spec 3.3.4.1 · Genn pp. 203-205 · REN-03

A rural community in Pakistan is considering a low-head helical (Archimedes screw) turbine on a river rather than joining a high-head dam scheme.

[5 marks]01.1Explain why low-head turbines are more suitable than high-head HEP schemes for many rural areas.
[1 mark]01.2Suggest one reason why such schemes may not be installed even at suitable sites.
[2 marks]01.3The maximum flow rate at the site is 6.5 m3 s-1 giving an output of 100 kW at 90% turbine efficiency. At 10% of maximum flow the efficiency falls to 40%. Calculate the output at 10% of maximum flow.
[2 marks]01.4Outline two ways a high-head reservoir can affect the river downstream.
Show mark scheme
01.1 (5 marks)
  • No large dam / reservoir needed so no flooding of farmland, homes or habitats and no barrier across the whole river (run-of-river / micro-hydro diverts part of the flow)
  • Suitable for rivers with a small drop in height and lower flow rates that are common in lowland rural areas
  • Small scale matches local demand; electricity used locally without long transmission lines / grid connection
  • Low construction cost and simple technology maintained by the community
  • Helical turbines tolerate high turbidity / debris without damage and fish pass through unharmed; Kaplan turbines with adjustable blades cope with variable flow
/ 5
01.2 (1 marks)
  • Lack of capital / finance, technical knowledge or grid access; seasonal drying of rivers; conflicts with irrigation water; planning / land ownership
/ 1
01.3 (2 marks)
  • Output is proportional to flow x efficiency: 100 x 0.10 x (40 / 90)
  • = 4.4 kW (accept 4.4-4.5)
/ 2
01.4 (2 marks)
Any 2 from:
  • Constant regulated flow removes seasonal extremes: dry-season sandbanks used by nesting birds / turtles lost; high flows that clear gravel spawning beds lost; floodplain no longer flooded
  • Sudden flow increases for peak generation raise turbidity, wash away species, reduce light for photosynthesis
  • Sediment trapped so less nutrient replenishment downstream; barrier to migrating fish (salmon, sturgeon) and isolation of populations
/ 2
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 203-205; AQA spec 3.3.4.1.

Question 310 marks · Global climate change · spec 3.2.1.2 · Genn pp. 100-102 · CC-05
[3 marks]03.1Explain why it is difficult to be certain that a particular flood or heatwave is caused by anthropogenic climate change.
[2 marks]03.2Explain why historic temperature records collected in towns may overestimate global warming.
[5 marks]03.3Describe how each of the following sources of proxy data is used to estimate past climates and give one limitation.
Show mark scheme
03.1 (3 marks)
  • The climate naturally fluctuates (solar output, Earth's orbit) so extreme events can occur without human influence
  • An individual event, or even several, does not show a trend; the change may be in the frequency of events which can only be assessed years later
  • Changes occur at different time and spatial scales (local storm vs global trend); interconnected systems make attribution complex
/ 3
03.2 (2 marks)
  • Towns have a heat-island effect (heat emissions, dark surfaces absorbing light)
  • As the town grows around the weather station the warming recorded is partly local, not global; older equipment / sparse global coverage also reduce reliability
/ 2
03.3 (5 marks)
  • Tree rings: width of each annual ring shows the growth rate, indicating temperature / rainfall in that year; rings easily dated by counting; limitation: growth also affected by other factors (water, pests, shading), only covers the life of the tree / limited regions
  • Ice cores: annual snow layers compact into ice; air bubbles trapped in the ice give the CO2 (and methane) concentration when trapped; oxygen isotope ratios indicate temperature; up to 800 000 years (Dome C); limitation: deep layers compressed / less distinct, dating needs radio-isotope analysis and volcanic ash layers; only polar regions
  • Others accepted: coral growth rings; pollen in lake sediments dated by radiocarbon
/ 5
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 100-102; AQA spec 3.2.1.2.

Question 1010 marks · Properties of pollutants & environmental factors · spec 3.4.1 · Genn pp. 236-240 · PPRO-07
[9 marks]10.1Discuss how an understanding of the properties of pollutants can be used to predict their behaviour in the environment and to plan strategies to control them.
[1 mark]10.2State what is meant by bioaccumulation.
Show mark scheme
10.1 (9 marks)
Levels of response: L3 7-9 comprehensive, well-structured, applied to context, supported judgement; L2 4-6 sound but incomplete application/evaluation; L1 1-3 basic, limited structure.
Indicative content:
  • State of matter: solids deposit near the source, gases disperse widely in the atmosphere; density (lead dust, hydrogen cyanide at Bhopal) controls how far particles/gases travel
  • Persistence / half-life: persistent pollutants (organochlorines, CFCs, heavy metals, radioactive wastes with long half-lives) travel further, accumulate and need containment rather than dilution; non-persistent pollutants (sewage, organophosphates) can be allowed to degrade
  • Toxicity, specificity, mode of action (enzyme inhibition, neurotoxins) determine acceptable concentrations and which species are at risk; LD50 / MDAF / MDNF used to set doses
  • Solubility in water (nitrates disperse and leach - control by nitrate vulnerable zones) vs liposolubility (DDT, mercury bioaccumulate and biomagnify - control by bans / restrictions)
  • Reactivity: reactive pollutants may degrade quickly or form secondary pollutants (ozone, acid rain, PANs) - controlling primary pollutants (catalytic converters, FGD) prevents secondary pollutants
  • Adsorption onto clay / sediments immobilises metals and caesium; can be used deliberately (activated carbon filters, polymer adsorption)
  • Mutagenic / carcinogenic / teratogenic action means no safe dose; exposure kept As Low As Reasonably Achievable (ALARA)
  • Synergism (ozone + SO2; smoke + SO2) means pollutants must be considered together
  • Environmental features (temperature inversions, currents, pH, oxygen) interact with properties: Critical Pathway Analysis uses properties + environment to predict where pollutants go and to focus monitoring
  • Evaluation: properties of new chemicals can be predicted from similar ones (precautionary principle); understanding allows prevention rather than clean-up
/ 9
10.2 (1 marks)
  • The increase in the amount / concentration of a substance within an (individual) organism over time, because intake exceeds excretion or breakdown
/ 1
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 236-240; AQA spec 3.4.1.
Question 11 — Essay (answer ONE)25 marks
[25 marks]11.1Discuss how knowledge of the properties of energy resources can be used to reduce the severity of their impact on the environment.
Indicative content
Levels: L5 21-25 · L4 16-20 · L3 11-15 · L2 6-10 · L1 1-5. Reward breadth across the specification, accurate detail, application to the question and a supported conclusion.
  • Properties: energy density, renewability, abundance, location, intermittency / predictability, storage, transport, conversions, technology level, environmental impacts
  • Fossil fuels: choosing lower-impact fuels (gas over coal), desulfurisation, CCS, efficient combustion, siting power stations near cooling water and coal ports; new extraction technologies (directional drilling, secondary / tertiary recovery) and their impacts (fracking, tar sands)
  • Nuclear: high energy density reduces transport; waste properties (half-life) determine storage; thorium and fusion prospects
  • Renewables: matching resource to location (wind, HEP, solar, wave, tidal, geothermal, biofuel siting); intermittency managed by storage (pumped HEP, batteries, hydrogen, P2G, V2G), grid management and diversity; low-impact designs (helical turbines, VAWT, tidal lagoons)
  • Energy conservation reduces demand: transport, buildings, industry (CHP, heat exchangers)
  • Impacts before and during use; life-cycle / embodied energy
  • Evaluation: no resource is impact-free; knowledge allows the best mix and mitigation; judgement
/ 25 (only the higher of the two essays counts)
