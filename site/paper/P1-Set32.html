Indicative content - credit any of the following (and other relevant, accurate points):
  • Organic nutrients (sewage, manure, silage effluent, paper mill / tannery / food processing effluent) are food for aerobic microbes whose respiration deoxygenates the water, killing fish and invertebrates; bacterial growths cover the bed, blocking light
  • Pathogens in sewage (cholera, typhoid, dysentery) spread via contaminated water; decay of organic matter releases inorganic nutrients
  • Inorganic nutrients: nitrates (fertiliser / manure runoff, leaching) and phosphates (sewage effluent, detergents, eroded soil) cause cultural eutrophication: algal blooms, shading of macrophytes, cyanobacterial toxins, night-time and end-of-summer deoxygenation
  • Human health: nitrites and methaemoglobinaemia; possible carcinogen via nitrosamines
  • Control of organic wastes: sewage treatment (pre-treatment, primary sedimentation removing >95% organic matter, aeration / activated sludge or trickling filters, anaerobic digestion of sludge with biogas)
  • Control of nutrients: tertiary phosphate stripping with iron(III) sulfate, denitrification in filter beds, micro-strainers / UV; Nitrate Vulnerable Zones, fertiliser timing / amount, low-solubility fertilisers, legumes, manure management; dredging sediments
  • Evaluation: sewage treatment has virtually eliminated deoxygenation and waterborne disease in developed countries but storm overflows and combined sewers still discharge untreated sewage; tertiary treatment is expensive so only used at sensitive sites; diffuse agricultural nitrate is hard to control and aquifers respond slowly; phosphate legacy in sediments
  • Judgement on point-source (sewage) control being more successful than diffuse-source (agriculture) control
Model answers: what a Level 3 answer looks like
Model answer · Level 3 (9 marks)

Organic wastes - sewage, manure, silage effluent, paper-mill and food-processing effluent - are food for aerobic bacteria, whose respiration strips dissolved oxygen from the water so fish and invertebrates suffocate and bacterial growths smother the bed; sewage also carries pathogens such as cholera and typhoid. Inorganic nutrients act differently: nitrate leached from fertiliser and manure and phosphate from sewage, detergents and eroded soil cause cultural eutrophication, in which algal blooms shade macrophytes, cyanobacteria release toxins and night-time respiration and decay deoxygenate the water. Nitrate in drinking water can cause methaemoglobinaemia and may form carcinogenic nitrosamines.

Point sources are controlled by sewage treatment: screening and grit removal, primary sedimentation removing most organic solids, aeration with activated sludge or trickling filters, and anaerobic digestion of sludge yielding biogas. Nutrients need tertiary treatment - phosphate stripping with iron(III) sulfate, denitrifying filter beds, micro-strainers and UV. Diffuse agricultural sources are managed by Nitrate Vulnerable Zones, limits on the timing and amount of fertiliser, low-solubility fertilisers, legumes, manure management and buffer strips.

Sewage treatment has virtually eliminated river deoxygenation and waterborne disease in developed countries, but storm overflows on combined sewers still discharge untreated sewage, and tertiary treatment is expensive so it is used only where eutrophication is a risk. Diffuse nitrate from farmland is much harder to control because it comes from thousands of sources and depends on farmers' behaviour and the weather. Control of point sources has therefore been far more successful than control of diffuse ones.

Weaker answer · Level 1 (3 marks)

Sewage in rivers uses up the oxygen because bacteria break it down, so fish die. Fertilisers wash into rivers and cause eutrophication, where algae grow and block the light and then the water runs out of oxygen. Sewage is treated in sewage works to clean it before it goes into rivers. Farmers should use less fertiliser. Sewage treatment works well but fertiliser is still a problem.

What makes the difference
  • Level 1 has the outline of both mechanisms but Level 3 explains them precisely (aerobic respiration, shading, night-time respiration, pathogens, health effects) and names the pollutant sources.
  • Level 3 describes the stages of sewage treatment and the specific nutrient controls; Level 1 says 'sewage works' and 'less fertiliser'.
  • Level 3's evaluation gives reasons (storm overflows, cost of tertiary treatment, diffuse sources) for its point-vs-diffuse judgement; Level 1 asserts it.
