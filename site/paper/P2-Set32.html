Model answers: what a Level 3 answer looks like
Model answer · Level 3 (8 marks)

Sustainability matters because the population may reach 12 billion, land has been degraded since farming began in the fertile crescent, and current yields depend on artificial fertilisers, pesticides and irrigation that draws down groundwater, with little new land to cultivate. Pest control can move from chemicals to cultural methods - weeding, mulching, rotation, barrier and companion crops, habitats for predators - biological control, sterile males and pheromones, resistant varieties and integrated control, with fewer antibiotics in livestock.

Nutrients can come from natural processes: legumes fixing nitrogen, recycling manure and compost, rotation, permaculture and conserving soil biota and mycorrhizae, reducing manufactured nitrate and the non-renewable rock phosphate it accompanies. Energy use falls with low tillage, less machinery, renewable power, low food miles and lower methane from cattle diets and drought-tolerant rice. Water is conserved with low water-use crops, drip irrigation, reservoirs and aquifer recharge and by avoiding saline water; soil by maintaining organic matter and controlling erosion. Retaining hedgerows, ditches, ponds and woodland, conserving crop wild relatives in seed banks and agri-environment schemes protect the biodiversity that supplies pollination and pest control, while Fairtrade, local food and consumer choices address the social side.

The tension is between yield and sustainability: some strategies cost more or yield less in the short term and need policy support to be adopted. But since the unsustainable inputs themselves depend on finite fossil fuels and phosphate, the strategies that use natural processes are the only ones that can feed a larger population indefinitely.

Weaker answer · Level 2 (4 marks)

Agriculture can be made more sustainable by using less pesticides and using biological control instead, using manure instead of artificial fertilisers, growing legumes which add nitrogen to the soil, and using crop rotation. Drip irrigation saves water. Farmers can keep hedgerows to provide habitats for wildlife. Organic farming uses these methods. These strategies reduce pollution and keep the soil healthy so food can be produced in the future.

What makes the difference
  • Level 2 lists valid strategies but does not explain why they are needed (population growth, land degradation, dependence on finite inputs) or group them by the problem they solve.
  • Level 3 covers energy, soil, biodiversity and social strategies as well as pests, nutrients and water.
  • Level 3 discusses the yield-sustainability tension and the need for policy support; Level 2 has no evaluation.
