Question 710 marks · Adaptation, terminology, succession & population control · spec 3.1.3.2 · Genn pp. 82-85 · LP-02
[8 marks]07.1Define each of the following ecological terms and give an example.
[2 marks]07.2Explain the difference between an ecosystem and a biome.
Show mark scheme
07.1 8 marks
2 marks for each point (partial credit for a partly correct point).
  • 2Species: a group of closely related organisms that naturally interbreed to produce fertile offspring and are reproductively isolated from other groups (Passer domesticus, house sparrow); the only 'real' taxon
  • 2Niche: the role a species plays in its habitat - how it uses resources and relates to other species (tawny owl: nocturnal carnivore nesting in tree holes eating small mammals); two species cannot share exactly the same niche in one habitat
  • 2Community: all the populations of all species (plants, animals, fungi, bacteria, protists, archaea) living in an area
  • 2Biome: a large geographical region with a specific climate and characteristic community, including all areas where it occurs (all tropical rainforests, tundra, savannah)
/ 8
07.2 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1An ecosystem is the biotic community plus abiotic factors of one area, relatively self-contained (a particular coral reef)
  • 1A biome includes all separate ecosystems with the same climate and community type (all coral reefs); each reef is a different ecosystem within the same biome
/ 2

Question 910 marks · Captive breeding, habitat creation & management · spec 3.1.2.3.3 · Genn pp. 35-37 · CBR-04
[3 marks]09.1Describe how cloning could be used to conserve the northern white rhinoceros, and explain why success has been limited.
[3 marks]09.2Explain how micro-propagation and cloning are used to conserve rare plants such as the Atlantic black poplar.
[4 marks]09.3Describe the purpose and methods of the Millennium Seed Bank.
Show mark scheme
09.1 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1The nucleus of a stem cell from the endangered species is transferred into an empty egg of a closely related species (southern white rhino)
  • 1The embryo is implanted into a surrogate female of the related species which gives birth to a northern white rhino
  • 1Very low success rates with wildlife so far; deep-frozen cells could allow clones even after the last individuals die
/ 3
09.2 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Micro-propagation: tissue culture producing many cell clusters from one plant, each grown into a genetically identical plant
  • 1Black poplar: cuttings / pollarded stems of pure native trees are rooted to produce many identical trees
  • 1Needed because the flowers are pollinated by introduced subspecies producing hybrids, so cloning maintains the pure gene pool (but no genetic diversity)
/ 3
09.3 4 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Stores seeds of wild plants (especially threatened species and crop wild relatives) so species are not lost if extinct in the wild
  • 1Seeds collected worldwide; target of 10 000 seeds per species to maintain a diverse gene pool
  • 1Stored dry and refrigerated / frozen in an underground reinforced vault to reduce risk of accident
  • 1Seeds germinated periodically to check viability and replenish stocks; can be used for reintroduction / breeding
