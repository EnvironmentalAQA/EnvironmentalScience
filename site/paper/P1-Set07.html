Question 110 marks · Thermal & oil pollution · spec 3.4.3.2.7 · Genn pp. 258-259 · WPOL-02
[3 marks]01.1Give three sources of oil pollution of the sea other than tanker accidents.
[3 marks]01.2Explain how an oil spill at sea can kill seabirds.
[2 marks]01.3Explain two ways in which an oil slick can harm organisms that live in the water beneath it.
[2 marks]01.4Explain why oil pollution is most serious when it occurs inshore or in an enclosed body of water.
Show mark scheme
01.1 (3 marks)
Any 3 from:
  • Discharge of tank-washing water from tankers
  • Oil rig / drilling accidents / leakage during drilling
  • Pipeline leaks
  • Leakage from storage tanks / refinery spills
  • Waste lubricating oil from vehicles / industry washed into drains
  • Other ship accidents (fuel oil)
/ 3
01.2 (3 marks)
  • Oil sticks feathers together so they lose their insulating properties and birds die of hypothermia
  • Feathers lose buoyancy / waterproofing so heavily oiled birds drown
  • Birds swallow toxic oil when preening; time spent cleaning feathers reduces time for feeding themselves / their young
/ 3
01.3 (2 marks)
Any 2 from:
  • The surface film reduces the diffusion of oxygen from the atmosphere into the water, so aerobic organisms suffer from low dissolved oxygen
  • Toxic components (benzene, xylene) dissolve and poison plankton near the surface
  • Oil blocks light so algae / phytoplankton cannot photosynthesise
  • Oil masks odours / narcotises so animals cannot find mates or food by smell
  • Shore organisms (molluscs, crustaceans) are covered and asphyxiated
/ 2
01.4 (2 marks)
  • Dispersal / dilution by currents and waves is prevented so oil stays concentrated; in open water most spills disperse in days or weeks
  • Inshore areas have more sensitive ecosystems (shores, reefs, mangroves, breeding sites) and more human activities (fishing, tourism) to be affected
/ 2
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 258-259; AQA spec 3.4.3.2.7.
Question 210 marks · Sampling techniques & measuring abiotic factors · spec 3.7.2.1-3.7.2.2 · Genn pp. 410-413, 417 · RM-10
[3 marks]02.1Describe how soil texture is determined using a stack of sieves.
[2 marks]02.2Explain why a soil auger gives a more representative sample than a trowel.
[2 marks]02.3Explain why the organic matter content will be overestimated if a soil sample is heated above 550 °C.
[3 marks]02.4Describe how a turbidimeter measures water turbidity and explain how quantitative results are obtained.
Show mark scheme
02.1 (3 marks)
  • Sieves assembled with the coarsest mesh on top; a dried, crushed soil sample is placed in the top sieve
  • Stack shaken (about 2 minutes) until no more particles pass; each fraction weighed
  • Percentages of sand (0.02-2 mm), silt (0.002-0.02 mm) and clay (base pan, below 0.002 mm) calculated; material over 2 mm (stones, twigs) is not soil; plotted on a soil triangle
/ 3
02.2 (2 marks)
  • Auger collects a core of known cross-sectional area and depth
  • So samples from different sites are directly comparable (also needed for bulk density = dry mass / volume)
/ 2
02.3 (2 marks)
  • Minerals such as calcium carbonate break down releasing carbon dioxide
  • So mass is lost that is not organic matter
/ 2
02.4 (3 marks)
  • A beam of light passes through the sample in a dark chamber; a light sensor at 90° measures light scattered by suspended particles - more turbid, more scatter
  • Standardise bulb brightness / colour, distance and container
  • A calibration curve is produced using samples with known amounts of suspended matter so readings convert to concentrations; Secchi discs / turbidity bottles give only semi-quantitative results
/ 3
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 410-413, 417; AQA spec 3.7.2.1-3.7.2.2.

Question 410 marks · The atmosphere: energy processes (UV, IR, visible light) · spec 3.2.1.1, 3.2.2.3 · Genn pp. 87, 96-97 · ATM-03
[4 marks]04.1Explain how energy absorbed in tropical regions is distributed to higher latitudes such as the UK.
[2 marks]04.2Explain why the UK has milder winters than Moscow or Churchill, Canada, which are at similar latitudes.
[2 marks]04.3Explain why the atmosphere would not retain liquid water if atmospheric pressure were much lower.
[2 marks]04.4Give two industrially important gases extracted from the atmosphere.
Show mark scheme
04.1 (4 marks)
  • Most solar energy is absorbed at the surface in the tropics where insolation is most intense / the Sun is overhead
  • The warm surface heats the air above; warm winds (eg south-westerlies) carry heat energy to higher latitudes
  • Winds blowing over the oceans create currents (North Atlantic Conveyor / Gulf Stream) carrying warm water from the tropics to NW Europe
  • Evaporation in the tropics stores latent heat in water vapour which is released when it condenses at higher latitudes; also transports water to areas that would be dry
/ 4
04.2 (2 marks)
  • The North Atlantic Conveyor brings warm water from the tropics to the coast of NW Europe
  • Prevailing south-westerly winds blowing over the warm sea carry the heat onto the land
/ 2
04.3 (2 marks)
  • Atmospheric pressure controls how easily water molecules evaporate / escape from the surface
  • At low pressure water would boil / evaporate at low temperatures so all surface water would become vapour
/ 2
04.4 (2 marks)
Any 2 from:
  • Nitrogen
  • Oxygen
  • Carbon dioxide
  • Argon / neon / krypton / xenon
/ 2
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 87, 96-97; AQA spec 3.2.1.1, 3.2.2.3.

06.1 (1 marks)
  • (44 - 8) / 50 = 0.72 m yr-1

06.2 (2 marks)
  • Abstraction (for irrigation) exceeds the rate of natural recharge by infiltration / percolation of precipitation
  • The dynamic equilibrium between inflow and outflow has been upset so the stored volume declines
/ 2
06.3 (3 marks)
  • Porosity: a high proportion of the rock volume is space that can hold water (chalk, limestone, sandstone)
  • Permeability: the spaces are interconnected and large enough for water to flow through (clay is porous but not permeable)

Any 4 from:

  • Subsidence: emptied pore spaces no longer support the rock so it compacts, damaging buildings and pipes (eg Mexico City)
  • Drying up of groundwater-fed rivers, lakes and wetlands as the water table falls below their beds
  • Ecological impacts: plants needing high water availability die; wetland animals lost; food chain effects
  • Reduced supplies: abstraction may suddenly have to stop after years of hidden decline
/ 4

[3 marks]08.1Explain how the acidic drainage water is produced in the spoil heap.

[2 marks]08.3Suggest why the pH and zinc concentration have partly recovered 2 km downstream.

Show mark scheme
08.1 (3 marks)
  • Spoil contains residual sulfide ores (eg iron pyrites, zinc sulfide)
  • These react with oxygen in the air spaces between the waste particles (oxidation)
  • Producing metal oxides and oxides of sulfur which dissolve in drainage water to form sulfuric acid
/ 3
08.2 (3 marks)
  • The low pH (3.4) directly damages exposed cells / denatures proteins and dissolves calcium-based exoskeletons
  • Low pH increases the solubility of zinc (0.02 to 6.5 mg dm-3) which is toxic (inhibits enzymes)
  • Only a few tolerant taxa survive; the sensitive mayfly larvae (indicator of clean water) are eliminated; dissolved oxygen is not the limiting factor here
/ 3
08.3 (2 marks)
  • Dilution by tributaries / more water
  • Neutralisation by alkaline rocks (limestone) in the stream bed; zinc precipitates / adsorbs onto sediments as pH rises
/ 2
08.4 (2 marks)
  • Collect the drainage water and pass it through crushed limestone / add lime to neutralise the acid
  • Higher pH also reduces the solubility of toxic metals so they precipitate and can be removed

Question 1010 marks · Noise pollution · spec 3.4.3.2.13 · Genn pp. 286-295 · NOISE-06
[9 marks]10.1Using named examples, explain how the sources of noise pollution can be controlled by changes to design, operation and location.
[1 mark]10.2State what is meant by the Leq 57 dB contour used around UK airports.
Show mark scheme
10.1 (9 marks)
Levels of response: L3 7-9 comprehensive, well-structured, applied to context, supported judgement; L2 4-6 sound but incomplete application/evaluation; L1 1-3 basic, limited structure.
Indicative content:
  • Principles: dB is logarithmic (10 dB = 10x; 3 dB = 2x) so small reductions in dB are large reductions in intensity; effects on humans (hearing, stress, behaviour) and wildlife (breeding birds, bats, cetaceans)
  • Aircraft design: high bypass ratio engines, chevron nozzles, hush kits, acoustic liners, fairings, composites, blended wing
  • Aircraft operation: take-off angle, flight paths avoiding urban areas, constant descent, reduced reverse thrust, night restrictions, Chapter noise limits, fines and noise charges, quota count
  • Airport design / location: away from population centres, taxi and engine-test areas away from housing, acoustic walls, baffle mounds, land-use restrictions, free acoustic insulation
  • Railway: track polishing, sound-absorbing ballast and suspension, pantograph fairings, wheel lubrication, composite brakes
  • Road: porous / rubber asphalt, acoustic fences, embankments, tree planting, aerodynamics, engine insulation, traffic management and routing, double glazing
  • Industrial: silencers on compressors, pressing instead of stamping, nylon rollers, drilling instead of pile-driving, bubble curtains, timing of blasting, acoustic mats / curtains, remote operation, ear protection
  • Domestic: acoustic absorbers in appliances, volume limiters, selection of quieter appliances, ear defenders
  • Evaluation: which approaches are most effective / cost-effective; conflicts (airports near cities for convenience); legislation and monitoring (Leq 57 dB contours) needed to enforce
/ 9
10.2 (1 marks)
  • The area within which the average (equivalent continuous) sound level between 07:00 and 23:00 in summer exceeds 57 dB - taken as the onset of significant community annoyance
/ 1
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 286-295; AQA spec 3.4.3.2.13.
