Question 110 marks · Energy conservation: transport, buildings, industry · spec 3.3.4.2.2 · Genn pp. 228-232 · ECON-05
[6 marks]01.1Explain how each of the following building design features reduces energy use.
[2 marks]01.2Compare the embodied energy of concrete with alternative building materials.
[2 marks]01.3Describe how a heat exchanger allows a building to be ventilated with minimal heat loss.
Show mark scheme
01.1 (6 marks)
  • Orientation: south-facing windows (northern hemisphere) maximise passive solar gains; rooms needing warmth on the sunny side; fewer / smaller windows on the north side where losses are greatest; solar screens / brise soleil prevent summer overheating
  • Low SA:V (compact shapes, terraced / joined buildings, fewer storeys of small footprint) means less external surface per unit volume through which heat is lost
  • Earth sheltering: the ground is warmer than winter air and shields the building from wind, so conduction and wind-chill losses are reduced
/ 6
01.2 (2 marks)
  • Cement manufacture (heating limestone) is very energy intensive so concrete has high embodied energy
  • Limecrete has about half the embodied energy of concrete; rammed earth, straw bales and lime mortar are lower still
/ 2
01.3 (2 marks)
  • Warm stale air leaving and cold fresh air entering pass through the exchanger in opposite directions (counter-current) separated by thin conducting pipes / plates with a large surface area
  • Heat transfers from the outgoing to the incoming air so fresh air enters already warmed
/ 2
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 228-232; AQA spec 3.3.4.2.2.

Question 410 marks · Fluctuations in supply/demand & energy storage · spec 3.3.4.1 · Genn pp. 222 · EST-02

A pumped-storage HEP station has an upper reservoir 500 m above its turbines. It can hold 7 million m3 of water. The density of water is 1000 kg m-3 and g = 9.8 N kg-1.

[3 marks]04.1Describe how pumped-storage HEP is used for peak shaving.
[3 marks]04.2Calculate the maximum gravitational potential energy stored when the upper reservoir is full. Give your answer in standard form.
[2 marks]04.3The station returns 75% of the electricity used for pumping. Explain why the process is still worthwhile despite this loss.
[2 marks]04.4Give two locational requirements of a pumped-storage station.
Show mark scheme
04.1 (3 marks)
  • At times of low demand / surplus supply (night, windy periods) surplus electricity pumps water from the lower to the upper reservoir, storing it as gravitational potential energy
  • At peaks in demand water flows down through the turbines generating electricity
  • Can go from standby to full power in under 15 seconds so it meets sudden peaks (TV pickup) that fossil / nuclear stations cannot follow
/ 3
04.2 (3 marks)
  • Mass = 7 x 106 x 1000 = 7 x 109 kg
  • PE = m g h = 7 x 109 x 9.8 x 500
  • = 3.43 x 1013 J (accept 3.4 x 1013)
/ 3
04.3 (2 marks)
  • The electricity used for pumping is surplus that would otherwise be wasted / lost as heat, or cheap off-peak electricity
  • It avoids building rapid-response low-efficiency peaking plants (open-cycle gas turbines) and allows intermittent renewables to be used
/ 2
04.4 (2 marks)
Any 2 from:
  • Two water bodies / sites with a large height difference close together (mountainous)
  • Impermeable, stable rock
  • Close to the grid / demand
  • Adequate water supply
/ 2
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 222; AQA spec 3.3.4.1.
Question 510 marks · Sampling techniques & measuring abiotic factors · spec 3.7.2.1 · Genn pp. 395-396, 415-416 · RM-06

A student compared the invertebrates in a stream above and below a sewage outfall using kick sampling.

[3 marks]05.1Describe how kick sampling is carried out so that the samples from the two sites are comparable.
[3 marks]05.2Give three limitations of kick sampling.
[2 marks]05.3Explain how a Surber sampler improves on kick sampling.
[2 marks]05.4Explain why the invertebrates found give a better indication of long-term sewage pollution than a single measurement of dissolved oxygen.
Show mark scheme
05.1 (3 marks)
  • Net held with its straight edge on the river bed facing the current (open to the flow)
  • The bed in front is disturbed by kicking / scraping a boot for a standard number of kicks and time (eg 30 s)
  • Same substrate type, water depth and flow rate at both sites; same net mesh; repeat samples; invertebrates identified with a key and counted
/ 3
05.2 (3 marks)
Any 3 from:
  • Difficult to standardise the vigour / area kicked
  • Buried organisms or those fixed to stones are not dislodged
  • Some organisms swim and avoid the net
  • Flow rate varies and affects capture
  • Fragile organisms destroyed
/ 3
05.3 (2 marks)
  • A fixed area of bed is enclosed by a frame so the sampled area is standardised; side nets reduce escapes
  • But only works in fast enough flow and is hard to set on rough substrates / deep water
/ 2
05.4 (2 marks)
  • Pollutant / oxygen levels fluctuate (eg with flow, time of day) so one reading may miss a pollution event
  • Sensitive taxa (stonefly, mayfly larvae) disappear and tolerant ones (midge larvae, hoglouse) remain, integrating conditions over weeks / months (biotic index)
/ 2
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 395-396, 415-416; AQA spec 3.7.2.1.
Question 610 marks · Heavy metals: lead, mercury · spec 3.4.3.2.11 · Genn pp. 275-279 · HM-05
[1 mark]06.1Which of the following is the main reason that lead-based paint is a greater risk to children than to adults? Tick one box.
  • A  Children inhale more lead vapour
  • B  Children pick up dust / flakes and suck their fingers
  • C  Lead is more soluble in children's blood
  • D  Children spend more time near roads
[3 marks]06.2Explain how tri-butyl tin (TBT) in anti-fouling paint caused pollution even though only small amounts dissolved into the sea.
[3 marks]06.3Iron is not toxic, yet iron-rich drainage from mine spoil heaps kills fish. Explain why, and describe how it is controlled.
[2 marks]06.4Give two uses of lead that continue because they do not cause significant lead release into the environment.
[1 mark]06.5Explain why heavy metals are described as neurotoxins.
Show mark scheme
06.1 (1 marks)
  • B
/ 1
06.2 (3 marks)
  • TBT is highly toxic to marine molluscs / crustaceans and an endocrine disruptor altering growth and reproduction of oysters / whelks even at very low concentrations
  • It bioaccumulated in shellfish and could pass to humans eating them
  • It was assumed dilution would make it harmless (illustrates the need for the precautionary principle); now prohibited and replaced by copper
/ 3
06.3 (3 marks)
  • In the low-oxygen conditions of the spoil heap iron is in its soluble reduced form and is leached into the river
  • In the river it is oxidised to insoluble iron oxide (orange sediment); the oxidation uses dissolved oxygen so the river is deoxygenated, killing aerobic organisms
  • Control: drainage water is passed over mesh screens where the iron oxidises and is deposited before the water reaches the river; the solid iron is removed periodically
/ 3
06.4 (2 marks)
Any 2 from:
  • Lead-acid car batteries (recycled)
  • Lead flashing on roofs / windows
  • Radiation shielding
/ 2
06.5 (1 marks)
  • They inhibit enzyme function, especially the enzymes of the nervous system / nerve cells

Question 810 marks · Nutrient pollution, sewage treatment & acid mine drainage · spec 3.4.3.2.9 · Genn pp. 270-273 · NUT-04
[4 marks]08.1Describe the process of anaerobic sludge digestion and give two benefits of the process.
[4 marks]08.2Explain two problems of using sewage sludge as an agricultural fertiliser and how each can be reduced.
[2 marks]08.3Explain why it is more efficient to have separate collection systems for foul sewage and for surface drainage water.
Show mark scheme
08.1 (4 marks)
Any 4 from:
  • Sludge from primary sedimentation is digested by anaerobic microbes in a warm tank for about four weeks
  • Kills most pathogens
  • Reduces odour and volume of the sludge
  • Produces biogas (mainly methane with some CO2) used as fuel to heat the digester / generate electricity
  • Digested sludge has a better C:N ratio and can be used as agricultural fertiliser
/ 4
08.2 (4 marks)
  • Heavy metals from urban wastes / road runoff may be present and absorbed by crops - reduced by adding lime to lower metal solubility
  • Remaining pathogens - destroyed by heat treatment of the sludge before use
  • Timing: spreading in the growing season covers crops; in winter vehicles compact wet soils - apply at suitable times / when soils are dry
  • Odour / public objection - digestion reduces odour
/ 4
08.3 (2 marks)
  • Foul waste is a small volume needing full treatment so smaller, cheaper treatment works can be built; surface water needs little treatment
  • The treatment works is not affected by weather-related fluctuations in flow (storms) so storm overflows of untreated sewage are avoided
/ 2

  • B - Steam washing (hot water / steam jets kill organisms in the sediment)

Question 1015 marks · Water treatment, conservation & new sources · spec 3.2.2.4-3.2.2.5 · Genn pp. 122-127 · HYD-13

Figure 1 shows the river level below a reservoir site during one year, before and after a river-regulation reservoir was built.

Figure 1
[2 marks]10.1Use Figure 1 to calculate the reduction in the annual range of river level produced by the reservoir.
[2 marks]10.2Explain how the reservoir produces the changes shown in Figure 1.
[2 marks]10.3Explain why human activities in the catchment may have increased the extremes in river flow before the reservoir was built.
[9 marks]10.4Discuss the factors that should be considered when selecting a site for a new reservoir, and evaluate the alternative strategies for increasing the sustainability of water supplies.
Show mark scheme
10.1 (2 marks)
  • No reservoir range = 3.9 - 0.7 = 3.2 m; with reservoir = 2.7 - 1.7 = 1.0 m
  • Reduction = 2.2 m
/ 2
10.2 (2 marks)
  • After heavy rainfall surplus water is stored, reducing peak flows / flooding downstream
  • During dry periods the dam is opened to release water and maintain river levels
/ 2
10.3 (2 marks)
  • Deforestation / urbanisation reduce infiltration and interception
  • So runoff reaches the river faster after rain and less water is stored to maintain flow in dry periods
/ 2
10.4 (9 marks)
Levels of response: L3 7-9 comprehensive, well-structured, applied to context, supported judgement; L2 4-6 sound but incomplete application/evaluation; L1 1-3 basic, limited structure.
Indicative content:
  • Site factors: topography - narrow exit from a large deep basin so a small dam holds a large volume; impermeable, strong bedrock without faults or seismic activity; large catchment with regular high rainfall and low evaporation; catchment land use with low pollution risk (pesticides, industry) and low soil erosion to avoid sedimentation; forest / vegetation in the flooded area decaying anaerobically releasing methane; access for workers and materials and distance to demand; value of the land lost (urban, conservation, agricultural) - loss-benefit analysis differs between countries
  • Alternatives for supply: rainwater collection, rivers, estuary barrages, desalination (energy-intensive so only where freshwater is inadequate), unexploited aquifers (N Africa, Kenya), inter-basin transfers (China, Australia, Wales-England), artificial aquifer recharge using surplus wet-season water, river-regulation reservoirs, afforestation to even out flow
  • Conservation strategies: low-water appliances, dual-flush toilets, xeriscaping, drip irrigation, grey-water re-use, leak repair (about 20% lost in the UK), metering
  • Treatment / pollution control allowing re-use of contaminated sources: sewage treatment, buffer strips, mine drainage control
  • Evaluation: reservoirs give large reliable supplies and flood control but flood land, cause habitat loss and methane; conservation is cheap and quick but limited by behaviour; transfers create conflicts between regions; desalination has high energy / carbon cost; combination needed as demand grows
  • Justified conclusion
