01.16 marksMarks shared

Up to 6 marks in total - share them between the 4 points below according to how completely each one is made.

  1. Detritivores (worms, beetle larvae, millipedes, woodlice) physically break up dead organic matter, increasing its surface area, and release nutrients

    • worms create tunnels improving drainage and aeration

  2. Decomposers (bacteria, fungi) secrete enzymes that break down dead organic matter, releasing nutrient ions (ammonification) and forming humus which binds particles into crumb peds

  4. Mycorrhizal fungi form symbiotic associations with roots increasing phosphate uptake

    • some species not dislodged / fly away
    • high branches unreachable

Model answers: what a Level 3 answer looks like
Model answer · Level 3 (8 marks)

Whether a resource suits a use depends on its features: renewable or not, abundance, energy density, locational constraints, intermittency and predictability, the conversions needed, ease of storage and transport, how developed the technology is and its impacts. Fossil fuels dominate because they combine high energy density, easy storage and transport, high temperatures for industry and mature infrastructure - which is why petrol and diesel still monopolise transport. But they are finite, cause acid rain, smoke and CO2, and are politically concentrated. Nuclear fuel has the highest energy density and gives reliable base load with few deliveries, yet the technology is complex and expensive and produces long-lived waste against public opposition.

Renewables have the opposite profile: low energy density needing large collectors, intermittency (wind and solar unpredictable, tidal predictable), strong locational constraints and no direct storage, so they need conversion, storage and grid links and cannot yet reach fossil-fuel temperatures or replace liquid fuels. Sustainability must count impacts before use - extraction, processing, embodied energy of equipment - as well as pollution, habitat damage and depletion during use, and economics and politics (subsidies, planning, external costs left out of prices, energy security) shape what is actually chosen.

No single resource is suitable for everything. Electricity, heat and transport have different requirements, so the most sustainable system is a diverse mix matched to each use with storage, and the transition must begin before depletion forces it.

Weaker answer · Level 1 (2 marks)

Energy resources have different features. Fossil fuels are non-renewable and will run out but they have a high energy density. Renewable energy like wind and solar will not run out but they are intermittent because the wind does not always blow. Nuclear power produces radioactive waste. Renewable energy is more sustainable because it does not produce carbon dioxide. So we should use more renewable energy.

What makes the difference
  • Level 1 mentions two or three features; Level 3 works through the full set and links each to a specific use (transport, base load, industry).
  • Level 3 includes sustainability concepts (impacts before and during use, external costs) and political factors that Level 1 omits entirely.
  • The conclusion in Level 3 is a reasoned position (a diverse mix matched to uses); Level 1's 'use more renewables' does not follow from a discussion.
