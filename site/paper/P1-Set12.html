Model answers: what a Level 3 answer looks like
Model answer · Level 3 (9 marks)

Because the decibel scale is logarithmic, a 10 dB cut is a tenfold reduction in intensity and 3 dB halves it, so modest-sounding design changes matter. Aircraft engines have been made quieter by high-bypass-ratio turbofans, chevron nozzles, hush kits and acoustic liners, with fairings and composite airframes reducing airframe noise. Operation can be changed too: steeper take-off angles and flight paths that avoid housing, constant-descent approaches, restricted reverse thrust, night curfews, Chapter noise limits enforced by fines and quota counts. Airport location and layout - away from population centres, engine-test areas and taxiways kept from housing, acoustic walls and baffle mounds, land-use planning and free insulation for residents - deal with the noise that remains.

Railways use track polishing, sound-absorbing ballast and suspension, pantograph fairings, wheel lubrication on curves and composite brakes. Roads use porous or rubber-crumb asphalt, acoustic fences and embankments, tree planting, engine insulation and aerodynamics, traffic routing and double glazing. Industry fits silencers to compressors, presses rather than stamps, uses nylon rollers, drills instead of pile-driving, surrounds piling with bubble curtains to protect cetaceans, times blasting, hangs acoustic mats and operates remotely, with ear protection as a last line. Domestic appliances use acoustic absorbers and volume limiters.

Design changes at source are the most effective because they reduce noise for everyone; operational limits depend on enforcement; location works only for new developments, and airports are still sited near cities for convenience. Legislation and monitoring of noise contours (57 dB Leq) are what turn these options into actual reductions.

Weaker answer · Level 2 (5 marks)

Noise pollution can be reduced in different ways. Aircraft can be designed with quieter engines and can fly at night less often. Airports can be built away from cities. Roads can have sound barriers next to them and quieter road surfaces. Trains can have their tracks polished and use better brakes. Factories can use quieter machines and workers can wear ear defenders. Noise can also be reduced by planting trees. These methods reduce the noise that reaches people.

What makes the difference
  • Level 2 gives correct but generic examples ('quieter engines'); Level 3 names the design features (high bypass ratio, chevron nozzles, acoustic liners) the question's 'named examples' requires.
  • Level 3 organises by design / operation / location as the question asks; Level 2 mixes them.
  • The decibel-scale point and the closing evaluation of which approaches work best lift Level 3 into the top band.
