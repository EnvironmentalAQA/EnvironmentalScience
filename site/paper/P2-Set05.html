Show mark scheme
01.16 marksMarks shared

Up to 6 marks in total - share them between the 4 points below according to how completely each one is made.

  1. Organic advantages: often waste products, locally available and cheap

    • add humus improving structure and water retention
    • increase soil biota
    • slow release reduces leaching

  2. Organic disadvantages: nutrient composition cannot be controlled

    • released slowly so needs long-term planning
    • bulky with high water content so expensive to transport
    • cannot be applied to a growing crop
    • odour / pathogens

  3. Inorganic advantages: composition controlled to match crop needs

    • nutrients released rapidly
    • concentrated and easy to apply

  4. Inorganic disadvantages: high energy (fossil fuel) manufacture (Haber process)

    • no organic matter so humus and biota decline
    • some toxic to worms
    • high solubility so leached causing eutrophication
    • raw materials (phosphate rock) finite
Also accept
  • Any other correct, relevant point of the same kind - wording never has to match these notes.
  • humus → dark, decomposed organic matter in soil
  • leaching → nutrients or ions washed down out of the soil / washed away by water
  • eutrophication → nutrient enrichment of water leading to algal growth
Do not credit
  • 'the algae use up all the oxygen' on its own - the oxygen is used by aerobic decomposers breaking down the dead algae

Indicative content — a guide to placing the mark, not a checklist:
  1. •

    Principles of natural systems: dynamic equilibria and negative feedback

    • diverse systems resistant to change
    • low energy-density renewable energy (solar-driven hydrological, carbon and nitrogen cycles)
    • low-temperature processes using enzymes
    • material cycles with wastes as raw materials
    • small number of abundant elements
    • biodegradable non-toxic wastes
  2. •

    Human systems: non-renewable energy, high temperatures (Haber process, incineration, manufacturing), linear resource use, dispersal of minerals and mixtures that are hard to separate, persistent toxins (PCBs, organochlorines, toxic metals in electronics)

  3. •

    Applications: renewable energy and heat pumps, bioleaching, recycling and design for re-use, industrial symbiosis, organic and integrated agriculture, retaining hedgerows and pollinators, circular economy

  4. •

    Human responses that override feedback (fertilisers, irrigation, pesticides) and positive feedback triggered by human activity (permafrost, methane hydrates, fires)

    • tipping points
  5. •

    Barriers: population size and affluence, attitudes of urban populations to distant resources, economics, need for international cooperation, existing infrastructure

  6. •

    Justified conclusion on how far natural principles can be applied

Also accept
  • Any other correct, relevant point of the same kind - wording never has to match these notes.
  • Any accurate, relevant material that is not in the indicative content.
  • sustainable → meeting present needs without stopping future generations meeting theirs
  • negative feedback → a change that cancels itself out / a self-correcting cycle
  • biodegradable → broken down by decomposers
Do not credit
  • 'renewables cause no pollution at all' - manufacture, construction and the land they occupy all have impacts
  • pests 'getting used to' a pesticide - the few naturally resistant individuals survive and pass the trait on
Model answers: what a Level 3 answer looks like
Model answer · Level 3 (8 marks)

Natural systems have remained sustainable because they run on low energy-density renewable energy, mainly sunlight driving the hydrological, carbon and nitrogen cycles; their reactions occur at low temperatures because enzymes lower activation energies; materials cycle so the waste of one process is the raw material of the next; only a few abundant elements are used; and wastes are biodegradable and non-toxic. Negative feedback keeps them in dynamic equilibrium and high diversity makes them resistant to change.

Human systems contrast on every point: non-renewable fossil fuels, high-temperature processes such as the Haber process and incineration, linear use that disperses minerals and mixes materials that cannot be separated, and persistent toxins like PCBs and the metals in electronics. We also override negative feedback - when degraded soil reduces yields we add fertiliser, irrigation and pesticides - and trigger positive feedback such as permafrost melting.

Copying natural principles is already possible in many areas: renewable energy and heat pumps, bioleaching at low temperature, recycling and design for disassembly, industrial symbiosis, legumes and organic matter instead of manufactured nitrate, and retaining hedgerows for pest predators - the basis of the circular economy. The limits are population size and affluence, the attitude of urban consumers who never see depleted resources, cost, and the need for international cooperation. So the principles can make individual activities far more sustainable, but only a large-scale, cooperative shift in lifestyle would make the whole human system sustainable.

Weaker answer · Level 2 (5 marks)

Natural systems use renewable energy from the Sun and recycle materials, so nothing runs out and there is no waste. Humans use fossil fuels which will run out and produce waste that is toxic. Natural processes happen at low temperatures but the Haber process needs high temperatures. Humans could copy natural systems by using renewable energy like solar and wind, recycling more and using less fertiliser. This would make human activities more sustainable. However it would be expensive and people would have to change their lifestyle.

What makes the difference
  • Level 2 states the contrast correctly but does not explain the mechanisms (enzymes, negative feedback, diversity, dispersal of minerals) that Level 3 uses.
  • Level 3 applies the principles to specific technologies (bioleaching, heat pumps, industrial symbiosis, legumes); Level 2 stays at 'recycle more'.
  • 'Evaluate the extent' - Level 3 distinguishes individual activities from the whole system in its conclusion; Level 2's evaluation is a single sentence about cost.
