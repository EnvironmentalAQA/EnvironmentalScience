Indicative content - credit any of the following (and other relevant, accurate points):
  • Stages of scientific method illustrated by ozone: identification (Antarctic UV data), hypothesis (Rowland-Molina 1974), data collection / analysis (Dobson units, satellites, balloons confirming ClO), proposed solutions, enactment (Vienna 1985, Montreal 1987)
  • Evidence of success: ozone hole area and depth stabilised and beginning to shrink; CFC concentrations in the atmosphere falling; UV levels not rising further; global ozone loss limited to about 4%
  • Reasons for success: international recognition of serious consequences (skin cancer); agreement of nearly every country; legally binding targets with a fund for developing countries; relatively few producers / products; alternatives (HCFCs, HFCs, pump sprays, hydrocarbons) already available and cheap; no lifestyle change needed
  • Limits: persistence of CFCs means recovery takes decades; some ODSs still used; HFC replacements are greenhouse gases

Model answers: what a Level 3 answer looks like
Model answer · Level 3 (9 marks)

Ozone depletion followed the scientific method almost textbook-fashion: Antarctic UV and ozone measurements identified a problem, Rowland and Molina's 1974 hypothesis explained it, Dobson-unit, satellite and balloon data confirmed chlorine monoxide in the stratosphere, and the Montreal Protocol enacted a solution. It has largely worked: CFC concentrations are falling, the ozone hole has stabilised and is starting to shrink, and global ozone loss was limited to about 4%.

Success came from particular conditions. The consequence - skin cancer - was clear and frightening, nearly every country agreed, targets were legally binding with a fund to help developing countries, there were relatively few producers and products, and alternatives (HCFCs, HFCs, hydrocarbons, pump sprays) already existed, so substitution cost little. Even so recovery will take decades because CFCs are so persistent, some ozone-depleting substances are still in use, and the HFC replacements are themselves greenhouse gases.

Climate change has none of these advantages. Greenhouse gases come from almost every activity - energy, transport, agriculture, industry - in every country, and fossil fuels underpin entire economies, so control means changing lifestyles and economies rather than swapping one chemical for another. Kyoto and Paris set weaker, largely non-binding targets, developed and developing nations dispute responsibility, and natural fluctuations, time lags and tipping points make cause and effect less obvious to the public. Emissions are still rising. The ozone story shows international agreement can work, but it was a technical substitution problem; climate change is systemic, so its lessons transfer only partly.

Weaker answer · Level 2 (4 marks)

Ozone depletion was caused by CFCs. Scientists found the hole over Antarctica and the Montreal Protocol banned CFCs, so the ozone layer is now recovering. This was successful because everyone agreed and there were alternatives to CFCs. Climate change has not been tackled as well. There have been agreements like Kyoto and Paris but carbon dioxide is still going up. This is because fossil fuels are used everywhere and countries do not agree. So ozone depletion was solved better than climate change.

What makes the difference
  • Level 2 gives the right outline but Level 3 supports it with evidence (falling CFC levels, 4% loss, Dobson units, ClO detection) and explains each reason for the difference.
  • Level 3 acknowledges the limits of the ozone success (persistence, HFCs) - balance is expected at the top level.
  • The final judgement in Level 3 characterises the difference (substitution vs systemic problem); Level 2 just restates the question.
