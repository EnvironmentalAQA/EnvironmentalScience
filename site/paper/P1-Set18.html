Model answers: what a Level 3 answer looks like
Model answer · Level 3 (8 marks)

Smoke is particulate matter from incomplete combustion of coal, diesel, wood and wastes. Its effects follow from particle size: PM10 and smaller particles stay suspended, travel far and penetrate the lungs, carrying acids, phenols and metals; smoke also reflects sunlight (cooling), blocks light for photosynthesis and blackens buildings. Under a temperature inversion in a valley it accumulates as smog, and it acts synergistically with sulfur dioxide - the London smog of 1952 killed about 12 000 people. Acid rain gases are different: sulfur dioxide from coal combustion and sulfide smelting, and nitrogen oxides from high-temperature combustion, are reactive and soluble, forming sulfuric and nitric acids that travel long distances across borders. Low pH denatures proteins in exposed cells such as gills, eggs and root hairs, dissolves exoskeletons and limestone, corrodes metals and leaches calcium and magnesium from soils while mobilising toxic aluminium, with severity depending on bedrock.

Smoke control has been the clearest success: the Clean Air Act's smokeless zones, the switch to gas and electricity, electrostatic precipitators, cyclones, bag filters and diesel particulate filters have virtually eliminated smoke smogs in developed countries. Sulfur dioxide has also fallen dramatically through fuel desulfurisation, flue-gas desulfurisation and the move from coal to gas and renewables, and lake pH is recovering, although FGD is costly and produces waste. Nitrogen oxides remain the hardest: catalytic converters and fluidised-bed combustion work, but converters need unleaded fuel and warm engines and vehicle numbers keep rising. Controls have therefore succeeded for particulates and sulfur, only partly for nitrogen.

Weaker answer · Level 2 (5 marks)

Smoke is made of small particles from burning coal and diesel. It causes breathing problems and smogs like in London in 1952. Acid rain is caused by sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides which dissolve in rain and make it acidic. Acid rain kills fish and trees and damages buildings. Smoke is controlled by smokeless zones and filters. Acid rain is controlled by taking the sulfur out of fuel and catalytic converters in cars. These methods have reduced pollution a lot, especially smoke.

What makes the difference
  • Level 3 links each effect to a property (particle size, solubility, reactivity, pH denaturing proteins); Level 2 describes effects without the mechanism.
  • Level 3 evaluates each control separately with evidence (recovering lake pH, cost of FGD, why NOx is harder); Level 2 gives one general sentence.
  • Both answers use the 1952 smog, but Level 3 explains the conditions (inversion, synergism with SO2) that caused it.
