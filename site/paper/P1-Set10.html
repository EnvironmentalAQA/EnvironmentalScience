Question 210 marks · Noise pollution · spec 3.4.3.2.13 · Genn pp. 293-295, 418 · NOISE-05

A motor-racing circuit has residents living 500 m away. The circuit operator wants to compare two designs of acoustic barrier: an earth baffle mound and a timber acoustic fence.

[5 marks]02.1Describe how you would carry out an investigation to compare the effectiveness of the two barriers.
[1 mark]02.2Explain why readings should be taken with the same wind direction.
[2 marks]02.3Explain the difference between the LA10 and LA90 measures of traffic noise.
[2 marks]02.4Outline two ways in which the residents' houses could be designed to reduce the noise reaching occupants.
Show mark scheme
02.1 (5 marks)
  • Use a calibrated sound meter (dB(A)) at fixed distances (eg 10, 50, 100, 250, 500 m) from the track behind each barrier and at a control site with no barrier
  • Standardise the noise source: same cars / same race or a standard test noise; same time of day, wind speed / direction, weather; same height of meter above ground
  • Take repeated readings at each distance and calculate means (and standard deviation) to assess variability
  • Use a measure such as LA10 or Leq over a set period rather than a single peak reading
  • Compare reduction in dB relative to the control at each distance; plot noise level against distance for each barrier; statistical test (t-test) to assess significance
/ 5
02.2 (1 marks)
  • Wind carries sound / affects how far it travels so different wind directions would change readings independently of the barrier
/ 1
02.3 (2 marks)
  • LA10 is the noise level exceeded for 10% of the period - a measure of the noisiest periods
  • LA90 is the level exceeded for 90% of the period - a measure of the quietest / background level
/ 2
02.4 (2 marks)
Any 2 from:
  • Double / triple glazing
  • Acoustic insulation in walls / roof
  • Bedrooms on the side facing away from the track
  • Dense hedges / trees and earth mounds in gardens
/ 2
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 293-295, 418; AQA spec 3.4.3.2.13.

Question 410 marks · Solid wastes · spec 3.4.3.2.12 · Genn pp. 282-283 · SW-04

A coal-mine spoil heap on a hillside has been abandoned. The spoil contains shale with some iron pyrites and residual coal.

[6 marks]04.1Explain three problems this spoil heap could cause and describe how each could be managed.
[2 marks]04.2Explain why deep layers of soil are applied when a spoil heap containing toxic materials is restored.
[2 marks]04.3Explain why an old steel-works spoil heap may be valuable for wildlife conservation.
Show mark scheme
04.1 (6 marks)
  • Instability / landslips on the hillside, especially after heavy rain - compaction, drainage, establishing vegetation whose roots bind the spoil, regrading slopes
  • Acid drainage from oxidation of pyrites, with toxic metals - collect and treat leachate, add lime to raise pH
  • Spontaneous combustion of residual coal / hydrocarbons - layers of fine-grained material to reduce air flow
  • Lack of nutrients / poor plant growth - add topsoil, sewage sludge, fertiliser
  • Unattractive topography - landscaping / reshaping
  • Dust / heavy metals - cover and keep at high pH
/ 6
04.2 (2 marks)
  • So that plant roots cannot reach the toxic layer
  • Otherwise toxins could be taken up and brought to the surface / dispersed via leaf litter and food chains
/ 2
04.3 (2 marks)
  • Steel slag is alkaline, producing unusual calcareous / high-pH soil conditions
  • Supports uncommon plants such as cowslips / orchids that cannot compete on fertile neutral soils
/ 2
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 282-283; AQA spec 3.4.3.2.12.

Question 910 marks · Thermal & oil pollution · spec 3.4.3.2.7 · Genn pp. 261-262 · WPOL-04

In 2020 the tanker MV Wakashio ran aground on a coral reef off Mauritius and leaked about 1000 tonnes of fuel oil into a shallow lagoon near mangroves and a marine park.

[3 marks]09.1Explain why inflatable booms were deployed immediately, and give one limitation of booms.
[3 marks]09.2Describe how skimmers and absorbent materials remove oil, and state how the collected oil is disposed of.
[2 marks]09.3Explain why dispersants were not used in the lagoon.
[2 marks]09.4Explain how bioremediation could help remove the oil that remained on the mangrove sediments, and suggest how it could be accelerated.
Show mark scheme
09.1 (3 marks)
  • Booms are floating tubes with a skirt that restrict the movement of floating oil, keeping it near the source / away from sensitive sites (mangroves, reef, marine park)
  • They work best in sheltered / calm water such as the lagoon
  • Limitation: they do not work in open water where currents or waves are strong; oil can flow under the skirt in a strong current
/ 3
09.2 (3 marks)
  • Skimmers: rotating (metal) discs / belts pick up floating oil which is scraped off and stored
  • Absorbent materials (high surface area, eg textile mops, straw, human hair) soak up oil and are then removed
  • Collected oil / absorbents are incinerated or sent to landfill (or oil recycled)
/ 3
09.3 (2 marks)
  • Dispersants break the oil into an emulsion of droplets, making it more mobile so it spreads through the water column (to the reef / seabed organisms) rather than staying on the surface
  • They do not reduce the amount of oil; the dispersant / oil mixture may be toxic to corals and plankton in shallow, enclosed water
/ 2
09.4 (2 marks)
  • Bacteria that break down hydrocarbons digest the residual oil (most rapid where temperature, oxygen and nutrients are high)
  • Accelerated by adding nutrients (nitrates / phosphates) and aerating (ploughing) / irrigating the sediment
/ 2
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 261-262; AQA spec 3.4.3.2.7.
Question 1010 marks · Properties of pollutants & environmental factors · spec 3.4.1 · Genn pp. 236-240 · PPRO-07
[9 marks]10.1Discuss how an understanding of the properties of pollutants can be used to predict their behaviour in the environment and to plan strategies to control them.
[1 mark]10.2State what is meant by bioaccumulation.
Show mark scheme
10.1 (9 marks)
Levels of response: L3 7-9 comprehensive, well-structured, applied to context, supported judgement; L2 4-6 sound but incomplete application/evaluation; L1 1-3 basic, limited structure.
Indicative content:
  • State of matter: solids deposit near the source, gases disperse widely in the atmosphere; density (lead dust, hydrogen cyanide at Bhopal) controls how far particles/gases travel
  • Persistence / half-life: persistent pollutants (organochlorines, CFCs, heavy metals, radioactive wastes with long half-lives) travel further, accumulate and need containment rather than dilution; non-persistent pollutants (sewage, organophosphates) can be allowed to degrade
  • Toxicity, specificity, mode of action (enzyme inhibition, neurotoxins) determine acceptable concentrations and which species are at risk; LD50 / MDAF / MDNF used to set doses
  • Solubility in water (nitrates disperse and leach - control by nitrate vulnerable zones) vs liposolubility (DDT, mercury bioaccumulate and biomagnify - control by bans / restrictions)
  • Reactivity: reactive pollutants may degrade quickly or form secondary pollutants (ozone, acid rain, PANs) - controlling primary pollutants (catalytic converters, FGD) prevents secondary pollutants
  • Adsorption onto clay / sediments immobilises metals and caesium; can be used deliberately (activated carbon filters, polymer adsorption)
  • Mutagenic / carcinogenic / teratogenic action means no safe dose; exposure kept As Low As Reasonably Achievable (ALARA)
  • Synergism (ozone + SO2; smoke + SO2) means pollutants must be considered together
  • Environmental features (temperature inversions, currents, pH, oxygen) interact with properties: Critical Pathway Analysis uses properties + environment to predict where pollutants go and to focus monitoring
  • Evaluation: properties of new chemicals can be predicted from similar ones (precautionary principle); understanding allows prevention rather than clean-up
/ 9
10.2 (1 marks)
  • The increase in the amount / concentration of a substance within an (individual) organism over time, because intake exceeds excretion or breakdown
/ 1
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 236-240; AQA spec 3.4.1.
