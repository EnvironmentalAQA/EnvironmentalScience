Question 110 marks · Water treatment, conservation & new sources · spec 3.2.2.5 · Genn pp. 122-124 · HYD-07
[4 marks]01.1Describe four factors that should be considered when choosing a site for a dam and reservoir.
[3 marks]01.2Give one advantage and two disadvantages of an estuary barrage compared with a reservoir on land.
[2 marks]01.3Explain why desalination of seawater is only used in some countries.
[1 mark]01.4Explain how artificial recharge can make aquifer supplies sustainable.
Show mark scheme
01.1 (4 marks)
Any 4 from:
  • Topography: a narrow exit from a large deep basin so a small dam holds a large volume
  • Geology: impermeable rock beneath (no seepage), strong enough to support the dam, no faults / seismic activity
  • Catchment area: large enough with regular, reliable rainfall / river inflow; climate not too hot (evaporation losses)
  • Catchment land use: low pollution risk (industry, pesticides); low soil erosion (sedimentation reduces capacity)
  • Infrastructure / proximity to demand: access for construction, cost of transporting water
  • Existing land use: value of what is lost (urban areas, conservation sites, farmland) - loss:benefit analysis
01.2 (3 marks)
  • Advantage: fewer land-use conflicts as no land is flooded; large volume near coastal cities
  • Disadvantages: destroys / changes important intertidal habitats (mudflats for wading birds); obstacle to shipping; collects pollution from the whole river catchment; sedimentation
01.3 (2 marks)
  • Very energy-intensive and expensive (reverse osmosis / distillation)
  • Only worthwhile where seawater is available and freshwater supplies are inadequate (eg Malta, Middle East)
01.4 (1 marks)
  • Surplus water in wet periods (when infiltration capacity is exceeded and runoff would go to the sea) is pumped underground or diverted into lagoons to infiltrate, so abstraction in dry periods is balanced by recharge
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 122-124; AQA spec 3.2.2.5.

[3 marks]03.1Explain why clay particles do not settle out in the sedimentation stage, and describe how they are removed in stage 4.
[2 marks]03.2Explain the purpose of aeration (stage 3).
[2 marks]03.3Explain why ozone or UV light may be used for sterilisation instead of chlorine when water comes from peaty upland sources.
[3 marks]03.4Describe how each of the following contaminants is removed from water.
Show mark scheme
03.1 (3 marks)
  • Clay particles are very small and carry electrostatic charges on their surfaces so they repel each other and stay suspended
  • Flocculants (aluminium sulfate / alum or polyelectrolytes) are added and mixed to neutralise the charges
  • The particles clump together (flocs) and settle in the clarifier tank
03.2 (2 marks)
  • Increases dissolved oxygen and removes hydrogen sulfide (bad-egg smell) from anaerobic sources
  • Oxidises some dissolved metals (eg iron) so they become insoluble and can be removed; improves taste
03.3 (2 marks)
  • Peaty water contains organic matter which reacts with chlorine to produce toxic substances (eg trihalomethanes)
  • Ozone / UV kill pathogens without forming these by-products (though chlorine / chloramine keeps water sterile during distribution)
03.4 (3 marks)
  • Ion exchange: toxic ions adsorb onto polymer resin beads or zeolite (also used to soften water by removing calcium / magnesium)
  • Reverse osmosis / distillation (desalination) - expensive, energy-intensive
  • Activated carbon: organic molecules causing odour / taste and pesticides adsorb onto the high-surface-area carbon particles
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 126-128; AQA spec 3.2.2.4.
Question 45 marks · Water treatment, conservation & new sources · spec 3.2.2.4-3.2.2.5 · Genn pp. 122-128 · HYD-10
[1 mark]04.1Which of the following water sources is usually the most convenient for large communities? Tick one box.
  • A  Desalinated seawater
  • B  River water
  • C  Rainwater catchment
  • D  Newly discovered aquifers
[2 marks]04.2Explain why the quality requirements for water differ between uses, using two named uses.
[2 marks]04.3Explain why inter-basin transfers may be used and give one disadvantage.
Show mark scheme
04.1 (1 marks)
  • B - rivers are accessible over a large area with few natural contaminants
04.2 (2 marks)
  • Potable / public supply: no pathogens, low toxins, good appearance / taste / smell (public health)
  • Industrial boiler / cooling water: soft water with low calcium so mineral deposits do not block pipes or reduce heat exchange; spray irrigation: low turbidity to avoid blocked pipes and low heavy metals
04.3 (2 marks)
  • Canals / pipes move water from areas with a surplus to areas with a shortage (eg Wales to England, China's Yangtze transfers)
  • Disadvantage: high cost / energy for pumping; ecological impacts on the donor river; transfer of pollutants or invasive species; political conflict

Question 715 marks · Water treatment, conservation & new sources · spec 3.2.2.4-3.2.2.5 · Genn pp. 122-127 · HYD-13

Figure 1 shows the river level below a reservoir site during one year, before and after a river-regulation reservoir was built.

Figure 1
[2 marks]07.1Use Figure 1 to calculate the reduction in the annual range of river level produced by the reservoir.
[2 marks]07.2Explain how the reservoir produces the changes shown in Figure 1.
[2 marks]07.3Explain why human activities in the catchment may have increased the extremes in river flow before the reservoir was built.
[9 marks]07.4Discuss the factors that should be considered when selecting a site for a new reservoir, and evaluate the alternative strategies for increasing the sustainability of water supplies.
Show mark scheme
07.1 (2 marks)
  • No reservoir range = 3.9 - 0.7 = 3.2 m; with reservoir = 2.7 - 1.7 = 1.0 m
  • Reduction = 2.2 m
07.2 (2 marks)
  • After heavy rainfall surplus water is stored, reducing peak flows / flooding downstream
  • During dry periods the dam is opened to release water and maintain river levels
07.3 (2 marks)
  • Deforestation / urbanisation reduce infiltration and interception
  • So runoff reaches the river faster after rain and less water is stored to maintain flow in dry periods
07.4 (9 marks)
Levels of response: L3 7-9 comprehensive, well-structured, applied to context, supported judgement; L2 4-6 sound but incomplete application/evaluation; L1 1-3 basic, limited structure.
Indicative content:
  • Site factors: topography - narrow exit from a large deep basin so a small dam holds a large volume; impermeable, strong bedrock without faults or seismic activity; large catchment with regular high rainfall and low evaporation; catchment land use with low pollution risk (pesticides, industry) and low soil erosion to avoid sedimentation; forest / vegetation in the flooded area decaying anaerobically releasing methane; access for workers and materials and distance to demand; value of the land lost (urban, conservation, agricultural) - loss-benefit analysis differs between countries
  • Alternatives for supply: rainwater collection, rivers, estuary barrages, desalination (energy-intensive so only where freshwater is inadequate), unexploited aquifers (N Africa, Kenya), inter-basin transfers (China, Australia, Wales-England), artificial aquifer recharge using surplus wet-season water, river-regulation reservoirs, afforestation to even out flow
  • Conservation strategies: low-water appliances, dual-flush toilets, xeriscaping, drip irrigation, grey-water re-use, leak repair (about 20% lost in the UK), metering
  • Treatment / pollution control allowing re-use of contaminated sources: sewage treatment, buffer strips, mine drainage control
  • Evaluation: reservoirs give large reliable supplies and flood control but flood land, cause habitat loss and methane; conservation is cheap and quick but limited by behaviour; transfers create conflicts between regions; desalination has high energy / carbon cost; combination needed as demand grows
  • Justified conclusion
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 122-127; AQA spec 3.2.2.4-3.2.2.5.
