Question 110 marks · Marine productivity & fishing · spec 3.5.2.1 · Genn pp. 338-339 · FISH-01

Figure 1 shows the seasonal changes in light intensity, nutrient concentration and phytoplankton biomass in the surface water of a temperate ocean in the northern hemisphere.

Figure 1
[4 marks]01.1Use Figure 1 to explain the spring peak and summer decline in phytoplankton biomass.
[2 marks]01.2Explain why tropical open oceans have low productivity all year despite high light and temperature.
[3 marks]01.3Explain why areas of upwelling and coastal waters are the most productive parts of the sea.
[1 mark]01.4Explain why phosphate is often the limiting nutrient in the sea.
Show mark scheme
01.1 4 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1In winter nutrients are high (storms disturb sediments, deep mixing) but light is too low for photosynthesis
  • 1In spring light rises while nutrients are still high so growth is rapid - the spring bloom (peak about May)
  • 1The bloom uses up nutrients (falling to 10 by July); dead plankton sink out of the photic zone carrying nutrients to the seabed
  • 1So nutrients become limiting in summer despite high light; a smaller autumn bloom follows when storms return nutrients while light is still adequate
01.2 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Warm surface water is stable (no seasonal mixing / storms) so nutrients lost to deep water in sinking dead organisms are not replaced
  • 1Nutrient (especially phosphate) concentrations in the photic zone remain very low - the limiting factor
01.3 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Upwelling (offshore winds, seamounts, currents deflected upwards) brings cold nutrient-rich deep water into the photic zone
  • 1Coastal waters receive nutrients washed off the land in rivers and storms disturb shallow sediments
  • 1Nutrients plus light in the shallow photic zone allow high algal productivity supporting rich food webs and fisheries (eg Peru anchovy)
01.4 1 mark
1 mark.
  • 1Phosphates have low solubility and no atmospheric reservoir; they are only added by river runoff or upwelling and are lost to sediments

Question 510 marks · Marine productivity & fishing · spec 3.5.2.2 · Genn pp. 344-348 · FISH-05
[8 marks]05.1Explain how each of the following reduces the environmental impact of fishing.
[2 marks]05.2Explain why catch quotas work well for pelagic species such as mackerel but poorly in mixed demersal fisheries.
Show mark scheme
05.1 8 marks
2 marks for each point (partial credit for a partly correct point).
  • 2Normal diagonal mesh closes as the net fills / stretches so small fish cannot escape; square-mesh panels do not close so juveniles escape to grow and breed; TEDs let turtles out; larger mesh sizes generally
  • 2Minimum size lets fish reach breeding age before capture (eg UK lobster minimum carapace above breeding size, cod 35 cm); maximum size / V-notching egg-carrying females protects large, highly fecund breeding adults (Hebridean lobsters over 145 mm, Nassau grouper)
  • 2NTZs (Lundy, Lamlash Bay, Chagos, Great Barrier Reef) protect breeding populations and habitats; surplus young spill over and increase surrounding catches; damaged communities recover
  • 2Closed seasons, ideally in the breeding season, let fish grow and breed; fishermen may oppose because shoaling breeders are easy to catch
05.2 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Pelagic species form single-species shoals so fishing stops when the quota is reached
  • 1In mixed fisheries many species are caught together; once one species' quota is reached it must be discarded (dead) while fishing for others continues

Indicative content - credit any of the following (and other relevant, accurate points):
  • Impacts: population decline of k-selected species, by-catch (immature target fish, over-quota fish, non-commercial species; drift nets - whales, dolphins, turtles; longlines - albatrosses; pair trawls - porpoises), ghost fishing, seabed damage by demersal trawls, coral and seagrass damage, dynamite fishing, food-web effects

Model answers: what a Level 3 answer looks like
Model answer · Level 3 (8 marks)

Fishing's impacts go beyond depleting target species: k-selected species that breed late and produce few young collapse and recover slowly; by-catch of immature target fish, over-quota fish and non-commercial species is thrown back dead, with drift nets killing whales, dolphins and turtles, longlines drowning albatrosses and pair trawls porpoises; lost gear keeps ghost fishing; demersal trawls destroy sea fans, corals and seagrass; and removing any species alters its competitors, prey and predators.

Catch quotas limit the weight landed and work well for single-species pelagic shoals where fishing stops when the quota is reached, but in mixed demersal fisheries over-quota fish are discarded dead, so the quota does not protect them. Gear design tackles by-catch directly: mesh sizes and square-mesh escape panels let small fish out because, unlike diagonal mesh, they do not close under drag; turtle exclusion devices, acoustic pingers for dolphins, curved hooks, decoys, sinkers and night setting protect turtles, cetaceans and albatrosses; biodegradable and radio-tracked traps limit ghost fishing. Restricting effort - boat size, engine power, days at sea, decommissioning - reduces total pressure, and banning drift nets or demersal trawling on sensitive seabeds such as Rockall Bank protects habitats.

Effectiveness depends on enforcement at sea, which is difficult, on reliable stock data for quotas, on the species and fishery type, and on international cooperation because fish migrate; some measures cut catches or raise costs, and as local stocks fall fleets simply move further away. Gear modifications are the most effective per unit cost because they reduce damage without reducing legal catch; quotas and effort limits only work where they are enforced.

Weaker answer · Level 2 (5 marks)

Fishing damages the environment by overfishing, catching by-catch such as dolphins and damaging the seabed with trawling. Quotas limit how many fish can be caught so stocks can recover. Bigger mesh sizes let small fish escape so they can grow and breed. Turtle exclusion devices let turtles escape from nets. Some fishing methods can be banned in sensitive areas. No-take zones let fish breed. These methods help but they are hard to enforce at sea and some fishermen ignore them.

What makes the difference
  • Level 2 covers impacts and methods soundly but without the mechanisms (why square mesh works, why quotas fail in mixed fisheries).
  • Level 3 evaluates each method on its own terms and then compares them; Level 2's evaluation is a single general sentence.
  • Named examples (Rockall Bank, albatross measures, ghost-fishing controls) give Level 3 the range the top band expects.
