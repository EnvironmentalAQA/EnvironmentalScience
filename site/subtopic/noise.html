Question 110 marks · Noise pollution · spec 3.4.3.2.13 · Genn pp. 286-287 · NOISE-01

Table 1 shows typical noise levels of some sounds.

Table 1

Threshold of human hearing (at 1000 Hz)0
Leaves rustling20

[2 marks]01.1Explain why a logarithmic scale is used to measure sound.
[2 marks]01.2Use Table 1 to calculate how many times greater the sound intensity of a chain saw is than that of a busy road.
[2 marks]01.3A machine produces 85 dB. A second identical machine is switched on next to it. State the new noise level and explain your answer.
[1 mark]01.4Explain what is meant by the statement that 0 dB does not mean there is no sound.
[3 marks]01.5State the frequency range of hearing for a typical young person and explain why the dB(A) scale is used for human hearing.
Show mark scheme
01.1 (2 marks)
  • The human ear is sensitive to a very wide range of sound pressures / powers (about 1012 range)
  • A logarithmic scale compresses this into a convenient range of numbers: each 10 dB increase represents a ten-fold increase in sound intensity
01.2 (2 marks)
  • Difference = 30 dB = 3 x 10 dB
  • = 103 = 1000 times
01.3 (2 marks)
  • 88 dB
  • Doubling the sound intensity increases the level by 3 dB (logarithmic scale)
01.4 (1 marks)
  • 0 dB is set as the quietest sound that can be heard (at 1000 Hz) - the threshold of human hearing; quieter sounds exist but cannot be heard
01.5 (3 marks)
  • 20 to 20 000 Hz
  • dB(A) weights sounds between about 1000 and 4000 Hz more heavily
  • Because the nerve cells / hair cells in the cochlea are more sensitive to these frequencies so they are heard more easily / cause more annoyance; range narrows with age or noise damage

Question 210 marks · Noise pollution · spec 3.4.3.2.13 · Genn pp. 287 · NOISE-02
[3 marks]02.1Describe three effects of noise on human health other than hearing damage.
[3 marks]02.2Explain how noise can damage non-living objects.
[2 marks]02.3Explain how noise can reduce the breeding success of birds.
[2 marks]02.4Explain why military sonar is a threat to cetaceans.
Show mark scheme
02.1 (3 marks)
Any 3 from:
  • Stress-related illness: ulcers, high blood pressure, heart disease
  • Behavioural changes: irritability, aggression, lack of concentration leading to accidents
  • Communication problems / educational underperformance (eg schools under flight paths)
  • Sleep disturbance
02.2 (3 marks)
  • Acoustic fatigue: objects exposed to sound at their natural resonant frequency vibrate, causing stress cracking / structural failure (eg early jet engine cowlings)
  • Vibration damage: repetitive vibration from heavy vehicles damages buildings, bridges, underground pipes
  • Shock impacts: the force of a sudden very loud noise (sonic boom) breaks windows / dislodges masonry
02.3 (2 marks)
  • Birds that sing to establish territories / attract mates cannot be heard against background noise
  • Birds scared by sudden noise leave nests so predators take eggs / chicks; cliff-nesting seabirds knock eggs or chicks into the sea
02.4 (2 marks)
  • Whales / dolphins use hearing / echolocation to find food and communicate; loud sonar causes hearing damage if close to the source
  • Behavioural changes / disorientation possibly linked to mass strandings; reduced feeding success
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 287; AQA spec 3.4.3.2.13.
Question 310 marks · Noise pollution · spec 3.4.3.2.13 · Genn pp. 288-292 · NOISE-03

Table 1 shows the bypass ratio and first year of use of jet engines used on four airliners.

Table 1
EngineAircraftBypass ratioFirst use
RR OlympusConcorde0 : 1Early 1950s
RR ConwayBoeing 7070.25 : 1Late 1950s
P&W JT9DBoeing 7475 : 11969
RR Trent 1000Boeing 78710 : 12011
[3 marks]03.1Explain how a high bypass ratio reduces engine noise.
[2 marks]03.2Describe two other features of aircraft design that reduce noise.
[4 marks]03.3Explain how each of the following airport operating procedures reduces noise nuisance.
[1 mark]03.4Explain why aircraft are noisier on take-off than on landing.
Show mark scheme
03.1 (3 marks)
  • A turbofan at the front forces bypass air around the inner cowling / core jet
  • The bypass air smooths the flow of the turbulent hot exhaust gases, reducing the turbulence that creates noise
  • The higher the ratio of bypass air to jet exhaust the quieter the engine (Trent 1000 quietest, Olympus noisiest)
03.2 (2 marks)
Any 2 from:
  • Chevron (serrated) nozzles mix bypass air with surrounding air more smoothly
  • Acoustic liners in the engine cowlings absorb noise
  • Hush kits fitted to older low-bypass engines
  • Fairings on undercarriage and wing-flap hinges reduce turbulence; welded rather than riveted panels
  • Lighter composite aircraft need less thrust
  • Blended wing with engines on top so the body shields the ground
03.3 (4 marks)
  • Constant (3°) descent avoids periods of high thrust in a stepped descent and keeps aircraft higher for longer
  • Night flights (especially take-offs, which are noisier) are restricted / banned when people are sleeping and background noise is low
  • Noisier aircraft pay higher charges / use up more quota points and fines for exceeding limits, so airlines are encouraged to buy quieter aircraft
  • More time to slow down after landing so wheel brakes can be used instead of noisy reverse thrusters
03.4 (1 marks)
  • Engines run at higher power / maximum thrust and the steep climb angle needs full power (although the aircraft climbs quickly out of earshot)
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 288-292; AQA spec 3.4.3.2.13.

[1 mark]04.2Explain why road traffic noise affects more people than any other form of noise pollution.
[3 marks]04.3Describe three ways of reducing road traffic noise reaching residents.
[2 marks]04.4Suggest how noise from pile-driving at sea for wind turbine foundations can be reduced to protect cetaceans.
Show mark scheme
04.1 (4 marks)
  • Wheel vibration: track polishing / grinding, sound-absorbing ballast, sound-absorbing suspension
  • Pantograph: aerodynamic fairing
  • Wheel squeal: lubrication of wheels / track on corners
  • Braking: composite-material brakes instead of cast iron
04.2 (1 marks)
  • Roads pass through / near almost all residential areas and traffic is continuous through the day, unlike airports or railways which affect limited areas
04.3 (3 marks)
Any 3 from:
  • Sound-absorbing road surfaces (porous asphalt / rubber-crumb asphalt)
  • Acoustic fences, embankments / baffle mounds, walls or dense tree planting
  • Traffic management for free flow (less acceleration noise); re-routing traffic away from residential areas
  • Double glazing / acoustic insulation of buildings
  • Vehicle design: better aerodynamics, engine insulation, quieter exhausts, quieter tyres
04.4 (2 marks)
Any 2 from:
  • Air bubble curtains around the pile driver absorb noise
  • Acoustic survey to detect cetaceans first and delay work if present; increase volume gradually (soft start) so animals can leave
  • Drilling instead of pile-driving

Question 510 marks · Noise pollution · spec 3.4.3.2.13 · Genn pp. 293-295, 418 · NOISE-05

A motor-racing circuit has residents living 500 m away. The circuit operator wants to compare two designs of acoustic barrier: an earth baffle mound and a timber acoustic fence.

[5 marks]05.1Describe how you would carry out an investigation to compare the effectiveness of the two barriers.
[1 mark]05.2Explain why readings should be taken with the same wind direction.
[2 marks]05.3Explain the difference between the LA10 and LA90 measures of traffic noise.
[2 marks]05.4Outline two ways in which the residents' houses could be designed to reduce the noise reaching occupants.
Show mark scheme
05.1 (5 marks)
  • Use a calibrated sound meter (dB(A)) at fixed distances (eg 10, 50, 100, 250, 500 m) from the track behind each barrier and at a control site with no barrier
  • Standardise the noise source: same cars / same race or a standard test noise; same time of day, wind speed / direction, weather; same height of meter above ground
  • Take repeated readings at each distance and calculate means (and standard deviation) to assess variability
  • Use a measure such as LA10 or Leq over a set period rather than a single peak reading
  • Compare reduction in dB relative to the control at each distance; plot noise level against distance for each barrier; statistical test (t-test) to assess significance
05.2 (1 marks)
  • Wind carries sound / affects how far it travels so different wind directions would change readings independently of the barrier
05.3 (2 marks)
  • LA10 is the noise level exceeded for 10% of the period - a measure of the noisiest periods
  • LA90 is the level exceeded for 90% of the period - a measure of the quietest / background level
05.4 (2 marks)
Any 2 from:
  • Double / triple glazing
  • Acoustic insulation in walls / roof
  • Bedrooms on the side facing away from the track
  • Dense hedges / trees and earth mounds in gardens
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 293-295, 418; AQA spec 3.4.3.2.13.
Question 610 marks · Noise pollution · spec 3.4.3.2.13 · Genn pp. 286-295 · NOISE-06
[9 marks]06.1Using named examples, explain how the sources of noise pollution can be controlled by changes to design, operation and location.
[1 mark]06.2State what is meant by the Leq 57 dB contour used around UK airports.
Show mark scheme
06.1 (9 marks)
Levels of response: L3 7-9 comprehensive, well-structured, applied to context, supported judgement; L2 4-6 sound but incomplete application/evaluation; L1 1-3 basic, limited structure.
Indicative content:
  • Principles: dB is logarithmic (10 dB = 10x; 3 dB = 2x) so small reductions in dB are large reductions in intensity; effects on humans (hearing, stress, behaviour) and wildlife (breeding birds, bats, cetaceans)
  • Aircraft design: high bypass ratio engines, chevron nozzles, hush kits, acoustic liners, fairings, composites, blended wing
  • Aircraft operation: take-off angle, flight paths avoiding urban areas, constant descent, reduced reverse thrust, night restrictions, Chapter noise limits, fines and noise charges, quota count
  • Airport design / location: away from population centres, taxi and engine-test areas away from housing, acoustic walls, baffle mounds, land-use restrictions, free acoustic insulation
  • Railway: track polishing, sound-absorbing ballast and suspension, pantograph fairings, wheel lubrication, composite brakes
  • Road: porous / rubber asphalt, acoustic fences, embankments, tree planting, aerodynamics, engine insulation, traffic management and routing, double glazing
  • Industrial: silencers on compressors, pressing instead of stamping, nylon rollers, drilling instead of pile-driving, bubble curtains, timing of blasting, acoustic mats / curtains, remote operation, ear protection
  • Domestic: acoustic absorbers in appliances, volume limiters, selection of quieter appliances, ear defenders
  • Evaluation: which approaches are most effective / cost-effective; conflicts (airports near cities for convenience); legislation and monitoring (Leq 57 dB contours) needed to enforce
06.2 (1 marks)
  • The area within which the average (equivalent continuous) sound level between 07:00 and 23:00 in summer exceeds 57 dB - taken as the onset of significant community annoyance
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 286-295; AQA spec 3.4.3.2.13.
