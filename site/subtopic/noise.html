[2 marks]01.1Explain why a logarithmic scale is used to measure sound.
[2 marks]01.2Use Table 1 to calculate how many times greater the sound intensity of a chain saw is than that of a busy road.
[2 marks]01.3A machine produces 85 dB. A second identical machine is switched on next to it. State the new noise level and explain your answer.

Show mark scheme
01.1 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1The human ear is sensitive to a very wide range of sound pressures / powers (about 1012 range)
  • 1A logarithmic scale compresses this into a convenient range of numbers: each 10 dB increase represents a ten-fold increase in sound intensity
01.2 2 marks
A correct final answer (with unit where asked) scores full marks. If the answer is wrong, award 1 mark for each correct step of working shown.
  • 1Difference = 30 dB = 3 x 10 dB
  • 1= 103 = 1000 times
01.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 188 dB
  • 1Doubling the sound intensity increases the level by 3 dB (logarithmic scale)
01.4 1 mark
1 mark.
  • 10 dB is set as the quietest sound that can be heard (at 1000 Hz) - the threshold of human hearing; quieter sounds exist but cannot be heard
01.5 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 120 to 20 000 Hz
  • 1dB(A) weights sounds between about 1000 and 4000 Hz more heavily
  • 1Because the nerve cells / hair cells in the cochlea are more sensitive to these frequencies so they are heard more easily / cause more annoyance; range narrows with age or noise damage

Question 210 marks · Noise pollution · spec 3.4.3.2.13 · Genn pp. 287 · NOISE-02
[3 marks]02.1Describe three effects of noise on human health other than hearing damage.
[3 marks]02.2Explain how noise can damage non-living objects.
[2 marks]02.3Explain how noise can reduce the breeding success of birds.
[2 marks]02.4Explain why military sonar is a threat to cetaceans.
Show mark scheme
02.1 3 marks
Any 3 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 3.
  • 1Stress-related illness: ulcers, high blood pressure, heart disease
  • 1Behavioural changes: irritability, aggression, lack of concentration leading to accidents
  • 1Communication problems / educational underperformance (eg schools under flight paths)
  • 1Sleep disturbance
02.2 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Acoustic fatigue: objects exposed to sound at their natural resonant frequency vibrate, causing stress cracking / structural failure (eg early jet engine cowlings)
  • 1Vibration damage: repetitive vibration from heavy vehicles damages buildings, bridges, underground pipes
  • 1Shock impacts: the force of a sudden very loud noise (sonic boom) breaks windows / dislodges masonry
02.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Birds that sing to establish territories / attract mates cannot be heard against background noise
  • 1Birds scared by sudden noise leave nests so predators take eggs / chicks; cliff-nesting seabirds knock eggs or chicks into the sea
02.4 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Whales / dolphins use hearing / echolocation to find food and communicate; loud sonar causes hearing damage if close to the source
  • 1Behavioural changes / disorientation possibly linked to mass strandings; reduced feeding success
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 287; AQA spec 3.4.3.2.13.

Table 1 shows the bypass ratio and first year of use of jet engines used on four airliners.

Table 1
EngineAircraftBypass ratioFirst use
RR OlympusConcorde0 : 1Early 1950s

P&W JT9DBoeing 7475 : 11969

[3 marks]03.1Explain how a high bypass ratio reduces engine noise.
[2 marks]03.2Describe two other features of aircraft design that reduce noise.
[4 marks]03.3Explain how each of the following airport operating procedures reduces noise nuisance.
[1 mark]03.4Explain why aircraft are noisier on take-off than on landing.
Show mark scheme
03.1 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1A turbofan at the front forces bypass air around the inner cowling / core jet
  • 1The bypass air smooths the flow of the turbulent hot exhaust gases, reducing the turbulence that creates noise
  • 1The higher the ratio of bypass air to jet exhaust the quieter the engine (Trent 1000 quietest, Olympus noisiest)
03.2 2 marks
Any 2 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 2.
  • 1Chevron (serrated) nozzles mix bypass air with surrounding air more smoothly
  • 1Acoustic liners in the engine cowlings absorb noise
  • 1Hush kits fitted to older low-bypass engines
  • 1Fairings on undercarriage and wing-flap hinges reduce turbulence; welded rather than riveted panels
  • 1Lighter composite aircraft need less thrust
  • 1Blended wing with engines on top so the body shields the ground
03.3 4 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Constant (3°) descent avoids periods of high thrust in a stepped descent and keeps aircraft higher for longer
  • 1Night flights (especially take-offs, which are noisier) are restricted / banned when people are sleeping and background noise is low
  • 1Noisier aircraft pay higher charges / use up more quota points and fines for exceeding limits, so airlines are encouraged to buy quieter aircraft
  • 1More time to slow down after landing so wheel brakes can be used instead of noisy reverse thrusters
03.4 1 mark
1 mark.
  • 1Engines run at higher power / maximum thrust and the steep climb angle needs full power (although the aircraft climbs quickly out of earshot)

Question 510 marks · Noise pollution · spec 3.4.3.2.13 · Genn pp. 293-295, 418 · NOISE-05

A motor-racing circuit has residents living 500 m away. The circuit operator wants to compare two designs of acoustic barrier: an earth baffle mound and a timber acoustic fence.

[5 marks]05.1Describe how you would carry out an investigation to compare the effectiveness of the two barriers.
[1 mark]05.2Explain why readings should be taken with the same wind direction.
[2 marks]05.3Explain the difference between the LA10 and LA90 measures of traffic noise.
[2 marks]05.4Outline two ways in which the residents' houses could be designed to reduce the noise reaching occupants.
Show mark scheme
05.1 5 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Use a calibrated sound meter (dB(A)) at fixed distances (eg 10, 50, 100, 250, 500 m) from the track behind each barrier and at a control site with no barrier
  • 1Standardise the noise source: same cars / same race or a standard test noise; same time of day, wind speed / direction, weather; same height of meter above ground
  • 1Take repeated readings at each distance and calculate means (and standard deviation) to assess variability
  • 1Use a measure such as LA10 or Leq over a set period rather than a single peak reading
  • 1Compare reduction in dB relative to the control at each distance; plot noise level against distance for each barrier; statistical test (t-test) to assess significance
05.2 1 mark
1 mark.
  • 1Wind carries sound / affects how far it travels so different wind directions would change readings independently of the barrier
05.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1LA10 is the noise level exceeded for 10% of the period - a measure of the noisiest periods
  • 1LA90 is the level exceeded for 90% of the period - a measure of the quietest / background level
05.4 2 marks
Any 2 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 2.
  • 1Double / triple glazing
  • 1Acoustic insulation in walls / roof
  • 1Bedrooms on the side facing away from the track
  • 1Dense hedges / trees and earth mounds in gardens

Question 610 marks · Noise pollution · spec 3.4.3.2.13 · Genn pp. 286-295 · NOISE-06
[9 marks]06.1Using named examples, explain how the sources of noise pollution can be controlled by changes to design, operation and location.
[1 mark]06.2State what is meant by the Leq 57 dB contour used around UK airports.

Indicative content - credit any of the following (and other relevant, accurate points):
  • Principles: dB is logarithmic (10 dB = 10x; 3 dB = 2x) so small reductions in dB are large reductions in intensity; effects on humans (hearing, stress, behaviour) and wildlife (breeding birds, bats, cetaceans)
  • Aircraft design: high bypass ratio engines, chevron nozzles, hush kits, acoustic liners, fairings, composites, blended wing
  • Aircraft operation: take-off angle, flight paths avoiding urban areas, constant descent, reduced reverse thrust, night restrictions, Chapter noise limits, fines and noise charges, quota count
  • Airport design / location: away from population centres, taxi and engine-test areas away from housing, acoustic walls, baffle mounds, land-use restrictions, free acoustic insulation
  • Railway: track polishing, sound-absorbing ballast and suspension, pantograph fairings, wheel lubrication, composite brakes
  • Road: porous / rubber asphalt, acoustic fences, embankments, tree planting, aerodynamics, engine insulation, traffic management and routing, double glazing
  • Industrial: silencers on compressors, pressing instead of stamping, nylon rollers, drilling instead of pile-driving, bubble curtains, timing of blasting, acoustic mats / curtains, remote operation, ear protection
  • Domestic: acoustic absorbers in appliances, volume limiters, selection of quieter appliances, ear defenders
  • Evaluation: which approaches are most effective / cost-effective; conflicts (airports near cities for convenience); legislation and monitoring (Leq 57 dB contours) needed to enforce
Model answers: what a Level 3 answer looks like
Model answer · Level 3 (9 marks)

Because the decibel scale is logarithmic, a 10 dB cut is a tenfold reduction in intensity and 3 dB halves it, so modest-sounding design changes matter. Aircraft engines have been made quieter by high-bypass-ratio turbofans, chevron nozzles, hush kits and acoustic liners, with fairings and composite airframes reducing airframe noise. Operation can be changed too: steeper take-off angles and flight paths that avoid housing, constant-descent approaches, restricted reverse thrust, night curfews, Chapter noise limits enforced by fines and quota counts. Airport location and layout - away from population centres, engine-test areas and taxiways kept from housing, acoustic walls and baffle mounds, land-use planning and free insulation for residents - deal with the noise that remains.

Railways use track polishing, sound-absorbing ballast and suspension, pantograph fairings, wheel lubrication on curves and composite brakes. Roads use porous or rubber-crumb asphalt, acoustic fences and embankments, tree planting, engine insulation and aerodynamics, traffic routing and double glazing. Industry fits silencers to compressors, presses rather than stamps, uses nylon rollers, drills instead of pile-driving, surrounds piling with bubble curtains to protect cetaceans, times blasting, hangs acoustic mats and operates remotely, with ear protection as a last line. Domestic appliances use acoustic absorbers and volume limiters.

Design changes at source are the most effective because they reduce noise for everyone; operational limits depend on enforcement; location works only for new developments, and airports are still sited near cities for convenience. Legislation and monitoring of noise contours (57 dB Leq) are what turn these options into actual reductions.

Weaker answer · Level 2 (5 marks)

Noise pollution can be reduced in different ways. Aircraft can be designed with quieter engines and can fly at night less often. Airports can be built away from cities. Roads can have sound barriers next to them and quieter road surfaces. Trains can have their tracks polished and use better brakes. Factories can use quieter machines and workers can wear ear defenders. Noise can also be reduced by planting trees. These methods reduce the noise that reaches people.

What makes the difference
  • Level 2 gives correct but generic examples ('quieter engines'); Level 3 names the design features (high bypass ratio, chevron nozzles, acoustic liners) the question's 'named examples' requires.
  • Level 3 organises by design / operation / location as the question asks; Level 2 mixes them.
  • The decibel-scale point and the closing evaluation of which approaches work best lift Level 3 into the top band.
06.2 1 mark
1 mark.
  • 1The area within which the average (equivalent continuous) sound level between 07:00 and 23:00 in summer exceeds 57 dB - taken as the onset of significant community annoyance
