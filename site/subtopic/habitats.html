01.14 marks1 mark each

    • deep fertile soils built by leaf litter and root binding
    • strong seasonality

  2. Deciduous canopy present spring to autumn shades the floor during the best growing temperatures

  3. Bluebells, snowdrops, lesser celandine grow and flower early in spring before the trees leaf

Model answers: what a Level 3 answer looks like
Model answer · Level 3 (8 marks)

Protected areas are the core of rainforest conservation. Korup National Park in Cameroon protects 1260 km2 with about 600 tree species, including the endemic Ancistrocladus korupensis whose alkaloids have anti-HIV and antimalarial activity; Brazil's Tumucumaque National Park covers 39 000 km2 and adjoins a reserve in French Guiana; the Rio Bravo area in Belize is funded by charities, companies, ecotourism and selective logging of high-value mahogany that does not damage the forest long term. Debt-for-nature swaps have cancelled Guatemalan and Brazilian debt in return for protection, CITES listing restricts trade in Honduras mahogany, the ITTO sets sustainable timber aims, ecotourism and community employment give local people an income from standing forest, and carbon sequestration payments are emerging.

Where they are enforced, protected areas work, but they face illegal logging, mining, poaching, road building and conflict, and ecotourism itself has damaged sites in Costa Rica and Malaysia. The deeper problem is that the drivers of deforestation - population growth and international demand for beef, soya, palm oil, timber and minerals - originate outside the forest, and climate change threatens even well-protected areas; the ITTO's aims are largely unmet and overall loss continues.

The most effective schemes are those that combine large protected areas with economic alternatives for local people, such as Rio Bravo, but they can only slow the loss unless demand in importing countries also falls.

Weaker answer · Level 2 (4 marks)

Rainforests are protected by national parks where logging is banned, for example in Brazil. Debt-for-nature swaps mean a country's debt is cancelled if it protects its forest. Ecotourism brings money from tourists so the forest is worth more standing than cut down. Sustainable logging only takes some trees. These efforts have saved some areas but deforestation is still happening because of illegal logging and farming, so they have only been partly effective.

What makes the difference
  • Level 2 covers the main types of effort and a fair conclusion, but the question says 'describe' - Level 3 gives named examples with figures (Korup, Tumucumaque, Rio Bravo) as the mark scheme expects.
  • Level 3 explains why effectiveness is limited (drivers originate outside the forest, ITTO aims unmet, climate change) rather than just noting continued loss.
  • The judgement in Level 3 identifies what works best and what would be needed; Level 2 stops at 'partly effective'.

Model answers: what a Level 3 answer looks like
Model answer · Level 3 (9 marks)

Tropical coral reefs cover about one per cent of the oceans yet support over a quarter of marine species, a diversity built on the symbiosis between polyps and zooxanthellae that recycles nutrients in nutrient-poor water. They matter to people for fisheries of fish, lobsters and conch; for medicines such as AZT, Ara-C, dolastatin, secosteroids, bryostatin and trabectedin discovered in reef organisms; as a carbon sink, since every carbonate unit in coral skeleton absorbs a molecule of carbon dioxide; for dissipating wave energy that would erode low-lying islands; and for tourism income.

They are also unusually vulnerable because they need narrow abiotic conditions. Anchors, divers, litter and coral extraction kill polyps; ornaments are collected; sediment from rivers and coastal development smothers them; oil, sewage and nutrients cause algal shading; ocean acidification hinders skeleton formation; warming causes bleaching; overfishing of giant tritons lets crown-of-thorns starfish destroy reefs; introduced lionfish eat native fish; and loss of mangroves and seagrass removes the nurseries and sediment traps reefs depend on.

Marine protected areas such as the Great Barrier Reef, Belize and Chagos combine mooring buoys, diver education, no-take zones that raise catches outside them, sustainable tourism and protection of linked habitats, while fishing controls set closed seasons, minimum and maximum sizes and ban dynamite, trawling and small mesh. These work well against local threats where they are enforced, and no-take zones have measurable benefits, but they cannot touch warming and acidification, which need international action, enforcement in remote areas is weak, and restrictions conflict with local livelihoods. Local conservation buys time; only global climate action can secure the reefs.

Weaker answer · Level 1 (3 marks)

Coral reefs are important because lots of fish live there and people catch them for food. Tourists visit reefs to dive and this brings money. Reefs also protect the coast from waves. Reefs are threatened by climate change which causes bleaching, and by pollution and damage from boats. They are conserved by making marine protected areas where fishing is banned. This helps but climate change is still a problem.

What makes the difference
  • Level 1 has the right headings but Level 3 explains each (why reefs are diverse, why they are a carbon sink, how each threat acts).
  • Level 3 uses named reserves and specific management measures; Level 1 gives one generic measure.
  • Level 3's evaluation separates local from global threats and reaches a nuanced judgement; Level 1 states that climate change is 'still a problem'.
