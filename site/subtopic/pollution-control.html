Question 110 marks · Principles of pollution control (CPA, CGM, new technologies) · spec 3.4.3.1 · Genn pp. 243, 303-304 · PCON-01

A nuclear reprocessing plant on the coast discharges low-level liquid radioactive waste into the sea through a pipeline.

[2 marks]01.1Explain what is meant by Critical Pathway Analysis (CPA).
[3 marks]01.2Give three factors that would be considered in a CPA for this discharge.
[3 marks]01.3Describe how Critical Group Monitoring (CGM) would be used to assess the risk to the public from this discharge.
[2 marks]01.4Suggest two types of environmental sample that would be collected to check the CPA predictions.
Show mark scheme
01.1 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Predicting the route / movement a pollutant will take through the environment (based on its properties and features of the environment)
  • 1To assess where it will become concentrated / which locations and organisms are most at risk, so monitoring can focus on those sites
01.2 3 marks
Any 3 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 3.
  • 1Sea current velocity and direction
  • 1Properties of the radionuclides: half-life, solubility, tendency to adsorb onto sediments / bioaccumulate
  • 1Food chain pathways, eg uptake by seaweed, shellfish, fish eaten by people
  • 1Geology / hydrology of the coast; sediment deposition areas (mud flats)
  • 1Human activities in the area: fishing, seaweed collection, beach use
01.3 3 marks
Any 3 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 3.
  • 1Identify the group of people whose lifestyle gives them the highest exposure (eg people who eat a lot of locally caught shellfish / fish, live near the mud flats, collect seaweed)
  • 1Monitor their exposure / the concentration of radioactive material in the foods they eat or their body
  • 1If their exposure / risk is acceptably low, everyone else is assumed to be at even lower risk; if not, emissions are controlled
  • 1CGM assesses exposure before health effects occur (not used to detect damage)
01.4 2 marks
Any 2 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 2.
  • 1Seawater
  • 1Sediment / mud
  • 1Seaweed
  • 1Shellfish / molluscs / fish
  • 1Milk, vegetables or meat from nearby farms
  • 1Atmospheric dust
  • 1Soil

Question 210 marks · Principles of pollution control (CPA, CGM, new technologies) · spec 3.4.3.1 · Genn pp. 243-245 · PCON-02
[6 marks]02.1Explain how the control of emission location and emission timing can reduce the severity of pollution. Use examples.
[2 marks]02.2Explain the polluter pays principle and suggest why it encourages pollution prevention.
[2 marks]02.3Explain the precautionary principle as applied to the release of a new chemical.
Show mark scheme
02.1 6 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Location: discharge into the sea / large water bodies where currents dilute and disperse the pollutant
  • 1Location: chimneys / emissions sited downwind of urban areas so pollutants are not carried over populations; avoiding valleys and enclosed water bodies where pollutants concentrate
  • 1Location: not discharging wastes onto permeable rock above an aquifer / not siting landfill on aquifers
  • 1Timing: restrict polluting activities (industrial combustion, vehicle use) during temperature inversions when dispersal is poor
  • 1Timing: discharge liquid effluent when river flow is high / on an ebb tide so it is diluted and carried away
  • 1Timing: pesticide spraying at night / on still days; noisy operations restricted to daytime; night-flight restrictions
02.2 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1The organisation / person that causes the pollution is responsible for (paying for) the damage caused
  • 1If the cost of preventing the pollution is less than the cost of the damage / fines, there is a financial incentive to prevent it; works best when penalties are high and escape is unlikely
02.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1It is assumed that the waste will cause pollution / harm until research shows it is unlikely to
  • 1So release is not permitted until it has been tested; lack of knowledge of a problem is not an excuse / does not reduce responsibility
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 243-245; AQA spec 3.4.3.1.
Question 310 marks · Principles of pollution control (CPA, CGM, new technologies) · spec 3.4.3.1 · Genn pp. 244-245 · PCON-03

Figure 1 shows the relationship between the cost of pollution control equipment at a factory and the percentage of a pollutant emission that is prevented.

Figure 1
[2 marks]03.1Describe the relationship shown in Figure 1.
[1 mark]03.2Use Figure 1 to calculate the extra cost of increasing the pollution prevented from 90% to 99%.
[4 marks]03.3Explain what is meant by the terms ALARA and BATNEEC and how they would be applied by the factory.
[3 marks]03.4Suggest why the control methods used originally by a factory may no longer be sufficient after several years.
Show mark scheme
03.1 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Cost increases as the percentage prevented increases
  • 1The relationship is not linear: cost rises slowly at first then very steeply / exponentially above about 80-90%
03.2 1 mark
1 mark for the correct answer.
  • 116.0 - 5.0 = 11.0 (£ million)
03.3 4 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1ALARA: emissions should be As Low As Reasonably Achievable
  • 1It is rarely practical / affordable to reduce emissions to zero; low-level emissions may have acceptable impacts so an acceptable emission level is set
  • 1BATNEEC: Best Available Technology Not Entailing Excessive Cost - choosing the most effective control equipment whose cost is not excessive
  • 1The factory would install equipment that gives a high level of control (eg 80-90%) rather than paying £16 million for 99%
03.4 3 marks
Any 3 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 3.
  • 1Industry / city may have expanded so there are more pollutant sources and total emissions have risen
  • 1Better understanding of the health / environmental effects so stricter standards are set
  • 1Improved technology becomes available (BATNEEC changes) / cost of better control falls
  • 1Pollutant may have accumulated (persistent) so background levels are higher

Question 410 marks · Principles of pollution control (CPA, CGM, new technologies) · spec 3.4.3.1, 3.4.3.3 · Genn pp. 244, 303-304 · PCON-04

Table 1 shows four general approaches to pollution control.

[4 marks]04.1Complete Table 1 by giving one example of each approach.
Table 1
ApproachExample
Production prevention
Prevention of release
Post-release remediation
Alternative processes
[2 marks]04.2Explain why prevention of production is generally preferable to post-release remediation.
[4 marks]04.3Describe how each of the following new technologies helps to control pollution.
Show mark scheme
04.1 4 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Production prevention: desulfurisation of fuels before combustion; low-temperature (fluidised bed) combustion to prevent NOx; smokeless fuels
  • 1Prevention of release: electrostatic precipitators / bag filters / cyclones for smoke; catalytic converters; flue gas desulfurisation; bund walls; landfill liners
  • 1Post-release remediation: oil spill clean-up (booms, skimmers, dispersants, bioremediation); phytoremediation of metal-contaminated land; liming of acidified lakes
  • 1Alternative processes: electric vehicles instead of petrol / diesel; renewable energy instead of fossil fuels; pyrethroids instead of organochlorines; fuel cells instead of internal combustion engines
04.2 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Remediation is often incomplete / cannot recover all the pollutant (eg oil spills, dispersed pollutants), so some damage still occurs
  • 1Prevention avoids the damage entirely and is usually cheaper than clean-up / avoids polluter-pays costs; moving from post-production treatment to non-release is a circular economy principle
04.3 4 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Satellites detect and track the extent / movement of oil slicks over large areas, quickly and repeatedly, so clean-up can be directed and polluters identified
  • 1GPS tracking monitors ship positions so navigation errors / collisions / groundings are reduced and offshore shipping routes are enforced
  • 1Polymers with a high surface area adsorb metal ions from contaminated water / effluent so they can be removed and recovered
  • 1Plants that absorb heavy metals (hyperaccumulators) are grown on contaminated land, harvested and disposed of / metals recovered, cleaning the soil
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 244, 303-304; AQA spec 3.4.3.1, 3.4.3.3.

Indicative content - credit any of the following (and other relevant, accurate points):
  • Critical Pathway Analysis: predicting movement of mobile persistent pollutants (state of effluent, wind, currents, geology, pH / oxygen and solubility, bioaccumulation, food sources, half-lives) so monitoring focuses on sites at risk; mainly radioactive discharges; checked by sampling
  • Critical Group Monitoring: exposure of the most-exposed group monitored before health effects; if their risk is acceptable everyone else is safer; emissions reduced if not
  • Emission control strategies: location (marine discharges, downwind of cities, not over aquifers) and timing (avoiding temperature inversions, low river flow)
  • Principles: polluter pays (incentive depends on penalties and enforcement), precautionary principle (assume harm until shown otherwise; ignorance no excuse), ALARA / BATNEEC and the non-linear cost-efficiency relationship
  • Selection of methods: production prevention (desulfurisation), prevention of release (precipitators, catalytic converters), post-release remediation (oil spill clean-up, phytoremediation), alternative processes (electric vehicles, renewables, pyrethroids)
  • Evaluation: prevention usually cheaper and more effective than remediation; costs rise steeply near 100%; CPA relies on understanding of pathways; enforcement and international agreement needed for transboundary pollutants; judgement about acceptable risk is political as well as scientific
  • Justified conclusion
