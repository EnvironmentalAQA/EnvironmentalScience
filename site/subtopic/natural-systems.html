Question 110 marks · Energy & material cycles: natural vs human systems · spec 3.6.2 · Genn pp. 373 · SUS-03

Table 1 compares nitrogen fixation by Rhizobium bacteria in legume root nodules with the Haber process.

[4 marks]01.1Use Table 1 to explain how natural processes differ from human industrial processes in their use of energy.
[3 marks]01.2Give three other natural processes driven by solar energy that human society depends on.
[3 marks]01.3Explain how the principle of low-temperature natural processes is being applied in human systems.
Show mark scheme
01.1 4 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Natural processes are driven by renewable, low energy-density solar energy captured by photosynthesis; human processes usually use high energy-density non-renewable fossil fuels
  • 1Natural processes occur at low temperatures because enzymes lower the activation energy of reactions; human processes need high temperatures and pressures
  • 1Natural processes produce no toxic wastes / their products are raw materials for other processes; industrial processes emit CO2 and NOx
  • 1So natural fixation is sustainable indefinitely with a low carbon footprint
01.2 3 marks
Any 3 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 3.
  • 1Hydrological cycle (evaporation, precipitation)
  • 1Carbon cycle / photosynthesis
  • 1Atmospheric circulation / winds
  • 1Thermohaline circulation (density differences from heating / evaporation)
  • 1Decomposition
01.3 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Bioleaching and phytomining extract metals at low temperature instead of high-temperature smelting
  • 1Anaerobic digestion and composting treat wastes; enzymes in industry (biological washing powders, biofuels from algae / microorganisms)
  • 1Heat pumps and solar thermal capture low-density heat; low-temperature manufacturing using biological polymers; fuel cells instead of combustion

Question 210 marks · Energy & material cycles: natural vs human systems · spec 3.6.3 · Genn pp. 373-374 · SUS-04
[4 marks]02.1Explain how natural material cycles differ from linear human systems of material use.
[2 marks]02.2Explain why the use of toxic metals in electronic products is a particular problem for sustainability.
[4 marks]02.3Describe how the design of a product can move it from a linear to a cyclical system.
Show mark scheme
02.1 4 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Natural cycles use a small number of abundant elements (C, H, O, N, P) built into monomers and polymers (carbohydrates, proteins); wastes of one process are raw materials for another so nothing is depleted or accumulates
  • 1Natural wastes are biodegradable / non-toxic and are broken down by decomposers into raw materials
  • 1Human systems are linear: extract - process - manufacture - use - discard; minerals are dispersed after use or mixed so recovery is difficult
  • 1Human wastes are often persistent and toxic (organochlorines, PCBs, heavy metals in electronics) and build up; fossil fuel use is inherently linear and unsustainable
02.2 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Metals are dispersed in small amounts within complex mixed products so they cannot easily be separated / recovered at end of life
  • 1They leach from landfill or are released by incineration; persistent, bioaccumulating
02.3 4 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Design for durability, repair and re-use (replaceable parts, no built-in batteries, spare parts and support, no planned obsolescence)
  • 1Easy identification of materials (coding) and separation of components so they can be recycled
  • 1Separation of technical materials (metals, plastics) from biological materials (which can be composted / digested)
  • 1Use of recycled and recyclable / biodegradable materials; reduced packaging and material use (Cradle to Cradle)
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 373-374; AQA spec 3.6.3.

[2 marks]04.2Explain why natural systems can be sustained using a relatively small number of elements.
[2 marks]04.3Explain why the human use of minerals makes them difficult to recover and re-use.
[2 marks]04.4Explain why toxic metals in electronic products cause problems that the wastes of natural systems do not.
Show mark scheme
04.1 4 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Energy: low energy-density renewable (solar) / non-renewable fossil fuels
  • 1Materials: cyclic - wastes of one process are raw materials of the next / linear - used once then discarded, depletion
  • 1Wastes: biodegradable, non-toxic or do not build up / toxic, persistent (eg PCBs, organochlorines, toxic metals) that bioaccumulate
  • 1Temperature: low / high (Haber process, incineration)
04.2 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Abundant simple raw materials build into monomers and then a wide range of polymers (carbohydrates, proteins)
  • 1They are re-used in cycles so there is no depletion of raw materials or build-up of wastes
04.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Use often disperses the minerals (eg in products spread over the world / corrosion)
  • 1Or produces mixtures of materials that are difficult to separate, so sustainable exploitation is reduced
04.4 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1They cannot easily be removed from end-of-life appliances and are not biodegradable
  • 1Natural wastes are non-toxic / broken down to raw materials for other processes, whereas persistent toxins may bioaccumulate and biomagnify

Question 55 marks · Energy & material cycles: natural vs human systems · spec 3.6.2-3.6.3 · Genn pp. 373 · SUS-15
[1 mark]05.1Which of the following is a feature of natural systems? Tick one box.
  • A  Reactions that require high temperatures
  • B  Reliance on non-renewable energy
  • C  Waste products that are the raw materials for other processes
  • D  Dispersal of elements so they cannot be re-used
[2 marks]05.2Explain why nitrogen fixation by bacteria is more sustainable than the Haber process.
[2 marks]05.3Explain why incineration of wastes is less sustainable than natural decomposition.
Show mark scheme
05.1 1 mark
Correct answer only.
  • 1C  Waste products that are the raw materials for other processes
05.2 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Bacteria fix nitrogen at low temperature using enzymes and energy ultimately from sunlight (renewable)
  • 1The Haber process needs high temperature and pressure powered by non-renewable fossil fuels, releasing CO2
05.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Incineration uses high temperatures / fuel and releases pollutants; nutrients are not returned as usable raw materials
  • 1Decomposition at low temperature recycles nutrients as the raw materials for other natural processes (material cycle)
