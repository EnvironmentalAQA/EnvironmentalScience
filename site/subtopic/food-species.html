Question 110 marks · Manipulation of food species & agricultural energetics · spec 3.5.1.2 · Genn pp. 321-323 · FS-01
[6 marks]01.1Compare selective breeding, asexual reproduction and cross-breeding as methods of improving livestock or crops.
[4 marks]01.2Explain how artificial insemination and embryo transfer increase the rate at which desirable characteristics spread through a livestock population.
Show mark scheme
01.1 (6 marks)
  • Selective breeding: parents chosen for desirable genetic characteristics (Limousin muscle growth, Merino wool, Cheviot hardiness); combines genes but characteristics of offspring not fully predictable; many generations needed; risk of inbreeding
  • Asexual reproduction (cuttings, runners, tissue culture, cloning): offspring genetically identical to the parent so characteristics are predictable and survival high; but no variation so no improvement and vulnerability to the same disease; cloning of animals (nucleus transfer into an enucleated egg, surrogate) still developing
  • Cross-breeding: two breeds combined to give a mix of characteristics with hybrid vigour (heterosis) and lower inbreeding risk, eg Zebu (heat tolerant) x Ayrshire (high milk yield)
01.2 (4 marks)
  • AI: semen from a male with desirable characteristics can fertilise many more females than natural mating; frozen semen stored and transported easily and used after the male dies
  • Embryo transfer: FSH stimulates a superior female to release many eggs, which are fertilised (IVF) with sperm from a superior male
  • Each embryo is implanted in a different surrogate female
  • So the donor produces many offspring per year rather than one per pregnancy
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 321-323; AQA spec 3.5.1.2.
Question 210 marks · Manipulation of food species & agricultural energetics · spec 3.5.1.2 · Genn pp. 323-324 · FS-02

Table 1 describes three genetically modified (GM) crops.

Table 1
GM cropGene sourceCharacteristic
Bt maize / cottonBacillus thuringiensis bacteriumProduces a toxin that kills insect pests
Roundup Ready soyaAgrobacteriumResistant to the herbicide glyphosate
Golden riceDaffodil and Erwinia bacteriumProduces vitamin A in the grain
[2 marks]02.1Explain the main advantage of GM over conventional selective breeding.
[3 marks]02.2For each crop in Table 1, explain one potential benefit.
[4 marks]02.3Explain four concerns about GM crops.
[1 mark]02.4Give one reason why GM crops have been slow to enter cultivation in Europe.
Show mark scheme
02.1 (2 marks)
  • Single genes can be transferred from any species, not just closely related ones that produce fertile hybrids
  • Desired characteristics introduced without linked unwanted ones; faster
02.2 (3 marks)
  • Bt: reduces insecticide spraying so fewer non-target deaths, lower cost / energy
  • Roundup Ready: weeds controlled by spraying glyphosate without harming the crop, so higher yields with less cultivation / tillage
  • Golden rice: provides vitamin A in the staple diet, reducing about 500 000 cases of blindness a year in India / sub-Saharan Africa
02.3 (4 marks)
  • Gene transfer by pollen to conventional / organic crops or wild relatives (eg Bt gene killing insects feeding on wild plants)
  • Antibiotic-resistance marker genes could transfer to pathogenic bacteria; possible allergies; gene transfer to gut bacteria
  • Detection in the human food chain from crops approved only for animal feed
  • Economic: seeds must be bought each year from patent-holding companies, raising costs and reducing local crop diversity and seed saving in LEDCs; herbicide-resistant crops may increase herbicide use
02.4 (1 marks)
  • Public / political concern that risks are not fully assessed - more evidence needed (precautionary principle)

Question 310 marks · Manipulation of food species & agricultural energetics · spec 3.5.1.2 · Genn pp. 324-327 · FS-03

Table 1 shows the energy inputs and food energy outputs of three farming systems.

Table 1
SystemEnergy input / GJ ha-1 yr-1Food energy output / GJ ha-1 yr-1
Extensive cereal farming (low inputs)420
Intensive cereal farming (fertiliser, pesticide, machinery)2060
Intensive beef (grain-fed)404
[2 marks]03.1Calculate the energy ratio (output / input) for each system.
[3 marks]03.2Use your answers to explain the difference between productivity and efficiency.
[3 marks]03.3Explain what is meant by an energy subsidy and give three examples.
[2 marks]03.4Explain why spreading the same total inputs over a larger area would increase total food production.
Show mark scheme
03.1 (2 marks)
  • Extensive cereal 5.0; intensive cereal 3.0
  • Intensive beef 0.1
03.2 (3 marks)
  • Intensive cereal is most productive per hectare (60 GJ) but less efficient (3.0) than extensive (5.0)
  • Each extra unit of input gives a smaller extra yield (law of diminishing returns)
  • Beef has very low productivity and efficiency because energy is lost at an extra trophic level
03.3 (3 marks)
  • Any input that increases productivity but requires the use of energy (usually fossil fuel)
  • Manufacture of nitrate fertiliser (Haber process) and pesticides; fuel for machinery; pumping irrigation water; grain drying; food processing and transport; embodied energy of machinery
03.4 (2 marks)
  • Because of diminishing returns, the first units of input on unfertilised land give the largest yield increases
  • Moving inputs from intensively farmed land (where extra input adds little) to extensively farmed land gives a greater total yield - though land, cost and distribution limit this

Question 410 marks · Manipulation of food species & agricultural energetics · spec 3.5.1.2 · Genn pp. 326-328 · FS-04

Table 1 shows food conversion ratios (FCR) for livestock: the mass of feed needed to produce 1 kg of live mass gain.

Table 1
LivestockFCR / kg feed per kg gain
Farmed salmon1.2
Chicken1.8
Pork3.0
Beef (feedlot)7.0
Sheep8.0
[3 marks]04.1Use Table 1 to explain why intensive poultry and fish production convert feed to food more efficiently than beef.
[3 marks]04.2Explain three ways farmers reduce food chain energy losses in livestock production.
[4 marks]04.3Explain why growing crops for direct human consumption usually produces more food per hectare than rearing livestock, and describe one situation where livestock is the better option.
Show mark scheme
04.1 (3 marks)
  • Chicken and salmon need far less feed per kg gained (1.2-1.8 vs 7.0)
  • Fish are ectotherms so no energy is used for thermoregulation and water supports their mass; poultry grow fast and are kept warm indoors with movement restricted
  • Cattle are large endotherms, use energy in movement, and lose energy in methane from cellulose digestion
04.2 (3 marks)
  • Control of movement (housing / restricted grazing) so less energy is used in muscle activity
  • Temperature control (heated / insulated buildings) so less energy is used in maintaining body temperature
  • Species selection: choose species with low FCRs (poultry, fish); high-energy easily digested diets; slaughter at the fastest growth stage
04.3 (4 marks)
  • Only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level is passed to the next; most is lost as heat in respiration / metabolism or in undigested material
  • Humans eating plants (primary consumers) receive more of the energy captured by photosynthesis than eating animals fed on those plants (secondary consumers)
  • Livestock better where land cannot grow crops (upland, semi-arid, steep, thin soils): grazing animals with symbiotic gut bacteria digest cellulose in grass that humans cannot
  • Omnivores (pigs) convert food wastes (dairy by-products, catering waste) into meat
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 326-328; AQA spec 3.5.1.2.
Question 55 marks · Manipulation of food species & agricultural energetics · spec 3.5.1.2 · Genn pp. 321-322 · FS-05
[1 mark]05.1State what is meant by hybrid vigour.
[2 marks]05.2Explain how cross-breeding Zebu and Ayrshire cattle produced a breed suited to hot climates.
[2 marks]05.3Give two potential applications of cloning to livestock farming.
Show mark scheme
05.1 (1 marks)
  • Cross-bred offspring are healthier / more productive because the problems caused by homozygous recessive genes in inbred varieties are reduced (heterosis)
05.2 (2 marks)
  • Zebu tolerate heat but have a low milk yield; Ayrshire have a high milk yield but are not heat tolerant
  • Cross-breeding combined the desirable characteristics: heat tolerance with a high milk yield
05.3 (2 marks)
Any 2 from:
  • Replacement of valuable animals that die with genetically identical individuals
  • Replacement of herds culled during a disease outbreak
  • Production of large numbers of individuals with desirable characteristics

Question 610 marks · Manipulation of food species & agricultural energetics · spec 3.5.1.2 · Genn pp. 326-328 · FS-06

Table 1 shows typical energy values for an agroecosystem. Values are units of energy remaining from 100 000 units of solar energy.

Table 1
StageEnergy / arbitrary units
Solar energy reaching the crop100 000
Energy captured during photosynthesis4 000
Energy stored in crop biomass1 000
[1 mark]06.1Calculate the percentage of the solar energy reaching the crop that is stored in crop biomass.
[3 marks]06.2Explain why so little of the solar energy is stored in the crop.
[2 marks]06.3Explain why few food chains have more than four trophic levels.
[2 marks]06.4Explain why rearing sheep or cattle is the best option for food production in upland and semi-arid areas.
[2 marks]06.5Explain how pigs can increase the efficiency of a food production system.
Show mark scheme
06.1 (1 marks)
  • 1000 / 100 000 x 100 = 1%
06.2 (3 marks)
  • Some light is reflected by the leaves
  • Some light is transmitted (passes through the leaves without being absorbed) or is of wavelengths not absorbed
  • Much of the energy captured is used in the plant's metabolic processes and released as low energy-density heat (respiration)
06.3 (2 marks)
  • Less energy is passed to each trophic level than was received from the previous one (heat losses in respiration)
  • After four levels too little energy remains to support a further population
06.4 (2 marks)
  • Permanent grassland is a more sustainable land use than arable crops in these areas (soil / rainfall unsuitable for crops)
  • Grazing animals can digest grass cellulose using symbiotic bacteria in their stomachs, producing food that humans can eat
06.5 (2 marks)
  • Pigs are omnivores that can eat food wastes not wanted by people (eg cheese-making wastes, catering leftovers)
  • So edible meat is produced from material that would otherwise be wasted, without using land / crops that could feed people
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 326-328; AQA spec 3.5.1.2.

[2 marks]07.1Describe the relationship shown in Figure 1.
[2 marks]07.2Calculate the extra yield produced by the second unit of input and by the fifth unit of input.
[2 marks]07.3Explain why, if enough land is available, spreading inputs evenly over a large area gives a greater total yield than concentrating them on a small area.
[2 marks]07.4Suggest why inputs are nevertheless often used unevenly across the world.
[2 marks]07.5Explain why intensive agriculture may be very productive but not very efficient.
Show mark scheme
07.1 (2 marks)
  • Yield increases as inputs increase
  • But each extra unit of input produces a smaller increase in yield than the previous one (the curve flattens / law of diminishing returns)
07.2 (2 marks)
  • Second unit: 70 - 50 = 20 units
  • Fifth unit: 94 - 90 = 4 units
07.3 (2 marks)
  • The first units of input on each area give the largest yield increases
  • Concentrating inputs means later units add very little, whereas the same inputs spread thinly all work at the steep part of the curve
07.4 (2 marks)
  • Some areas have much greater potential productivity so investment there is sensible
  • Affluent societies can afford to buy more inputs while poorer communities that would benefit most cannot afford them (economic reasons)
07.5 (2 marks)
  • Yield per unit area is very high because of large inputs
  • But the extra yield per unit of input is small, so efficiency (yield per unit input / energy ratio) is low
