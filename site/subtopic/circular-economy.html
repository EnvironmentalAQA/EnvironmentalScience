[2 marks]01.2Explain what is meant by 'optimum production rather than maximum production', using a comparison between a natural ecosystem and an agroecosystem.
[2 marks]01.3Explain why international cooperation (eg through the EU or UN) is needed to implement circular economy initiatives.

  • 1Energy from renewable sources
  • 1Human activities should support ecosystems / conserve biodiversity
  • 1Separation of technical and biological materials; connected systems where the waste of one process feeds another (industrial symbiosis)
  • 1Diverse systems are more resistant to change

Question 210 marks · The circular economy, biocapacity & ecological footprints · spec 3.6.4.1 · Genn pp. 375-379 · SUS-06
[9 marks]02.1Evaluate the extent to which the principles of the circular economy can be applied to three of the following: water supplies, mineral supplies, waste management, pollution control, energy supplies, food production.
[1 mark]02.2State what is meant by industrial symbiosis.

Indicative content - credit any of the following (and other relevant, accurate points):
  • Water: catchment management, conservation, metering, greywater, re-use of treated effluent, aquifer recharge, sustainable urban drainage - largely cyclical already; limits in arid regions
  • Minerals: exploiting low-grade ores with low-temperature processes (bioleaching), extending product lifespans, repair / re-use / repurpose / recycle, design for disassembly, reduced packaging; limits: dispersal, alloys, energy of collection
  • Waste management: repair, re-use, refurbish, recycle, composting and anaerobic digestion returning nutrients, minimal landfill, energy from residual waste; EU targets on packaging, batteries, WEEE
  • Pollution control: shift from post-production treatment to non-release - fuel desulfurisation not FGD, fuel cells / electric vehicles not combustion, industrial symbiosis (Kalundborg: waste heat, gypsum, fly ash, yeast used by neighbours); limits for legacy pollution
  • Energy: renewables, storage, energy density technologies, low-temperature manufacturing, CHP; limits of intermittency, transport fuels
  • Food: natural processes for nutrients (legumes, organic matter), pest control (IPM), soil biota, agri-environment schemes, IMTA aquaculture; tension with maximum yield for a growing population
  • Land uses supporting ecosystems: urban green infrastructure, gardens, permeable surfaces, habitat in redeveloped docks / lagoons
  • Judgement: high applicability in some sectors (water, waste), partial in others (minerals, energy for transport); needs policy support
Model answers: what a Level 3 answer looks like
Model answer · Level 3 (9 marks)

Taking water, waste and pollution control: water supply is already close to circular, because the hydrological cycle returns what is used. Catchment management, conservation, metering, grey-water re-use, re-use of treated effluent, aquifer recharge and sustainable urban drainage close the loop further, with the main limit in arid regions where abstraction exceeds recharge. Waste management can apply the principles almost completely: repair, re-use and refurbishment extend product life, recycling returns materials, composting and anaerobic digestion return nutrients and yield biogas, residual waste supplies energy and landfill is minimised, driven by EU targets on packaging, batteries and WEEE; the limits are mixed materials that cannot be separated and the energy cost of collection.

Pollution control changes character under circular principles: instead of treating wastes after production - flue-gas desulfurisation, dispersants - the aim becomes non-release, through fuel desulfurisation, fuel cells and electric vehicles, and industrial symbiosis, as at Kalundborg where one plant's waste heat, gypsum, fly ash and yeast become neighbours' raw materials. Land use can support ecosystems with green infrastructure, gardens, permeable surfaces and habitat in redeveloped docks.

Applicability is high for water and waste because natural cycles already exist to copy; it is partial for pollution control because many processes still depend on combustion, and it needs policy support - targets, producer responsibility, planning - to overcome the economics of linear systems. The principles can be applied widely, but not evenly.

Weaker answer · Level 1 (3 marks)

The circular economy means reusing and recycling things instead of throwing them away. For water this means saving water and reusing grey water. For waste management it means recycling more and putting less in landfill. For pollution control it means not producing pollution in the first place. The circular economy can be applied to all of these to make them more sustainable.

What makes the difference
  • Level 1 gives one idea per area; Level 3 gives several specific measures for each and names the limit in each case.
  • Level 3 uses a case study (Kalundborg) and policy examples (EU targets) to show application, not just principle.
  • 'Evaluate the extent' - Level 3 compares applicability across the three areas and explains the differences; Level 1 says it applies to all.

03.1 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Ecological footprint: the area of the Earth's surface (global hectares) needed to provide the resources used by a person / population / activity and to absorb the wastes produced
  • 1Biocapacity: the biological productivity of an area (forests, cropland, grazing, fisheries, built land) - its ability to supply resources and absorb wastes, expressed in global hectares
03.2 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1A hectare of land / sea with the world-average biological productivity
  • 1Biocapacities of real areas vary widely so a standard unit allows footprints and biocapacities of different places to be compared
03.3 1 mark
1 mark for the correct answer.
  • 18.1 / 1.7 = 4.8 (accept 4.7-4.8)
03.4 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Higher HDI is associated with a larger footprint (USA 8.1 vs Bangladesh 0.8), though the UK achieves the same HDI as the USA with about half the footprint
  • 1Global footprint (2.8) exceeds global biocapacity (1.7) so humanity uses resources faster than they regenerate and CO2 accumulates (overshoot)
  • 1Earth Overshoot Day is the date each year when a year's worth of resources / waste absorption has been used up; it falls earlier each year (8 August in 2016)
03.5 2 marks

  • 1Reduced meat consumption / food waste; local seasonal food

Table 1
SourceCapacity / kW

[3 marks]05.2Describe three other ways in which the Eigg community has increased its sustainability.
[2 marks]05.3Explain why small self-contained communities often find it easier to adopt sustainable lifestyles than large cities.

Show mark scheme
05.1 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Diversity: HEP (wet winters), solar (summer) and wind peak at different times so supply is more reliable - diverse systems resist change
  • 1No single source could meet demand all year; battery storage on the island grid smooths fluctuations
  • 1Calm, dry, dull periods still leave gaps so diesel generators provide security (fossil fuel use minimised, not yet eliminated)
05.2 3 marks
Any 3 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 3.
  • 1Community ownership (Isle of Eigg Trust with Scottish Wildlife Trust) managing the island for people and wildlife
  • 1Wood biomass heating; insulation such as straw-bale housing; some electric vehicles
  • 1Habitat protection and creation: fencing rare plants from grazing, planting indigenous woodland (rowan, birch, willow); eagles and hen harriers nesting
  • 1Recyclables shipped off; garden / food waste composted; low-impact food production; beach litter collection
05.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Small groups can organise, agree and act quickly; decisions benefit people who see the results directly
  • 1Resources and wastes are local and visible so feedback is immediate, unlike cities supplied from far away
05.4 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Renewable energy is abundant but geographically and temporally variable; a continental grid moves electricity from areas of surplus (desert solar, offshore wind) to areas of demand
  • 1DC grids do not need every generator synchronised in phase as AC grids do, so coordination across many countries is easier
