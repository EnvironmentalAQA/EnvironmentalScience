Table 1 shows the textural composition of four soil samples.

Table 1

A85105
B404020
C202555
D108010
[3 marks]01.1Identify the sample that is a loam and explain why loams are ideal for cultivating most crops.
[3 marks]01.2Explain why sample A is likely to have the highest permeability but the lowest nutrient retention.
[2 marks]01.3Compare the thermal capacity and root penetration of soils A and C.
[2 marks]01.4Describe how the texture of a soil sample could be determined by sedimentation.
Show mark scheme
01.1 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1B (about 40:40:20 sand:silt:clay)
  • 1Balanced mix gives good drainage and aeration from the sand fraction
  • 1Water retention and nutrient (ion) retention from the clay fraction; easy root penetration and cultivation
01.2 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Large sand particles leave large pore spaces so water drains rapidly (high permeability / aeration)
  • 1Nutrient ions adsorb onto the (charged) surfaces of clay particles but not onto sand
  • 1With only 5% clay there is little surface to hold ions so nutrients are leached; low water retention too
01.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1C (clay) holds much water so has a high thermal capacity: warms and cools slowly; A warms rapidly after cold weather
  • 1Clay particles held together by capillary water so roots find it hard to push between them; penetration is easy in sandy A
01.4 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Shake a known mass of soil with water in a measuring cylinder and leave to settle; particles settle in order of size (sand first, then silt, then clay; organic matter floats)
  • 1Measure the depth / volume of each layer to calculate the percentage of each fraction, then use the soil triangle to name the textural class

Question 210 marks · Soils: fertility, erosion & management · spec 3.2.5.1 · Genn pp. 158-161 · SOIL-02
[6 marks]02.1Explain the roles of three named groups of soil organisms in maintaining soil fertility.
[2 marks]02.2Explain why a soil pH between 5.5 and 7.0 is best for fertility.
[2 marks]02.3Explain how soil compaction by heavy machinery reduces fertility.
Show mark scheme
02.1 6 marks
Up to 6 marks in total; award marks in proportion to the completeness of each point.
  • up to 2Detritivores (worms, beetle larvae, millipedes, woodlice) physically break up dead organic matter, increasing its surface area, and release nutrients; worms create tunnels improving drainage and aeration
  • up to 2Decomposers (bacteria, fungi) secrete enzymes that break down dead organic matter, releasing nutrient ions (ammonification) and forming humus which binds particles into crumb peds
  • up to 2Nitrogen-fixing bacteria convert nitrogen gas to ammonium ions; nitrifying bacteria oxidise ammonium to nitrite then nitrate for root absorption
  • up to 2Mycorrhizal fungi form symbiotic associations with roots increasing phosphate uptake
02.2 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1This is the range of tolerance of most plants and soil biota; acidic soils leach nutrients and mobilise toxic aluminium, damaging root membranes
  • 1In alkaline soils phosphates become insoluble / unavailable
02.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Interstitial spaces are reduced so aeration falls (aerobic processes such as nitrification and decomposition slow) and roots cannot penetrate
  • 1Infiltration falls so waterlogging is more likely and runoff / erosion increase
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 158-161; AQA spec 3.2.5.1.

[4 marks]03.2Explain four ways in which vegetation reduces the rate of soil erosion.
[2 marks]03.3Explain why ploughing up and down a slope causes more erosion than contour ploughing.
[2 marks]03.4Explain why the region's climate makes the soil vulnerable to both wind and water erosion.
Show mark scheme
03.1 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Soil loss increases as vegetation cover decreases: forest lowest (0.5), bare soil highest (60 t ha-1 yr-1) - over 100 times more
  • 1Ploughing downslope loses nearly three times as much as contour ploughing for the same crop
03.2 4 marks
Any 4 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 4.
  • 1Leaves / leaf litter intercept raindrops so soil particles are not dislodged by rain splash
  • 1Roots bind soil particles together
  • 1Vegetation acts as a windbreak reducing wind velocity and the kinetic energy available to carry particles
  • 1Plants increase infiltration (root channels, organic matter) so less surface runoff
  • 1Organic matter / humus from vegetation binds particles into stable peds
03.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Furrows running downslope act as channels / gulleys that increase the velocity (kinetic energy) of runoff
  • 1Furrows along the contour trap the water so it loses energy, deposits particles and infiltrates
03.4 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1In the dry season the soil (especially low-clay soils) is loose and dry with little cohesion so wind blows it away
  • 1In the wet season heavy rain causes rain splash and surface runoff when infiltration capacity is exceeded

Show mark scheme
04.1 4 marks
2 marks for each point (partial credit for a partly correct point).
  • 2Overgrazing: vegetation is eaten faster than it grows so soil is exposed; hooves disturb the soil, damage roots and compact it, reducing infiltration and increasing runoff
  • 2Reduced biota (ploughing, agrochemicals, less organic matter): less humus so less adhesion between particles; fewer worms so less aeration and lower infiltration capacity; fewer nutrients released so less vegetation cover

  • 1Sedimentation in reservoirs reduces their storage volume
  • 1Coastal sedimentation covering reefs / seagrass; turbidity reducing light for aquatic plants
  • 1Increased atmospheric particulates from wind erosion worsening asthma; dust covering crops / urban areas
  • 1Desertification as vegetation cannot re-establish; reduced rainfall
  • 1Nutrients (phosphates) carried on soil particles causing eutrophication

Question 610 marks · Soils: fertility, erosion & management · spec 3.2.5.3 · Genn pp. 164-166 · SOIL-06
[5 marks]06.1Complete Table 1 to explain how each method reduces soil erosion.
Table 1
MethodHow it reduces erosion
Terracing
Tied ridging
Windbreaks / hedgerows
Mulching

[2 marks]06.2Explain why long-term crops such as tea, coffee or orchards reduce erosion compared with annual arable crops.
[3 marks]06.3Describe three ways livestock can be managed to reduce soil erosion.
Show mark scheme
06.1 5 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Terracing: sloping land divided into narrow level fields held by retaining walls along contours; water slows as it crosses each terrace and infiltrates instead of running off
  • 1Tied ridging: on nearly flat land a criss-cross of intersecting ridges retains rainwater, increasing infiltration and preventing runoff
  • 1Windbreaks: hedgerows / tree rows reduce wind velocity (for up to 30 times their height) so the wind has less kinetic energy to carry particles; protect bare fields after harvest
  • 1Mulching: organic matter on the surface protects soil from raindrop impact and wind; decomposes to humus which binds particles; retains moisture
  • 1Strip cropping: strips of different crops harvested at different times (at 90° to the wind) so there is always a crop protecting part of the field; slows runoff
06.2 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1No frequent replanting / soil disturbance (ploughing) that breaks up soil structure and exposes particles
  • 1Permanent root systems and canopy cover protect the soil all year
06.3 3 marks
Any 3 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 3.
  • 1Control stocking density so vegetation regrows faster than it is eaten
  • 1Restrict access to vulnerable areas such as river banks (fencing)
  • 1Move / remove livestock at high-risk times, eg after heavy rain when soil is wet
  • 1Rotational grazing to rest pastures

Question 85 marks · Soils: fertility, erosion & management · spec 3.2.5 · Genn pp. 158-169 · SOIL-08
[1 mark]08.1Which soil property is most directly responsible for capillary rise of water from below? Tick one box.
  • A  High sand content
  • B  High clay content
  • C  Crumb peds
  • D  High pH
[3 marks]08.2Distinguish between hygroscopic, capillary and gravitational water in soil and state which are available to plants.
[1 mark]08.3Explain why crumb peds produce more fertile soil than platy peds.
Show mark scheme
08.1 1 mark
Correct answer only.
  • 1  B - tiny pores between clay particles allow capillary rise; none in sand
08.2 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Hygroscopic: held tightly by adhesive forces on particle surfaces; cannot drain and cannot be absorbed by plants
  • 1Capillary: held in pores by surface tension; does not drain but can be absorbed by plants
  • 1Gravitational: drains freely through large pores; available to plants only briefly as it passes
08.3 1 mark
1 mark.
  • 1Small round crumbs leave spaces giving good drainage, aeration and easy root penetration; large flat platy peds pack together reducing all three
