Question 110 marks · Pesticides · spec 3.4.3.2.8 · Genn pp. 262-263 · PEST-01

Figure 1 shows how the percentage mortality of a pest species and of a non-target bee species change with the dose of an insecticide applied to a crop.

Figure 1
[1 mark]01.1Use Figure 1 to estimate the LD50 of the insecticide for the pest species.
[1 mark]01.2Define the term MDNF (maximum dose never fatal).
[3 marks]01.3Use Figure 1 to explain why it may not be possible to kill all of the pest species without harming bees.
[2 marks]01.4Suggest how the farmer could reduce the harm to bees without reducing the dose applied.
[3 marks]01.5Explain why a pesticide with high specificity is less likely to cause environmental damage.
Show mark scheme
01.1 (1 marks)
  • 25 g ha-1 (accept 24-27)
01.2 (1 marks)
  • The highest dose that can be applied without killing any members of the population / any individuals of the (non-target) species
01.3 (3 marks)
Any 3 from:
  • The MDAF (minimum dose always fatal) of the pest is about 50 g ha-1
  • This is higher than the MDNF of the bee (about 20 g ha-1)
  • So a dose that kills all pests kills some bees / the two curves overlap between about 20 and 50 g ha-1
  • A dose of 30-40 g ha-1 would kill most pests with limited bee deaths (compromise)
01.4 (2 marks)
Any 2 from:
  • Spray at night / when bees are not flying
  • Spray when the crop is not in flower
  • Spray on still days to reduce spray drift
  • Use a systemic pesticide / seed coating rather than spraying
  • Use smaller droplets for more even coverage
01.5 (3 marks)
Any 3 from:
  • Specificity is the extent to which the pesticide is toxic only to the target species / range of species affected
  • A highly specific pesticide has low toxicity to non-target species (eg bees, fish, mammals)
  • So fewer non-target deaths / less loss of predators, pollinators
  • Fewer indirect (food chain) effects / fewer inter-species relationships lost
  • Example: pyrethroids have high insect toxicity but low mammal toxicity
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 262-263; AQA spec 3.4.3.2.8.
Question 210 marks · Pesticides · spec 3.4.3.2.8 · Genn pp. 263-265 · PEST-02

Table 1 shows some properties of four groups of insecticide.

Table 1
Insecticide groupToxicity to insectsToxicity to mammalsPersistenceLiposolubility
Organochlorines (eg DDT)HighLowHighHigh
Organophosphates (eg malathion)HighHighLowLow
PyrethroidsHighLowLowLow
NeonicotinoidsHighLowModerateLow (water soluble)
[4 marks]02.1Use Table 1 to explain why organochlorine insecticides caused the decline of birds of prey such as the peregrine falcon in the 1950s and 1960s.
[2 marks]02.2Organophosphates replaced organochlorines for many uses. Use Table 1 to give one advantage and one disadvantage of this change.
[1 mark]02.3Explain why pyrethroids should not be used near rivers or fish farms.
[3 marks]02.4Describe how the properties of neonicotinoids allow them to be used as a seed coating, and explain why this method of application reduces harm to non-target species.
Show mark scheme
02.1 (4 marks)
Any 4 from:
  • High persistence means DDT remains in the environment for years / decades, so it has time to disperse and be taken up
  • High liposolubility means it dissolves in fat and is stored in body tissues rather than excreted (bioaccumulation)
  • Concentration increases at each trophic level as predators eat many contaminated prey (biomagnification)
  • Birds of prey are at the top of the food chain so receive the highest concentrations
  • Sub-lethal doses caused thin eggshells that broke / reduced breeding success; higher doses caused sterility or death
02.2 (2 marks)
  • Advantage: low persistence / low liposolubility so they do not bioaccumulate or biomagnify / break down quickly
  • Disadvantage: high mammalian toxicity so farm workers / humans are at risk of acute poisoning (neurotoxin inhibiting acetylcholinesterase); chronic effects (memory loss, depression); suspected carcinogens
02.3 (1 marks)
  • Pyrethroids are highly toxic to fish (even though mammal toxicity is low)
02.4 (3 marks)
Any 3 from:
  • Neonicotinoids are water soluble so they are absorbed by the roots (from the soil) and translocated through the plant (systemic)
  • They are moderately persistent so remain in the soil long enough to be absorbed
  • Seed coating avoids spraying so no spray drift / no dust onto flowers or surrounding habitats
  • So bees / pollinators are less exposed than when the crop is sprayed

Question 310 marks · Pesticides · spec 3.4.3.2.8 · Genn pp. 263-264, 266 · PEST-03

A pesticide was applied to a field of oilseed rape. Table 1 shows the concentration of the pesticide in organisms sampled from a pond next to the field.

Table 1
OrganismTrophic levelPesticide concentration / mg kg-1
Pond water-0.002
Phytoplankton10.04
Water fleas (Daphnia)20.5
Small fish34.2
Heron438.0
[2 marks]03.1Calculate how many times more concentrated the pesticide is in the heron than in the pond water. Give your answer to two significant figures.
[1 mark]03.2Name the process shown by the data in Table 1.
[4 marks]03.3Explain the two properties of the pesticide that cause the pattern shown in Table 1.
[3 marks]03.4The pesticide was probably an organochlorine. Suggest why the use of organochlorines is now banned or heavily restricted in most countries, even though DDT is still used to control malaria mosquitoes in some houses.
Show mark scheme
03.1 (2 marks)
  • 38.0 / 0.002
  • = 19 000 (19 000 times / 1.9 x 104)
03.2 (1 marks)
  • Biomagnification (accept bioaccumulation as part of the process only if biomagnification is also implied by increase up the food chain)
03.3 (4 marks)
  • Persistence: the pesticide is not broken down (by light, microbes, enzymes) so it remains in the environment / organisms long enough to pass along the food chain
  • Liposolubility: the pesticide dissolves in lipids / fat so it is stored in body tissue and not excreted in (water-based) urine
  • Each organism eats many organisms from the level below and retains the pesticide so concentration rises at each trophic level
  • Herons have long lives so accumulate over time
03.4 (3 marks)
Any 3 from:
  • Organochlorines killed non-target insects (bees, butterflies) and top predators (birds of prey, otters) through biomagnification
  • They persist for decades so the damage continued long after use
  • Use in houses (on walls / nets) does not enter food chains / no runoff into water, so does not biomagnify
  • Benefit of preventing malaria deaths outweighs the risk (risk:benefit)
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 263-264, 266; AQA spec 3.4.3.2.8.
Question 410 marks · Pesticides · spec 3.4.3.2.8 · Genn pp. 265-266 · PEST-04
[9 marks]04.1Neonicotinoids are the most widely used group of insecticides. Explain how the properties of neonicotinoids affect their impact on bee populations and describe the methods used to reduce this impact.
[1 mark]04.2State one way in which pesticide use in agriculture may be reduced by using natural processes.
Show mark scheme
04.1 (9 marks)
Levels of response: L3 7-9 comprehensive, well-structured, applied to context, supported judgement; L2 4-6 sound but incomplete application/evaluation; L1 1-3 basic, limited structure.
Indicative content:
  • Neonicotinoids are neurotoxins similar to nicotine; they inhibit the neurotransmitter acetylcholine
  • High toxicity to insects but low toxicity to vertebrates because they cannot cross the vertebrate blood-brain barrier
  • Very toxic to bees: sub-lethal doses reduce navigation ability, foraging and immunity to disease; linked to colony decline
  • Water soluble and moderately persistent: remain in soil, absorbed by roots and translocated (systemic), so present in pollen and nectar
  • Persistence may allow build-up in aquifers / soil; broken down fastest in sunlight and by soil microbes
  • Synergism with some fungicides increases toxicity to bees
  • Exposure greatest when sprayed onto crops or when seed-coating dust is blown by wind
  • Control: seed coating rather than spraying; spraying when crops are not in flower / at night; still days to reduce drift
  • Restrictions on use (EU bans on outdoor use on flowering crops); use of alternative pest control (cultural, biological, integrated control)
  • Evaluation: reduced pesticide use but neonicotinoid alternatives (pyrethroids) also harm pollinators; trade-off with crop yields
04.2 (1 marks)
Any 1 from:
  • Biological control (introduced predators / pathogens)
  • Predator habitats (hedgerows, beetle banks)
  • Crop rotation
  • Companion / barrier crops
  • Sterile male technique / pheromone traps
  • Genetic resistance
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 265-266; AQA spec 3.4.3.2.8.

  • The dose that kills 50% of the (test) population

  • Insecticide X: LD50 for the beetle (6) is very similar to that of the pest (5) so it has low specificity / kills the predator as well

Question 65 marks · Pesticides · spec 3.4.3.2.8 · Genn pp. 263-266 · PEST-06
[1 mark]06.1Define the term persistence as applied to a pollutant.
[4 marks]06.2Explain how the persistence of a pesticide is affected by two named environmental factors.
Show mark scheme
06.1 (1 marks)
  • The length of time a pollutant remains in the environment before it is broken down / degraded (chemically or biologically)
06.2 (4 marks)
Any 4 from:
  • Light: UV light degrades many pesticides (eg neonicotinoids break down fastest in sunlight) so persistence is shorter in bright conditions
  • Temperature: higher temperatures increase the rate of chemical breakdown / microbial decomposition
  • Soil microbes / oxygen: aerobic microbial activity breaks pesticides down so persistence is shorter in well-aerated, biologically active soils
  • pH / presence of other chemicals may increase or decrease the rate of degradation
  • Adsorption onto clay / organic matter may protect from degradation and increase persistence
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 263-266; AQA spec 3.4.3.2.8.
Question 710 marks · Pesticides · spec 3.4.3.2.8 · Genn pp. 262-266 · PEST-07

Figure 1 shows the number of breeding pairs of sparrowhawks in a study area in the UK between 1945 and 1985, and the years in which DDT use began and was banned.

Figure 1
[2 marks]07.1Describe the trend shown in Figure 1.
[2 marks]07.2Calculate the percentage decrease in breeding pairs between 1950 and 1965. Show your working.
[4 marks]07.3DDT use in the UK began in the late 1940s and was restricted from the mid 1960s. Explain how the properties of DDT could account for the pattern in Figure 1.
[2 marks]07.4Suggest why the recovery after 1970 was slow.
Show mark scheme
07.1 (2 marks)
  • Numbers fell from about 210 pairs in 1945 to a minimum of about 25 in 1965 (steepest fall 1955-1960)
  • Numbers then recovered / rose steadily after 1970, reaching about 190 by 1985 (not yet back to 1945 level)
07.2 (2 marks)
  • (200 - 25) / 200 x 100
  • = 87.5%
07.3 (4 marks)
Any 4 from:
  • DDT is persistent (remains for decades) and liposoluble so it bioaccumulates in prey (small birds) and biomagnifies in sparrowhawks (top predator)
  • Sub-lethal doses caused thin eggshells / reduced breeding success so the population declined even without adult deaths
  • Higher doses caused sterility / death
  • After restrictions, environmental concentrations slowly fell (persistence explains the delay before recovery)
  • Breeding success recovered so the population rose
07.4 (2 marks)
Any 2 from:
  • DDT persists in soil / sediments / food chains for many years so exposure continued
  • Birds of prey are k-selected: few young per year, slow to mature so populations grow slowly
  • Other pressures (habitat loss, persecution)

Question 85 marks · Pesticides · spec 3.4.3.2.8 · Genn pp. 262-266 · PEST-08
[1 mark]08.1Which of the following properties would make a pesticide least likely to biomagnify? Tick one box.
  • A  High liposolubility and high persistence
  • B  Low liposolubility and low persistence
  • C  High liposolubility and low persistence
  • D  Low liposolubility and high persistence
[2 marks]08.2Explain the difference between bioaccumulation and biomagnification.
[2 marks]08.3Explain why pesticides are described as an unusual type of pollutant.
Show mark scheme
08.1 (1 marks)
  • B - Low liposolubility and low persistence
08.2 (2 marks)
  • Bioaccumulation: build-up of a pollutant in the tissues of an individual organism over time because uptake is faster than excretion / breakdown
  • Biomagnification: increase in concentration of the pollutant at successive trophic levels of a food chain
08.3 (2 marks)
  • They are intended to be toxic (to the target species)
  • They are deliberately released / spread into the environment (rather than being an accidental or waste release)
