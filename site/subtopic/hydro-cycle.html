[3 marks]01.1The residence time of water in a reservoir is given by: residence time = volume in reservoir / annual transfer rate. Use Table 1 to calculate the residence time of water in the atmosphere. Give your answer to the nearest whole day.
[1 mark]01.2Calculate the residence time of water in the oceans.
[2 marks]01.3Explain why the atmosphere is an important reservoir even though it holds only 0.001% of global water.
[2 marks]01.4Explain why human changes to a small reservoir such as soil moisture are more obvious than changes to the oceans.
[2 marks]01.5Explain why the hydrological cycle is described as being in dynamic equilibrium.

01.1 3 marks
A correct final answer (with unit where asked) scores full marks. If the answer is wrong, award 1 mark for each correct step of working shown.
  • 113 / 480 = 0.0271 years
  • 10.0271 x 365
  • 1= 10 days (accept 9.9-10)
01.2 1 mark
1 mark for the correct answer.
  • 11 340 000 / 425 = 3153 years (accept 3100-3200)
01.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1It has a very short residence time / very rapid transfer rate (480 x 1015 dm3 per year)
  • 1So it moves large volumes of water each year, delivering precipitation to land; small reservoirs with fast transfers respond quickly to change
01.4 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1The ocean is so large that an added or removed volume is a tiny proportion of the total
  • 1The same volume is a large proportion of a small reservoir so its level / residence time changes noticeably
01.5 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Water is constantly moving between reservoirs (evaporation, precipitation, runoff, infiltration, transpiration)
  • 1But inflows and outflows for each reservoir balance so the volume in each reservoir stays constant over long periods

Question 210 marks · The hydrosphere: hydrological cycle & unsustainable exploitation · spec 3.2.2.1 · Genn pp. 116-117 · HYD-02
[6 marks]02.1Explain how each of the following human activities alters processes in the hydrological cycle.
[2 marks]02.2Describe two ways in which global climate change alters the hydrological cycle.
[2 marks]02.3Explain why the impact of industrial and domestic water use on the hydrological cycle is usually local.
Show mark scheme
02.1 6 marks
2 marks for each point (partial credit for a partly correct point).
  • 2Deforestation: less interception so less evaporation from leaves; less transpiration so less water returned to the atmosphere and less precipitation downwind; more infiltration and surface runoff, higher river flows / flooding
  • 2Urban development: impermeable concrete / tarmac reduce infiltration and aquifer recharge; runoff is faster and greater, increasing river flooding downstream; drains carry water quickly to rivers
  • 2Agriculture: soil compaction by machinery / trampling reduces infiltration and soil moisture, increases runoff; irrigation increases evaporation; loss of soil biota (worms) reduces infiltration and water retention; drainage lowers water tables
02.2 2 marks
Any 2 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 2.
  • 1Higher temperatures increase evaporation and change the amount, timing, type (rain vs snow) and location of precipitation
  • 1More rapid melting of snow and ice changes river regimes: more flooding after rain, lower flows in dry periods
  • 1Changed wind patterns move humid air to different areas
  • 1Sea level rise / salt incursion into coastal aquifers
02.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Most water abstracted is cleaned and returned to the river / sea so the main impact is on quality (pollution) not quantity
  • 1Effects are noticeable only where large urban centres draw on restricted local supplies (aquifers, small rivers)
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 116-117; AQA spec 3.2.2.1.

The Aral Sea in central Asia was once the fourth largest lake in the world. From the 1960s water from the two rivers feeding it (the Amu Darya and Syr Darya) was diverted to irrigate cotton and rice. Table 1 shows changes in the lake.

[2 marks]03.1Calculate the percentage reduction in the area of the Aral Sea between 1960 and 2015.
[2 marks]03.2Explain why the salinity of the lake increased as the area fell.
[4 marks]03.3Explain why the fish catch fell to zero, and describe two other environmental consequences of the shrinking of the lake.
[2 marks]03.4Suggest why disagreements over the rivers continue between the countries of the region.
Show mark scheme
03.1 2 marks
A correct final answer (with unit where asked) scores full marks. If the answer is wrong, award 1 mark for each correct step of working shown.
  • 1(68 000 - 7 000) / 68 000 x 100
  • 1= 89.7% (accept 90%)
03.2 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Inflow from the rivers was reduced (sometimes to zero) so less water entered while evaporation continued
  • 1Evaporation removes pure water leaving the dissolved salts behind, so they became concentrated in a smaller volume
03.3 4 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Salinity rose above the range of tolerance of the freshwater fish species / osmotic stress; pollution by pesticides, fertilisers and industrial waste concentrated in the lake
  • 1Exposed lake bed of salt and pesticide-contaminated dust blown by wind onto farmland and settlements (health problems, soil salinisation)
  • 1Loss of wetland habitats and wildlife; local climate more extreme (loss of the moderating effect of the water body)
  • 1Loss of livelihoods; conflict between the countries sharing the rivers
03.4 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Upstream abstraction for irrigation reduces the flow available downstream
  • 1Upstream HEP dams hold water in summer for winter generation, reducing summer irrigation water downstream; growing populations increase demand

Question 410 marks · The hydrosphere: hydrological cycle & unsustainable exploitation · spec 3.2.2.1-3.2.2.2 · Genn pp. 119-121 · HYD-04

Figure 1 shows the depth of the water table below the ground surface in an aquifer beneath a coastal farming region between 1970 and 2020.

Figure 1
[1 mark]04.1Calculate the mean rate at which the water table fell between 1970 and 2020.
[2 marks]04.2Explain why the water table is falling.
[3 marks]04.3Explain the features a rock must have to form an aquifer.
[4 marks]04.4Describe four consequences of over-exploiting this coastal aquifer.
Show mark scheme
04.1 1 mark
1 mark for the correct answer.
  • 1(44 - 8) / 50 = 0.72 m yr-1
04.2 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Abstraction (for irrigation) exceeds the rate of natural recharge by infiltration / percolation of precipitation
  • 1The dynamic equilibrium between inflow and outflow has been upset so the stored volume declines
04.3 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Porosity: a high proportion of the rock volume is space that can hold water (chalk, limestone, sandstone)
  • 1Permeability: the spaces are interconnected and large enough for water to flow through (clay is porous but not permeable)
  • 1Impermeable rock (granite, clay) beneath to stop water escaping, and permeable rock / soil above to allow recharge
04.4 4 marks
Any 4 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 4.
  • 1Salt water incursion: seawater flows into the aquifer to replace the fresh water, making it unusable for irrigation (osmotic dehydration of crops)
  • 1Subsidence: emptied pore spaces no longer support the rock so it compacts, damaging buildings and pipes (eg Mexico City)
  • 1Drying up of groundwater-fed rivers, lakes and wetlands as the water table falls below their beds
  • 1Ecological impacts: plants needing high water availability die; wetland animals lost; food chain effects
  • 1Reduced supplies: abstraction may suddenly have to stop after years of hidden decline

Question 510 marks · The hydrosphere: hydrological cycle & unsustainable exploitation · spec 3.2.2.1-3.2.2.2 · Genn pp. 119-121 · HYD-05
[3 marks]05.1Explain why abstraction from an aquifer containing 'fossil' (palaeo) water, such as the High Plains / Ogallala Aquifer in the USA, cannot be sustainable.
[3 marks]05.2Describe how the GRACE satellites are used to monitor aquifer depletion and give one advantage over monitoring boreholes.
[3 marks]05.3Describe three measures being introduced on the North China Plain to reduce aquifer depletion.
[1 mark]05.4Suggest why large-scale afforestation on the North China Plain may reduce aquifer recharge even though trees reduce soil erosion.
Show mark scheme
05.1 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1The water was recharged during or before the last ice age (10 000-20 000 years ago) when the climate was wetter
  • 1Current recharge is little or none so any abstraction reduces the stored volume
  • 1The large volume means shortages take a long time to appear, but eventually the supply runs out; ancient water is often saline, causing soil salinisation
05.2 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Two satellites whose orbits are affected by the force of gravity
  • 1The mass of water in aquifers below changes gravity, so changes in the orbit / distance between the satellites indicate changes in groundwater mass
  • 1Advantage: the whole Earth is surveyed every 30 days, whereas borehole water-table measurements are slow and hard to coordinate over large areas
05.3 3 marks
Any 3 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 3.
  • 1Low-pressure irrigation pipes to reduce leaks
  • 1Growing crops that need less water than wheat / maize
  • 1Charging consumers by volume used (metering)
  • 1Inter-basin transfer of water 450 km from the Yangtze
05.4 1 mark
1 mark.
  • 1Trees intercept rainfall and transpire large volumes, reducing soil moisture and the water percolating down to the aquifer

Question 610 marks · The hydrosphere: hydrological cycle & unsustainable exploitation · spec 3.2.2.1 · Genn pp. 117 · HYD-06

A dam and reservoir are to be built on a river to supply water and regulate flow.

[6 marks]06.1Explain three effects the reservoir may have on wildlife in and around the river.
[2 marks]06.2Explain why decomposition of flooded vegetation in a new reservoir contributes to climate change.
[2 marks]06.3Explain how a river-regulation reservoir reduces both flooding and water shortages downstream.
Show mark scheme
06.1 6 marks
Up to 6 marks in total; award marks in proportion to the completeness of each point.
  • up to 2Habitat change: flooding destroys the previous terrestrial / river habitats but creates a wetland which may be valuable (wetlands are uncommon)
  • up to 2Wildlife barrier: the dam blocks migrating fish (salmon, sturgeon) and prevents recolonisation along the river
  • up to 2Changed river regime downstream: regulated flow removes low-flow periods needed by species such as river turtles nesting on sandbanks and high flows that wash sediment from gravel spawning beds
  • up to 2Sedimentation: sediment settles in the reservoir instead of fertilising the floodplain and building river banks / coastlines downstream, increasing erosion
  • up to 2Microclimate: water's high heat capacity reduces temperature fluctuations; higher wind speeds; more evaporation, humidity, cloud and precipitation downwind
06.2 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Vegetation / dead organic matter under water decays anaerobically
  • 1Releasing methane, a powerful greenhouse gas
06.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1After heavy rain surplus water is stored, reducing peak flow / flooding downstream
  • 1In dry periods the dam releases water to maintain river levels / supplies
