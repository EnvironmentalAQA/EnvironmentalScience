Question 110 marks · Conditions for life on Earth · spec 3.1.1.1 · Genn pp. 5-7 · LIFE-01

Table 1 shows some features of Earth, Mars and Venus.

Table 1
FeatureVenusEarthMars
Mean distance from Sun / million km108150228
Mass relative to Earth0.821.00.11
Atmospheric pressure relative to Earth921.00.006
Mean surface temperature / °C46015-60
Magnetic fieldVery weakStrongVery weak
Rotation period / hours5 8002425
[3 marks]01.1Use Table 1 to explain why liquid water cannot exist on the surface of Mars.
[3 marks]01.2Explain how the Earth's mass and its distance from the Sun created suitable conditions for life.
[2 marks]01.3Explain the importance of the Earth's magnetic field and its speed of rotation for living organisms.
[2 marks]01.4Explain how the tilt of the Earth's axis affects living organisms.
Show mark scheme
01.1 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Mars has low mass so weak gravity and could not retain a dense atmosphere
  • 1Very low atmospheric pressure means water would boil / evaporate away even at low temperatures
  • 1Greater distance from the Sun gives low insolation and a mean temperature of -60 °C so water is frozen
01.2 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Mass great enough for gravity to retain an atmosphere containing the elements needed (C, H, O, N as CO2, methane, ammonia, water vapour) and to maintain pressure so liquid water does not boil
  • 1Distance from the Sun (the 'Goldilocks zone') gives insolation producing temperatures at which water is liquid (0-35 °C over most of the surface) and proteins are not denatured
  • 1Too close (Venus) - too hot; too far (Mars) - too cold
01.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1The molten core produces a magnetosphere that deflects the solar wind / charged particles, preventing biologically damaging radiation reaching the surface
  • 1The 24-hour rotation limits heating by day and cooling by night, reducing temperature extremes (Venus' slow rotation gives extreme conditions)
01.4 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1The tilted axis causes seasonal variation in insolation as the Earth orbits the Sun
  • 1Producing seasons (temperature, day length) to which species' life cycles (breeding, migration, dormancy) are adapted; the Moon's gravity keeps the tilt stable and creates tides
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 5-7; AQA spec 3.1.1.1.
Question 210 marks · Conditions for life on Earth · spec 3.1.1.1-3.1.1.2 · Genn pp. 7-8 · LIFE-02
[4 marks]02.1Explain four properties of water that make it essential for life.
[4 marks]02.2Describe how photosynthetic organisms changed the atmosphere and explain how this allowed life to colonise the land.
[2 marks]02.3Explain how carbon sequestration by early photoautotrophs helped maintain a suitable temperature on Earth.
Show mark scheme
02.1 4 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1General physiological solvent: most biological reactions take place between substances dissolved in water
  • 1Transport medium in blood / sap carrying gases, sugars, amino acids, nutrients, wastes
  • 1High specific heat capacity moderates temperature changes; evaporation absorbs heat (cooling)
  • 1Anomalous expansion: ice floats so water bodies do not freeze solid; absorbs UV protecting early marine life; provides aquatic habitats
02.2 4 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1From about 2.7 billion years ago photosynthetic archaea / bacteria (then algae and plants) released oxygen; at first it reacted with dissolved iron (forming iron ore deposits) then built up in the oceans and atmosphere (from about 2.45 billion years ago)
  • 1Oxygen in the stratosphere absorbed UV, forming ozone (O3) in a dynamic equilibrium of O, O2 and O3
  • 1The ozone layer reduced UV reaching the surface so organisms could survive out of the water
  • 1Oxygen allowed aerobic respiration releasing more energy so complex multicellular organisms evolved; many anaerobic archaea died out
02.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Photosynthesis removed CO2 (a greenhouse gas) from the atmosphere, storing carbon in sediments, carbonate rocks and fossil fuels
  • 1This reduced infrared retention, offsetting the increasing brightness of the Sun (about 10% per billion years) so temperatures did not rise excessively
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 7-8; AQA spec 3.1.1.1-3.1.1.2.
Question 310 marks · Conditions for life on Earth · spec 3.1.1.2-3.1.1.3 · Genn pp. 8-9 · LIFE-03
[3 marks]03.1Explain how the development of biogeochemical cycles and of transpiration by land plants helped to maintain stable conditions for life.
[3 marks]03.2Describe three limitations of early methods of studying past conditions on Earth.
[4 marks]03.3Describe how each of the following modern techniques provides information about past conditions.
Show mark scheme
03.1 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Interconnected biological processes recycle nutrient elements so small amounts support life indefinitely without depletion; waste products of one process are raw materials of another so wastes do not build up
  • 1Transpiration returned water vapour to the atmosphere increasing rainfall elsewhere
  • 1Allowing more plant growth, further stabilising climate and soils
03.2 3 marks
Any 3 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 3.
  • 1Lack of ancient historical / instrumental data
  • 1Reliance on proxy data (tree rings, pollen) of limited reliability for ancient conditions
  • 1Lack of sophisticated equipment / inability to measure many factors
  • 1Limited coordination between researchers; lack of data collection in many areas (oceans, poles)
03.3 4 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1The ratio of oxygen-18 to oxygen-16 in the ice indicates the temperature when the snow fell
  • 1Air bubbles trapped in ice give the composition of the past atmosphere (CO2, methane)
  • 1Ratio of carbon-14 to carbon-12 dates organic material (eg pollen in sediments)
  • 1More magnesium is incorporated into calcite at higher temperatures, so the ratio estimates past sea temperature
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 8-9; AQA spec 3.1.1.2-3.1.1.3.
Question 45 marks · Conditions for life on Earth · spec 3.1.1.1 · Genn pp. 5-7 · LIFE-04
[1 mark]04.1Which feature of the Earth prevents most atmospheric gases from escaping into space? Tick one box.
  • A  Its mass
  • B  Its magnetic field
  • C  Its distance from the Sun
  • D  Its speed of rotation
[2 marks]04.2Explain how the speed of rotation of the Earth and the tilt of its axis affect the conditions for life.
[2 marks]04.3Explain why the presence of liquid water on Earth depends on both the distance from the Sun and atmospheric pressure.
Show mark scheme
04.1 1 mark
Correct answer only.
  • 1A  Its mass
04.2 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1The 24-hour rotation period means the surface is not exposed to sunlight for long, so temperature extremes / excessive heating and cooling are reduced
  • 1The axis is tilted relative to the orbit so the amount of insolation at each latitude changes through the year, producing seasonal variations in conditions
04.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1The distance from the Sun gives surface temperatures between 0 and 35 °C in most areas: too close and water would boil / evaporate, too far and it would freeze
  • 1Atmospheric pressure (produced by the mass of gases held by gravity) is high enough to stop the water boiling / evaporating away; at much lower pressure there would be no liquid water
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 5-7; AQA spec 3.1.1.1.
Question 510 marks · Conditions for life on Earth · spec 3.1.1.2 · Genn pp. 6-8 · LIFE-05

Table 1 shows some events in the history of life on Earth.

Table 1
EventTime before present / billion years
Formation of the Earth4.6
First life (Archaea) on Earth3.5
First photosynthetic organisms2.7
Oxygen concentration in the atmosphere starts to rise2.45
End of the period of oxygen build-up (Proterozoic)0.54
[1 mark]05.1Use Table 1 to calculate how many million years passed between the appearance of photosynthetic organisms and the start of the rise in atmospheric oxygen.
[2 marks]05.2Explain why atmospheric oxygen did not start to rise as soon as photosynthesis began.
[3 marks]05.3Explain how the rise in atmospheric oxygen made life on the land surface possible.
[2 marks]05.4Explain how early photosynthetic organisms helped to prevent a long-term rise in the Earth's temperature.
[2 marks]05.5Give two other ways in which living organisms changed the conditions on Earth.
Show mark scheme
05.1 1 mark
1 mark for the correct answer.
  • 12.7 - 2.45 = 0.25 billion = 250 million years
05.2 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1For millions of years all the oxygen produced reacted with (dissolved) iron in the oceans
  • 1Only when all the iron had reacted did surplus dissolved oxygen build up in the oceans and then escape into the atmosphere
05.3 3 marks
Any 3 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 3.
  • 1Oxygen in the atmosphere absorbed ultraviolet light
  • 1A dynamic equilibrium of reactions between O, O2 and O3 (the ozone layer) developed
  • 1Less UV reached the surface so organisms were no longer restricted to water (which had absorbed the UV)
  • 1Aerobic respiration became possible so more complex / larger organisms (plants and animals) evolved; many anaerobic Archaea and bacteria died out
05.4 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Photoautotrophs absorbed carbon dioxide, a greenhouse gas, and some of the carbon was stored in geological sediments such as carbonate rocks and fossil fuels (carbon sequestration)
  • 1The lower CO2 concentration reduced the retention of heat, offsetting the increase in the brightness of the Sun (about 10% per billion years)
05.5 2 marks
Any 2 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 2.
  • 1Biogeochemical cycles developed so small amounts of nutrient elements could support life for long periods without depletion
  • 1Transpiration by land plants returned water vapour to the atmosphere and increased rainfall in other areas
  • 1Regulation of atmospheric composition (eg oxygen / CO2 by photosynthesis and respiration)
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 6-8; AQA spec 3.1.1.2.

[2 marks]06.3Explain why an understanding of continental drift and ocean currents has been important in explaining past changes in conditions.
[3 marks]06.4Life probably first developed around volcanic vents on the seabed. Explain why the conditions in the oceans were more suitable than those on land 3.5 billion years ago.
Show mark scheme
06.1 1 mark
1 mark.
  • 1Direct measurements of past conditions cannot be taken, so indirect evidence that is related to the condition must be used
06.2 4 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Age / date of the sample (radioisotope dating)
  • 1Past temperatures
  • 1Composition of the past atmosphere (eg CO2 / methane concentrations)
  • 1Temperature: more magnesium is incorporated into calcite at higher temperatures
06.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Movement of continents changed the position of land masses / latitude and therefore the climate and habitats present
  • 1Ocean currents redistribute heat and nutrients; changes in currents (eg as continents moved) altered climates, so past conditions cannot be explained without knowing how the systems worked
06.4 3 marks
Any 3 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 3.
  • 1There was no oxygen / ozone layer so high levels of UV radiation reached the land surface; water absorbed the UV and protected organisms in the oceans
  • 1Warm temperatures around the vents and a rich mix of chemicals / increasingly complex organic molecules in the sea made biological reactions more likely
  • 1The atmosphere contained toxic gases such as ammonia; the early Archaea were anaerobic so did not need oxygen
  • 1Water acted as the solvent for reactions / transport
