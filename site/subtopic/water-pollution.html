Question 110 marks · Thermal & oil pollution · spec 3.4.3.2.6 · Genn pp. 256-257 · WPOL-01

Figure 1 shows the maximum concentration of dissolved oxygen in fresh water at different temperatures. A power station discharges warm cooling water into a river. The lowest dissolved oxygen concentration that trout can tolerate is 6.5 mg dm-3.

Figure 1
[1 mark]01.1Describe the relationship shown in Figure 1.
[1 mark]01.2Use Figure 1 to determine the highest water temperature at which trout could survive if the water were fully saturated with oxygen.
[2 marks]01.3In summer the river upstream of the power station is at 20 °C and the effluent raises the temperature by 7 °C. Use Figure 1 to calculate the reduction in maximum dissolved oxygen concentration caused by the effluent.
[2 marks]01.4Explain why the actual dissolved oxygen concentration downstream may be much lower than the values in Figure 1.
[3 marks]01.5Describe three effects of warmer water on aquatic organisms other than reduced dissolved oxygen.
[1 mark]01.6Describe how a cooling tower reduces the temperature of power station effluent water.
Show mark scheme
01.1 1 mark
1 mark.
  • 1As temperature increases the maximum dissolved oxygen concentration decreases (non-linear: falls faster at low temperatures)
01.2 1 mark
1 mark for the correct answer.
  • 1About 39 °C (accept 38-40)
01.3 2 marks
A correct final answer (with unit where asked) scores full marks. If the answer is wrong, award 1 mark for each correct step of working shown.
  • 19.1 - 7.9 (value at 27 °C, accept 7.8-8.0)
  • 1= 1.2 mg dm-3 (accept 1.1-1.3)
01.4 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Figure 1 shows the maximum / saturation values; processes that remove oxygen (aerobic decomposition of organic matter / respiration) can give values below the line
  • 1Higher temperature increases the metabolic rate of organisms and the rate of bacterial decomposition so oxygen is used faster; values above the line are impossible
01.5 3 marks
Any 3 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 3.
  • 1Increased metabolic rate so more energy used / less surplus for growth
  • 1Eggs develop faster / hatch earlier (out of step with food supply)
  • 1Non-indigenous warm-water species may thrive and out-compete indigenous species
  • 1Toxicity of other pollutants is greater as toxins are metabolised faster
  • 1Reduced resistance to disease
  • 1Proteins / enzymes denatured if temperature is very high (above about 40 °C)
01.6 1 mark
1 mark.
  • 1Hot water is sprayed from a shower floor in a tall hollow tower; air absorbs the heat and rises, drawing in more air (updraught), so the water returned is cooler (and saturated with oxygen)
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 256-257; AQA spec 3.4.3.2.6.
Question 210 marks · Thermal & oil pollution · spec 3.4.3.2.7 · Genn pp. 258-259 · WPOL-02
[3 marks]02.1Give three sources of oil pollution of the sea other than tanker accidents.
[3 marks]02.2Explain how an oil spill at sea can kill seabirds.
[2 marks]02.3Explain two ways in which an oil slick can harm organisms that live in the water beneath it.
[2 marks]02.4Explain why oil pollution is most serious when it occurs inshore or in an enclosed body of water.
Show mark scheme
02.1 3 marks
Any 3 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 3.
  • 1Discharge of tank-washing water from tankers
  • 1Oil rig / drilling accidents / leakage during drilling
  • 1Pipeline leaks
  • 1Leakage from storage tanks / refinery spills
  • 1Waste lubricating oil from vehicles / industry washed into drains
  • 1Other ship accidents (fuel oil)
02.2 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Oil sticks feathers together so they lose their insulating properties and birds die of hypothermia
  • 1Feathers lose buoyancy / waterproofing so heavily oiled birds drown
  • 1Birds swallow toxic oil when preening; time spent cleaning feathers reduces time for feeding themselves / their young
02.3 2 marks
Any 2 from the points below - 1 mark each, maximum 2.
  • 1The surface film reduces the diffusion of oxygen from the atmosphere into the water, so aerobic organisms suffer from low dissolved oxygen
  • 1Toxic components (benzene, xylene) dissolve and poison plankton near the surface
  • 1Oil blocks light so algae / phytoplankton cannot photosynthesise
  • 1Oil masks odours / narcotises so animals cannot find mates or food by smell
  • 1Shore organisms (molluscs, crustaceans) are covered and asphyxiated
02.4 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Dispersal / dilution by currents and waves is prevented so oil stays concentrated; in open water most spills disperse in days or weeks
  • 1Inshore areas have more sensitive ecosystems (shores, reefs, mangroves, breeding sites) and more human activities (fishing, tourism) to be affected
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 258-259; AQA spec 3.4.3.2.7.
Question 310 marks · Thermal & oil pollution · spec 3.4.3.2.7 · Genn pp. 259-260 · WPOL-03

Table 1 shows features of oil tanker design and operation that reduce the risk of oil pollution.

[5 marks]03.1Complete Table 1 by explaining how each feature reduces oil pollution.
Table 1
FeatureHow it reduces oil pollution
Double hull
Inert gas system
Separate oil and ballast tanks
Twin engines / rudders
Recirculation of oil during unloading (crude oil washing)
[2 marks]03.2Explain how each of the following reduces the risk of tanker accidents.
[2 marks]03.3Describe how a bund wall prevents oil pollution from a storage tank.
[1 mark]03.4Explain the purpose of an oil interceptor on the drainage system of a car park.
Show mark scheme
03.1 5 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Double hull: a gap of up to 2 m between the hulls so damage to the outer hull in a collision / grounding does not release oil
  • 1Inert gas system: cooled engine exhaust gas (no oxygen) fills the space above the oil so an explosive oil vapour / air mixture cannot form
  • 1Separate ballast tanks: ballast water is not carried in oil tanks so no residual oil is discharged when ballast is pumped out
  • 1Twin engines / rudders: if one fails the ship can still be steered / propelled and is not carried onto rocks by currents or wind
  • 1Crude oil washing: sludges are mixed and removed with the cargo so tanks do not need washing with seawater that was then discharged; oily water is unloaded at the terminal
03.2 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Offshore routes keep tankers further from the coast so if there is engine failure there is more time to deal with it before the ship is carried ashore
  • 1GPS gives accurate navigation; AIS lets ship positions be monitored and coordinated to avoid collisions and groundings
03.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1The tank stands on an impermeable base surrounded by a wall
  • 1The volume enclosed by the wall is greater than the tank volume so all the oil is contained if the tank splits / cannot overflow
03.4 1 mark
1 mark.
  • 1Separates floating oil from drainage water so the oil is retained (for later treatment) and only water flows to the drains / river
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 259-260; AQA spec 3.4.3.2.7.
Question 410 marks · Thermal & oil pollution · spec 3.4.3.2.7 · Genn pp. 261-262 · WPOL-04

In 2020 the tanker MV Wakashio ran aground on a coral reef off Mauritius and leaked about 1000 tonnes of fuel oil into a shallow lagoon near mangroves and a marine park.

[3 marks]04.1Explain why inflatable booms were deployed immediately, and give one limitation of booms.
[3 marks]04.2Describe how skimmers and absorbent materials remove oil, and state how the collected oil is disposed of.
[2 marks]04.3Explain why dispersants were not used in the lagoon.
[2 marks]04.4Explain how bioremediation could help remove the oil that remained on the mangrove sediments, and suggest how it could be accelerated.
Show mark scheme
04.1 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Booms are floating tubes with a skirt that restrict the movement of floating oil, keeping it near the source / away from sensitive sites (mangroves, reef, marine park)
  • 1They work best in sheltered / calm water such as the lagoon
  • 1Limitation: they do not work in open water where currents or waves are strong; oil can flow under the skirt in a strong current
04.2 3 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Skimmers: rotating (metal) discs / belts pick up floating oil which is scraped off and stored
  • 1Absorbent materials (high surface area, eg textile mops, straw, human hair) soak up oil and are then removed
  • 1Collected oil / absorbents are incinerated or sent to landfill (or oil recycled)
04.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Dispersants break the oil into an emulsion of droplets, making it more mobile so it spreads through the water column (to the reef / seabed organisms) rather than staying on the surface
  • 1They do not reduce the amount of oil; the dispersant / oil mixture may be toxic to corals and plankton in shallow, enclosed water
04.4 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Bacteria that break down hydrocarbons digest the residual oil (most rapid where temperature, oxygen and nutrients are high)
  • 1Accelerated by adding nutrients (nitrates / phosphates) and aerating (ploughing) / irrigating the sediment
Source: R. Genn, Environmental Science A level AQA (Insight & Perspective, 2018), pp. 261-262; AQA spec 3.4.3.2.7.

Question 65 marks · Thermal & oil pollution · spec 3.4.3.2.6-3.4.3.2.7 · Genn pp. 254-262 · WPOL-06
[1 mark]06.1Which of the following oil spill treatments is most likely to kill organisms living deep in beach sediments that were not affected by the oil? Tick one box.
  • A  Absorbent mops
  • B  Steam washing
  • C  Inflatable booms
  • D  Polymerising agents
[2 marks]06.2Explain how polymerising agents make an oil spill easier to clean up.
[2 marks]06.3Explain why deoxygenation is a more common problem in water than in air.
Show mark scheme
06.1 1 mark
Correct answer only.
  • 1  B - Steam washing (hot water / steam jets kill organisms in the sediment)
06.2 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Chemicals cause the oil molecules to join together / solidify
  • 1Producing more solid material that can be collected / lifted more easily than liquid oil
06.3 2 marks
1 mark for each point.
  • 1Air contains about 210 cm3 of oxygen per litre whereas water at 20 °C holds a maximum of about 6 cm3 per litre (less than 3% of the amount in air)
  • 1So a small amount of oxygen-consuming activity (decomposition, respiration) or warming can remove most of the oxygen; many aquatic organisms are near the lower end of their range of tolerance
