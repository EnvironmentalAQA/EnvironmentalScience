  4. Oil masks odours / narcotises so animals cannot find mates or food by smell

  5. Shore organisms (molluscs, crustaceans) are covered and asphyxiated

Question 310 marks · Thermal & oil pollution · spec 3.4.3.2.7 · Genn pp. 259-260 · WPOL-03

Table 1 shows features of oil tanker design and operation that reduce the risk of oil pollution.

[5 marks]03.1Complete Table 1 by explaining how each feature reduces oil pollution.
Table 1
FeatureHow it reduces oil pollution
Double hull
Inert gas system
Separate oil and ballast tanks
Twin engines / rudders
Recirculation of oil during unloading (crude oil washing)
[2 marks]03.2Explain how each of the following reduces the risk of tanker accidents.
[2 marks]03.3Describe how a bund wall prevents oil pollution from a storage tank.
[1 mark]03.4Explain the purpose of an oil interceptor on the drainage system of a car park.
Show mark scheme
03.15 marks1 mark each

1 mark for each of the 5 points below.

  1. Double hull: a gap of up to 2 m between the hulls so damage to the outer hull in a collision / grounding does not release oil

  2. Inert gas system: cooled engine exhaust gas (no oxygen) fills the space above the oil so an explosive oil vapour / air mixture cannot form

  3. Separate ballast tanks: ballast water is not carried in oil tanks so no residual oil is discharged when ballast is pumped out

  4. Twin engines / rudders: if one fails the ship can still be steered / propelled and is not carried onto rocks by currents or wind

  5. Crude oil washing: sludges are mixed and removed with the cargo so tanks do not need washing with seawater that was then discharged

    • oily water is unloaded at the terminal
Also accept
  • Any other correct, relevant point of the same kind - wording never has to match these notes.
03.22 marks1 mark each

1 mark for each of the 2 points below.

  1. Offshore routes keep tankers further from the coast so if there is engine failure there is more time to deal with it before the ship is carried ashore

  2. GPS gives accurate navigation

    • AIS lets ship positions be monitored and coordinated to avoid collisions and groundings
Also accept
  • Any other correct, relevant point of the same kind - wording never has to match these notes.
  • accurate → close to the true value
03.32 marks1 mark each

1 mark for each of the 2 points below.

  1. The tank stands on an impermeable base surrounded by a wall

  2. The volume enclosed by the wall is greater than the tank volume so all the oil is contained if the tank splits / cannot overflow

Also accept
  • Any other correct, relevant point of the same kind - wording never has to match these notes.
  • impermeable → does not let water through
03.41 mark

1 mark for the point below.

  1. Separates floating oil from drainage water so the oil is retained (for later treatment) and only water flows to the drains / river

Question 410 marks · Thermal & oil pollution · spec 3.4.3.2.7 · Genn pp. 261-262 · WPOL-04

In 2020 the tanker MV Wakashio ran aground on a coral reef off Mauritius and leaked about 1000 tonnes of fuel oil into a shallow lagoon near mangroves and a marine park.

[3 marks]04.1Explain why inflatable booms were deployed immediately, and give one limitation of booms.
[3 marks]04.2Describe how skimmers and absorbent materials remove oil, and state how the collected oil is disposed of.
[2 marks]04.3Explain why dispersants were not used in the lagoon.
[2 marks]04.4Explain how bioremediation could help remove the oil that remained on the mangrove sediments, and suggest how it could be accelerated.
Show mark scheme
04.13 marks1 mark each

1 mark for each of the 3 points below.

  1. Booms are floating tubes with a skirt that restrict the movement of floating oil, keeping it near the source / away from sensitive sites (mangroves, reef, marine park)

  2. They work best in sheltered / calm water such as the lagoon

  3. Limitation: they do not work in open water where currents or waves are strong

    • oil can flow under the skirt in a strong current
Also accept
  • Any other correct, relevant point of the same kind - wording never has to match these notes.
04.23 marks1 mark each

1 mark for each of the 3 points below.

  1. Skimmers: rotating (metal) discs / belts pick up floating oil which is scraped off and stored

  2. Absorbent materials (high surface area, eg textile mops, straw, human hair) soak up oil and are then removed

  3. Collected oil / absorbents are incinerated or sent to landfill (or oil recycled)

Also accept
  • Any other correct, relevant point of the same kind - wording never has to match these notes.
Do not credit
  • 'landfill gas is just carbon dioxide' - it is roughly half methane, a far stronger greenhouse gas
04.32 marks1 mark each

1 mark for each of the 2 points below.

  1. Dispersants break the oil into an emulsion of droplets, making it more mobile so it spreads through the water column (to the reef / seabed organisms) rather than staying on the surface

  2. They do not reduce the amount of oil

    • the dispersant / oil mixture may be toxic to corals and plankton in shallow, enclosed water
Also accept
  • Any other correct, relevant point of the same kind - wording never has to match these notes.
04.42 marks1 mark each

1 mark for each of the 2 points below.

  1. Bacteria that break down hydrocarbons digest the residual oil (most rapid where temperature, oxygen and nutrients are high)

  2. Accelerated by adding nutrients (nitrates / phosphates) and aerating (ploughing) / irrigating the sediment

Also accept
  • Any other correct, relevant point of the same kind - wording never has to match these notes.

06.22 marks1 mark each

1 mark for each of the 2 points below.

  1. Chemicals cause the oil molecules to join together / solidify

  2. Producing more solid material that can be collected / lifted more easily than liquid oil

Also accept
  • Any other correct, relevant point of the same kind - wording never has to match these notes.
06.32 marks1 mark each

1 mark for each of the 2 points below.

  1. Air contains about 210 cm3 of oxygen per litre whereas water at 20 °C holds a maximum of about 6 cm3 per litre (less than 3% of the amount in air)

  2. So a small amount of oxygen-consuming activity (decomposition, respiration) or warming can remove most of the oxygen

    • many aquatic organisms are near the lower end of their range of tolerance
Also accept
  • Any other correct, relevant point of the same kind - wording never has to match these notes.
  • deoxygenation → loss of dissolved oxygen from the water
  • range of tolerance → the range of conditions in which a species can survive
